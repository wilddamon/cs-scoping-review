"Background: The COVID-19 pandemic has significantly impacted pregnant individuals and their newborns. While symptoms often mirror nonpregnant adults, unique clinical patterns exist. Understanding predictors of adverse outcomes is crucial. This study aimed to evaluate the association between maternal clinical presentation, specifically key laboratory markers (inflammatory, coagulation, and immune) and symptoms, and subsequent maternal and neonatal outcomes in hospitalized COVID-19-positive pregnant women during a period of low vaccination prevalence. Method(s): We retrospectively reviewed data from pregnant women with confirmed COVID-19 who delivered at Imam Reza Hospital, Mashhad, Iran, between April 2021 and March 2022. Demographic, clinical, laboratory, maternal, and neonatal data were collected. Variable interactions were explored using partial correlation and logistic regression. Result(s): Among 293 mother-newborn pairs, preterm delivery risk increased with low maternal lymphocyte percentage, low platelet count, and high prothrombin time; advanced gestational age was protective (OR = 0.94). Intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR) risk decreased with higher maternal weight (OR = 0.04) and lower C-reactive protein (CRP) (OR = 0.08). Stillbirth risk rose with elevated ferritin (OR = 10.12) and prothrombin time (OR = 6.34) but declined with higher maternal respiratory rate (OR = 0.21) and later gestational age (OR = 0.78). Neonatal asphyxia risk increased with maternal height (OR = 3.67) and respiratory rate (OR = 4.70), while higher lymphocyte percentage was protective (OR = 0.21). Low lymphocyte percentage predicted several adverse outcomes, including intensive care unit (ICU) admission, low birth weight (LBW), and mechanical ventilation. Conclusion(s): Our findings underscore the predictive potential of specific maternal inflammatory (e.g., CRP, ferritin), coagulation [e.g., prothrombin time (PT), platelets], and immune (e.g., lymphocyte percentage) parameters for adverse maternal and, notably, neonatal outcomes such as preterm birth, stillbirth, and LBW in COVID-19-positive pregnancies. Unexpected factors like maternal respiratory rate and height also showed associations warranting further study.
