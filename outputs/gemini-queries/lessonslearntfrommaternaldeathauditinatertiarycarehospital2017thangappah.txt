"Aims: The aim of this audit was to analyze the risk factors that have contributed to maternal deaths in a tertiary care institution and to identify the cause of death as well as determine the preventability of death. Material(s) and Method(s): This was a retrospective analytical study conducted in a tertiary care hospital from January 2009 to December 2010. This audit involved detailed review of the medical records, and the relevant data were collected from 61 cases of maternal deaths. Result(s): The maternal mortality ratio for the above period was 207/100,000 live births. The highest mortality of 67.2% was seen in the age group between 20 and 29 years. More than 30% of deaths occurred in women who were unbooked. About 29.5% of women were admitted in a moribund condition and they died within 12 hours of admission. About 44.3% of deaths were due to direct obstetrical causes, and 55.7% were due to indirect causes. Among the direct obstetrical deaths, hemorrhage was the leading cause of death in 15 cases (55.5%), followed by pregnancy-induced hypertension and eclampsia in 9 cases (33.3%). The indirect obstetrical deaths resulted from anemia in 10 cases (29.4%), heart disease in 10 cases (29.4%), and fever in 9 cases (26.5%). Seven deaths due to hemorrhage and one death due to eclampsia could have been potentially prevented. Conclusion(s): In a small percentage of cases, maternal deaths do occur at the institutional level.
