"Background: Spinal anaesthesia is commonly used for lower segment caesarean section (LSCS), but it carries a high risk of maternal hypotension due to sympathetic blockade. Early prediction of hypotension allows timely prophylaxis and intervention, enhancing maternal and fetal outcomes. Perfusion Index (PI), a non-invasive measure of peripheral perfusion derived from pulse oximetry, has emerged as a potential predictor of hypotension. Objective(s): To assess the correlation between baseline PI and incidence of hypotension following spinal anaesthesia in parturients undergoing elective LSCS and to assess response to phenylephrine/ephedrine on Blood pressure. Method(s): This prospective, double-blind observational study included 120 ASA I-II parturients aged 20-35 years. Baseline PI was recorded and patients were grouped based on a PI cutoff of 3.5. Intraoperative hemodynamics were monitored and hypotension was managed with ephedrine or phenylephrine. Result(s): Hypotension occurred in 80% of patients with PI >3.5 compared to 16.6% with PI <3.5 (p<0.001). Baseline PI showed high predictive accuracy (AUC = 0.82). Phenylephrine was more effective than ephedrine in restoring MAP and required fewer repeat boluses. Conclusion(s): Baseline Perfusion Index is a reliable, non-invasive predictor of post-spinal hypotension in LSCS.
