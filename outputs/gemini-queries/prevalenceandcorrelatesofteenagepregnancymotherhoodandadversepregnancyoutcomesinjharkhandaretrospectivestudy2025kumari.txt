"Background: Teenage pregnancy remains a pressing public health issue, especially in low-resource settings such as Jharkhand, India, where early marriage and limited access to healthcare persist. This study aims to determine the prevalence of teenage pregnancies among married girls aged 15-19 years and assess associated maternal and neonatal outcomes, including antenatal complications, delivery characteristics, and perinatal health indicators. Objective(s): To assess the clinical and perinatal outcomes of teenage pregnancies among married girls aged 15-19 years in Jharkhand, including antenatal complications, delivery characteristics, and neonatal outcomes. To identify the prevalence and distribution of maternal and neonatal complications including anemia, hypertensive disorders, preterm birth, low birth weight, and perinatal mortality associated with teenage pregnancy in a community-based setting. Method(s): A retrospective study was conducted between January and June 2024 across selected tertiary care centers in Jharkhand. A total of 250 married teenage girls (15-19 years) who delivered during the study period were included. Data were collected from hospital records, including sociodemographic details, obstetric history, and delivery mode, maternal and neonatal complications. Descriptive statistical analysis was performed using Microsoft Excel and Stata 17.0 to present frequencies and percentages. Result(s): Among the 250 teenage mothers, only 60% were booked for antenatal care, highlighting significant gaps in early pregnancy monitoring. Full-term deliveries accounted for 78.4%, while 10.8% were preterm. Cesarean deliveries were performed in 10.8% of cases, predominantly due to fetal distress (28%). Notably, 15.2% of neonates had low birth weight, and 10.8% were born preterm. Maternal anemia was prevalent in 50.5% of participants. Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy were observed in 20.79%, with preeclampsia without severe features (40.48%) and eclampsia (16.67%) being notable contributors. Neonatal complications included prematurity (39.53%), birth asphyxia (23.26%), and meconium aspiration syndrome (13.95%). Perinatal mortality and neonatal deaths were reported at 2.33% and 6.98%, respectively. Postpartum complications such as puerperal pyrexia (50%) and PPH (33.33%) were also documented. Conclusion(s): Teenage pregnancies in Jharkhand are associated with high rates of maternal anemia, hypertensive disorders, and neonatal complications, often exacerbated by poor antenatal coverage and socio-demographic vulnerabilities. Despite most deliveries being full-term with acceptable birth weights, the burden of preventable complications remains significant. These findings underscore the urgent need for targeted public health interventions, including early ANC registration, nutritional support, and adolescent-friendly reproductive health services, especially in tribal and rural regions.
