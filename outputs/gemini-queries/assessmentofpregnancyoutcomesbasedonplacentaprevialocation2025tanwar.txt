"""abstract ID: assessmentofpregnancyoutcomesbasedonplacentaprevialocation2025tanwar
"Background: Placenta previa is a serious obstetric condition where the placenta partially or completely covers the cervical os, increasing the risk of maternal hemorrhage, preterm birth, and neonatal morbidity. The incidence of placenta previa has been rising, primarily due to increasing maternal age and higher rates of cesarean sections. The specific location of the placenta in cases of placenta previa-whether anterior, posterior, marginal, or complete-can significantly influence maternal and neonatal outcomes. Understanding this relationship is crucial for effective risk assessment and management. Aim(s): This study aims to evaluate pregnancy outcomes based on the location of placenta previa, assessing its impact on maternal complications, neonatal health, and mode of delivery. Method(s): A retrospective observational study was conducted at JLN Medical College, Ajmer, including 100 cases of placenta previa. Data were retrieved from hospital records, covering maternal age, parity, mode of delivery, postpartum hemorrhage, blood transfusion requirement, neonatal birth weight, APGAR scores, and NICU admissions. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 23.0, with a p-value < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Result(s): Placenta previa was most commonly located in the anterior (27%) and posterior (26%) regions, followed by marginal (24%) and complete (23%) locations. Cesarean section was the predominant mode of delivery, occurring in 85% of cases, while 15% had vaginal deliveries, mainly in marginal placenta previa cases. Maternal complications were significant, with postpartum hemorrhage occurring in 25% of cases and blood transfusion required in 20%, the highest rates being in complete placenta previa cases. Neonatal outcomes were also notably affected, with a mean birth weight ranging between 2.8 and 2.9 kg. APGAR scores at five minutes were lowest in complete placenta previa cases (mean: 6.6), and NICU admissions were highest (43.4%) among neonates born to mothers with complete placenta previa. Conclusion(s): The study findings suggest that placenta previa location significantly influences maternal and neonatal outcomes, with complete placenta previa presenting the highest risks for cesarean delivery, postpartum hemorrhage, and neonatal complications. Early identification and timely management can help mitigate these risks, improving both maternal and neonatal health. Recommendations: Routine ultrasonographic assessment of placental location should be emphasized in antenatal care. High-risk cases should be managed with early hospitalization, close fetal monitoring, and a multidisciplinary team approach involving obstetricians, anesthetists, and neonatologists.
