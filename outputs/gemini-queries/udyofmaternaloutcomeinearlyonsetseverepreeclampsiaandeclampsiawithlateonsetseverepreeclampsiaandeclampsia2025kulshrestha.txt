"Background: Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) are prevalent complications, affecting 7-15% of gestations and contributing significantly to antenatal admissions. Pre-eclampsia, a key HDP, is stratified into early-onset (<34 weeks) and late-onset (>=34 weeks), with the former associated with more severe maternal and perinatal outcomes due to aberrant placentation. Eclampsia, a severe sequela of pre-eclampsia, can precipitate critical maternal and fetal complications. Maternal mortality remains a global health concern, particularly in regions such as Madhya Pradesh, India. Aims and Objectives: This study compares maternal outcomes in early-onset versus late-onset severe pre-eclampsia and eclampsia, specifically focusing on admission-to-delivery and induction-to-delivery intervals. Material(s) and Method(s): This prospective observational study was conducted in the Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology at G.R. Medical College and J.A. The study cohort comprised 400 women admitted with early or late-onset severe pre-eclampsia and eclampsia, diagnosed according to ACOG criteria. Participants were stratified into two groups: Group A (diagnosed between 20-34 weeks of gestation) and Group B (diagnosed at >=34 weeks). Inclusion criteria mandated severe pre-eclampsia or eclampsia with specific features such as hypertension and organ involvement. Exclusion criteria encompassed gestational age <20 weeks and pre-existing conditions such as chronic renal or hepatic disease to ensure the study's focus on outcomes related to severe pre-eclampsia and eclampsia.
