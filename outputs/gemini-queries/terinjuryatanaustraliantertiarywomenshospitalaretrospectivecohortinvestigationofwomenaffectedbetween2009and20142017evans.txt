"Introduction: The scope of this research was to assess the cumulative impact of sustaining an obstetric anal sphincter injury (OASI). Materials & methods: This research is a retrospective cohort study of women giving birth at the Royal Hospital for Women, Sydney between October 2009 and May 2014 who sustained an OASI during the birth. Results: Of the 600 potential participants, 200 women responded with completed questionnaires at a mean time of 4 years post-OASI birth. The time since birth to survey completion ranged from 16 to 79 months, with time period assessed to be a non-significant factor in those women testing positive for an ACO score. A positive ACO score was identified in 108 women (51%), with those who had experienced a more severe grading of tear (3c or 4th degree) statistically more likely to suffer an ACO (p = 0.029), as were those who had experienced a forceps birth (p = 0.03). The OASI was reported to have had an impact on daily life by 93 respondents (46%), with women reporting various physical, emotional, sexual and functional impacts. Regarding sexual function, of the 169 sexually active women 64% reported some degree of pain during sexual intercourse, of which 11% reported this was "frequently" or "always".
