"Background: Incisional hernia is a frequent complication of abdominal surgery, often resulting from poor fascial healing. Despite advancements in surgical techniques, its incidence remains significant. This study was conducted to assess the incidence and associated risk factors of incisional hernia in a cohort of 100 patients undergoing abdominal surgery. Method(s): This prospective observational study included 100 patients who underwent abdominal surgery over a two-year period. Patients were followed up postoperatively for one year to identify cases of incisional hernia. Risk factors such as obesity, diabetes, wound infection, type of incision, and postoperative complications were analyzed for association with hernia development. Result(s): Of 100 surgery patients, 14 developed incisional hernias, yielding an incidence rate of 14%. The majority of patients with hernias were female (71%) and aged 30-60 years (64%). Infraumbilical midline incisions were involved in 57% of hernia cases. The most significant associated risk factors were obesity (35.7%), wound infection (28.5%), and diabetes mellitus (21.4%). Conclusion(s): Incisional hernias are common postoperative complications. Risk is higher in patients with obesity, diabetes, and wound infections, especially following infraumbilical incisions.
