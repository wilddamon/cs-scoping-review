"Background: Preterm labor remains a leading cause of neonatal morbidity and mortality worldwide. Emerging evidence suggests that maternal hyperlipidemia may play a contributory role in the pathogenesis of preterm birth through mechanisms involving inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and placental insufficiency. Aim(s): To evaluate the association between maternal hyperlipidemia and the incidence of preterm labor, and to analyze the lipid profile variations across different gestational ages. Material(s) and Method(s): This prospective cohort study was conducted at Gandhi Memorial Hospital, Shyam Shah Medical College, Rewa (M.P.), from September 2022 to August 2023. A total of 1200 pregnant women between 24-32 weeks of gestation were included. Fasting lipid profiles (total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, triglycerides) were obtained and participants were followed until delivery. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS v22 and Epi Info v7.2.1. Result(s): Elevated total cholesterol and triglyceride levels were significantly more common in early preterm deliveries 17.2% and 20.7%, respectively) compared to late preterm and term groups (p < 0.001 and p = 0.026, respectively). LDL was also significantly higher in early preterm cases (mean = 116.5 mg/dL, p < 0.001). No significant variation was observed in HDL levels across gestational groups (p = 0.932). NICU admissions were most frequent in early preterm births (100%), establishing a strong association between dyslipidemia and neonatal morbidity. Conclusion(s): Hyperlipidemia, particularly elevated total cholesterol, triglycerides, and LDL, is significantly associated with early preterm labor. Early identification and management of lipid abnormalities should be integrated into routine prenatal care to reduce the risk of preterm birth and improve neonatal outcomes.Copyright (c)2025Int. J.
