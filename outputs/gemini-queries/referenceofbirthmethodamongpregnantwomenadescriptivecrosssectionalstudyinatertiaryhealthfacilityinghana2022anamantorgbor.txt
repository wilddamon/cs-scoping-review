"Background: Pregnant women have difficulty deciding on birth methods because many factors influence the choice. This study described the factors influencing pregnant women's preferred method of birth in a tertiary health care facility in Ghana. Methods: This descriptive cross-sectional study used a systematic sampling method to select 222 pregnant women who sought antenatal services at the Ho Teaching Hospital to respond to a pre-tested questionnaire. The data was entered into Epi info version 3.1 and transferred to STATA version 15.1 for analysis. Data were summarized into descriptive statistics and analyzed using the kh2 association test. Results: The results showed that 50.5% of the pregnant women had previously had a spontaneous vaginal delivery (SVD), 16.2% had cesarean section (CS), 5.9% had both CS and SVD, and 27.5% were first-time pregnant women. There was a significant (P-value <= 0.00, kh2 = 27.95) relationship between the birth experiences and preferred birth methods of pregnant women, as those who have a history of SVD will prefer the same (58.0%). Also, those who previously had a CS will prefer the same method (52.2%). There was a significant association (P-value <= 0.04, kh2 = 3.39) between the choice of delivery and respondent's parity. SVD was chosen by nulliparous (34.2%), para one (28.6%), and grand multipara (10.6%). Conclusion: The women's preferred choice for birth is primarily influenced by their previous experience of delivery, and the higher the parity, the likelihood of choosing SVD for subsequent childbirth. Pregnant women need to be educated more on the different methods of birth and the health benefits of midwives during antenatal so that they can make informed choices for their subsequent childbirth.
