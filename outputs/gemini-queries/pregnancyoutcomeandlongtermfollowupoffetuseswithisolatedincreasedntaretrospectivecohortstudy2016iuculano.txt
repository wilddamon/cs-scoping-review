Methods: Retrospective cohort study of all singleton pregnancies were referred for the 1st trimester screening for Down syndrome between January 2004 and December 2006. Cases with NT >95th percentiles who undertook fetal karyotype were analyzed. We evaluated the prevalence of the following: (i) abnormal fetal karyotype, (ii) associated structural abnormalities, (iii) genetic syndromes, and (iv) children needing treatment (i.e. medications or psychopedagogical intervention) at school age. Conclusions: Parents should be informed that fetuses with increased NT and normal karyotype need detailed ultrasound scans during pregnancy and detailed pediatric evaluation at birth; if these are normal, the risk from adverse outcome at school age is the same as for general population.
