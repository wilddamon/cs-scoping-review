"""abstract ID: postpartumvoidingdysfunction2018duncan
"Introduction: There are many risk factors for post-partum urinary retention (PPUR), defined as the absence of spontaneous micturition within six hours after vaginal delivery, or after removal of an indwelling catheter following caesarean section1. It is not known which factors are predictive of ongoing voiding dysfunction (VD). Current Clinical Practice Guidelines indicate that after measurement of two post-void residual volumes (PVRV) of greater than 150ml, an indwelling catheter is to be inserted. The clinical implication of PPUR is long term VD, the risk of which increases with residual volumes of over 500ml2. Thus, the aims of this project were to: a) Establish the predictive validity of a PVRV of 150ml for ongoing VD in post-partum women b) Determine a PVRV that can predict long term VD if 150ml residual volume is not predictive c) Establish whether there is an association between PVRV and symptoms as determined by Incontinence Questionnaire -- Female Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms (ICIQ-FLUTS) scores d) Determine the clinical characteristics of participants who have ongoing VD Materials and methods: This was a prospective observational study. Participants were post-partum women who had any of the following symptoms: no or altered sensation to void, inability to void within six hours of giving birth or catheter removal after Caesarean birth, overflow incontinence, lower abdominal pain, palpable bladder, or voided volumes of less than 100ml. Women with suspected or diagnosed urinary tract infection, severe perineal or abdominal pain were excluded. Participants gave written informed consent at study entry. Ethical approval was obtained from the Royal Women's Hospital Human Ethics Research Committee Reference number: 13/35. Demographic data was gained from medical records. All participants had usual hospital inpatient care following current guidelines for their PPUR symptoms. They then attended outpatient physiotherapy assessment sessions at seven and 14 days post-discharge, then at monthly intervals from one to four months. The primary endpoint was resolution of bladder symptoms as determined by a score of less than 10 on the International Consultation on ICIQ-FLUTS, or at four months post-partum. The association between PVRV and total score on the ICIQ-FLUTS was investigated using Spearman's Rank Order correlation (predictive validity). Concurrent validity was established by correlating the change scores in the ICIQ-FLUTS from baseline to endpoint with PVRV. Results from Receiver Operating Characteristics (ROC) analysis was used to determine the optimal cut-off PVRV for predicting VD. Results: During the recruitment period, June 2015 to April 2016, 122 women were screened. Ninety consented, 10 withdrew prior to assessment one, leaving 80 in the analysis. Mean (SD) age was 31.8 (5.0) years and 69 (86%) were primiparous. Sixteen (20%) had a prolonged labour (>700 minutes), 35 (44%) women were induced, 48 (60%) had an instrumental delivery, and 25 (31%) had post-partum VD. A PVRV of >=150ml, measured at the first outpatient assessment (13+/--7 days post-natal) predicted only 48% of cases of VD at one month, and 14% of cases of VD at four months post-partum. Given this finding, determining an optimal cut-off as per this protocol was not appropriate. However, the PVRV had good specificity (76%) for predicting ongoing VD and good negative predictive value (70%) at one month. Spearman's Rho of 0.034 (N=74, p=0.78) indicated no association between PVRV and ICIQ-FLUTS total score at one-month post-partum. This relationship was also investigated using the three subscales on the ICIQ-FLUTS. Associations were weak and non-significant. There was no significant difference in the prevalence of known PPUR risk factors between those with and without VD at 30 and 120 days post-partum. Three participants had residual volumes greater than 500ml during their inpatient stay. These participants had symptom resolution by 30 days post-partum after management by indwelling catheter. Conclusions: A PVRV >=150ml at first outpatient assessment predicted less than 50% of VD. It is therefore a better test of exclusion rather than a predictor of post-partum VD. We found no clear association between PVRV and both total and subscale ICIQ-FLUTS scores which may suggest that the cut-off used in this study was set too high and/or the PVRV set too low. Given that there was no optimal cut-off PVRV value identified based on the parameters tested, we suggest 150ml continues to be used, given that it helps to exclude VD. Poorer long-term outcomes have previously been shown with PVRV >500ml. Symptom resolution in the three women in this study with this high PVRV was likely due to adherence to Clinical Practice Guidelines. Current practice appears to be appropriate for early identification and effective management of post-partum VD. Competing interest statement: Australian Bladder Foundation Grant 2014--2015."""
