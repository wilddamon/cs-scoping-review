"Background: The effects of b-adrenergic blockers on the fetus are not well understood. We analyzed the maternal and neonatal outcomes of b-adrenergic blocker treatment during pregnancy to identify the risk of fetal growth restriction (FGR). Methods and Results: We retrospectively reviewed 158 pregnancies in women with cardiovascular disease at a single center. Maternal and neonatal outcomes were analyzed in 3 categories: the carvedilol (a/b-adrenergic blocker; a/b group, n=13); b-adrenergic blocker (b group, n=45), and control groups (n=100). Maternal outcome was not significantly different between the groups. FGR occurred in 1 patient (7%) in the a/b group, in 12 (26%) in the b group, and in 3 (3%) in the control group; there was a significant difference between the incidence of FGR between the b group and control group (P<0.05). The b group included propranolol (n=22), metoprolol (n=12), atenolol (n=6), and bisoprolol (n=5), and the individual incidence of FGR with these medications was 36%, 17%, 33%, and 0%, respectively. Conclusions: As a group, b-adrenergic blockers were significantly associated with FGR, although the incidence of FGR varied with individual b-blocker. Carvedilol, an a/b-adrenergic blocker, had no association with FGR. More controlled studies are needed to fully establish such associations. (c) 2016, Japanese Circulation Society.
