"Introduction. Choosing the right local anesthetic for spinal anesthesia in lower segment caesarean sections (LSCS) is crucial for effective anesthesia and smooth postoperative recovery. Ropivacaine and levobupivacaine are favored over bupivacaine due to their lower risk of heart-related side effects and shorter duration of motor block. This study evaluates the spinal block characteristics of hyperbaric ropivacaine, levobupivacaine, and bupivacaine in LSCS. Methods. We conducted a prospective, randomized, double-blinded clinical trial on 156 ASA II patients undergoing LSCS. The patients were randomly assigned to receive one of the following at the L3-L4 space: 0.5% hyperbaric bupivacaine 2 ml (10 mg) plus fentanyl 10 mcg, 0.75% hyperbaric ropivacaine 2.2 ml (16.5 mg) plus fentanyl 10 mcg, or hyperbaric levobupivacaine plus fentanyl 10 mcg. The primary outcome was the duration of sensory block. Secondary outcomes included motor block duration, hemodynamic profile, onset of anesthesia, block quality, recovery profile, complications, and APGAR scores. Results. Hyperbaric bupivacaine provided the longest sensory (131 +- 22 min) and motor block (142 +- 30 min) durations, significantly longer than ropivacaine and levobupivacaine (p < 0.05). Bupivacaine also had the quickest onset of anesthesia (9 +- 6 min, p = 0.02) and highest block quality (1.5 +- 0.4, p = 0.03). Ropivacaine facilitated the fastest recovery (p = 0.02). Complication rates were low and similar across groups (p = 0.8). APGAR scores at 1 and 5 minutes were comparable across groups (p > 0.05). Conclusion. Hyperbaric bupivacaine offers the longest and highest quality anesthesia for LSCS but slower recovery. Hyperbaric ropivacaine is ideal for rapid postoperative recovery. Hyperbaric levobupivacaine, with its intermediate characteristics, remains a viable option, hence making it a more suitable agent as it aids in quicker postoperative recovery but adequate depth of analgesia and anesthesia.
