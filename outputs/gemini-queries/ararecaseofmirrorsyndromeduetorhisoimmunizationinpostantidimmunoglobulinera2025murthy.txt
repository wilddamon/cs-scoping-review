"Aim and background:Mirror syndrome (MS), also known as Ballantyne syndrome, involves fetal, placental, and maternal edema. Incidence of MS is very low, with only 113 cases reported in the literature. There are multiple maternal, fetal, and placental etiologies for MS of which Rh-isoimmunization has significant incidence. The pathogenesis of MS is unclear, though angiogenic and antiangiogenic factors along with functional alteration in placenta similar to pre-eclampsia have been suggested. It can be frequently misdiagnosed as pre-eclampsia due to overlapping of clinical features. It is associated with higher incidence of fetal mortality and maternal morbidity. Case description: We describe a case of MS in a G2P1 woman, in second trimester due to Rh-isoimmunization, even after postnatal anti-D immunoglobulin (Ig) administration post her previous delivery. She had rapid onset of clinical features at 25 weeks of gestation despite being nonsensitized [as per her indirect Coombs test (ICT) status at booking and in second trimester] and no external or internal signs of fetomaternal hemorrhage leading to MS and intrauterine fetal death (IUFD). Indirect Coombs test was reported positive on day of diagnosis of MS. Conclusion(s): Mirror syndrome is a rare condition with only 113 cases reported in literature. Incidence of Rh-isoimmunization has drastically reduced following administration of anti-D immunoglobin post-delivery or abortion and also prophylactically in third trimester.
