"Background: Diagnosing intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH) in preterm neonates during the initial hours after birth remains challenging. A crucial step in advancing neonatal neuroprotection is the identification and validation of biomarkers that indicate brain injury. This study evaluates the combined utility of S100B and Activin A proteins as early predictors of IVH in preterm neonates. Material(s) and Method(s): This observational cross-sectional study was conducted on 75 preterm neonates with gestational ages ranging from 28 to 32 weeks. Serial cranial ultrasounds were performed postnatally, and participants were categorized into cases or controls based on the development of IVH. Levels of serum S100B and activin A were assessed during the initial 24 h after birth. Result(s): Serum activin A and S100B protein concentrations were significantly higher among the IVH group (1.42 +/- 0.23 mug/L, 0.19 +/- 0.06 mug/L) than among the control group (0.71 +/- 0.2 mug/L, 0.07 +/- 0.02 mug/L) (P = 0.001 for both comparisons). Both markers exhibited a positive correlation with IVH severity. Activin A, at a threshold of 1.2 mug/L, achieved a sensitivity of 85.7% and specificity of 92.4%. S100B, at a cutoff of 0.15 mug/L, demonstrated 75.0% sensitivity and 94.6% specificity. The combined use of these biomarkers achieved nearly 100% sensitivity and specificity. Conclusion(s): The findings suggest that serum S100B and Activin A are promising biomarkers for the early identification of IVH in preterm neonates before clinical and imaging evidence becomes apparent.Copyright (c) 2025 Journal of Clinical Neonatology."""
