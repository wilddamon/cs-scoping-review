The management of PDPH often includes prolonged bed rest; however, the impact of early ambulation on the incidence and severity of PDPH remains underexplored. Method(s): This prospective, randomized study included 140 women undergoing elective caesarean section under spinal anaesthesia. Participants were divided into two groups: Group R (n=70) remained in the supine position for 12 hours postoperatively, while Group A (n=70) began ambulation after 6 hours. Result(s): The incidence of PDPH was significantly lower in Group A (10%) compared to Group R (50%) (P<0.001). The onset of PDPH was delayed in Group A, with only 26.7% of cases occurring within the first 24 hours compared to 60% in Group R (P=0.015). Furthermore, Group A had a shorter hospital stay (P=0.031) and fewer complications, including nausea and vomiting (P=0.042 and P=0.038, respectively). Conclusion(s):Early ambulation significantly reduces the incidence, severity, and onset of PDPH, and is associated with shorter hospital stays and fewer complications in cesarean section patients.
