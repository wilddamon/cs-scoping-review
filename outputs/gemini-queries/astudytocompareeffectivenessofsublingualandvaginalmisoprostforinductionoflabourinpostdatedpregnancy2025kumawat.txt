"Background:Induction of labour is a common obstetric intervention performed to reduce the maternal and perinatal risks associated with post-dated pregnancy. Misoprostol, a synthetic prostaglandin E1 analogue, is widely used for this purpose due to its efficacy, stability, affordability, and availability in various routes of administration. However, the ideal route for misoprostol administration remains under debate.The present study aimed to compare the efficacy and safety of sublingual versus vaginal administration of misoprostol (25 mcg every 6 hours, maximum four doses) for induction of labour in postdated pregnancies. Method(s):This randomized prospective comparative study included 100 women with singleton pregnancies between 41+0 and 41+6 weeks of gestation, unfavorable Bishop scores (<6), and reactive non-stress tests. Participants were randomized into two groups: Group A received sublingual misoprostol, and Group B received vaginal misoprostol. Primary outcomes included the rate of vaginal delivery within 24 hours and induction-to-delivery interval. Secondary outcomes assessed cesarean indications, neonatal outcomes, and maternal side effects. Result(s):Baseline characteristics including maternal age, gestational age, and Bishop scores were comparable between groups. The rate of vaginal delivery was similar in the sublingual (40%) and vaginal (38%) groups (p = 0.832). Fetal distress was the most common indication for cesarean in both groups. Neonatal outcomes and maternal side effects showed no statistically significant differences between groups. The mean induction-to-delivery interval was slightly shorter in the sublingual group (7.55 +/- 1.01 hours) compared to the vaginal group (7.98 +/- 2.35 hours) (p = 0.528). Conclusion(s):Both sublingual and vaginal routes of misoprostol are effective and safe for induction of labour in post-dated pregnancies. Although the sublingual group showed a marginally shorter induction-to-delivery interval and fewer doses required, the differences were not statistically significant. The choice of route may therefore be individualized based on patient comfort, clinical setting, and practitioner experience.Copyright (c)2025Int. J. Life Sci. Biotechnol. Pharma. Res."""
