"Background: Changes in body temperature anticipate labor onset in numerous mammals, yet this concept has not been explored in humans. Methods: We evaluated patterns in continuous skin temperature data in 91 (n = 54 spontaneous labors) pregnant women using a wearable smart ring. In a subset of 28 pregnancies, we examined daily steroid hormone samples leading up to labor to analyze relationships among hormones and body temperature trajectory. During cross-validation AE-LSTM average error (true - predicted) dropped below 2 days at 8 days before labor, independent of gestational age. Labor onset windows were calculated from the AE-LSTM output using a probabilistic distribution of model error. For these windows AE-LSTM correctly predicted labor start for 79% of the spontaneous labors within a 4.6-day window at 7 days before true labor, and 7.4-day window at 10 days before true labor.
