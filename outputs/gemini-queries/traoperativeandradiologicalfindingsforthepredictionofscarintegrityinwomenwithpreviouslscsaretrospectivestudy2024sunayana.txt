"Background: Lower segment caesarean section (LSCS) is one of the most common surgical procedures done in obstetrics. The growing rate of caesarean sections globally, particularly in India, highlights the need for effective methods to assess the scar integrity. This study aims to compare the clinical findings (presence of scar tenderness) with radiological assessment (scar thickness) to predict intraoperative scar status in women with a history of previous LSCS. Methodology: This retrospective study involved 60 antenatal women with a history of previous LSCS. Data collection was done over six months, including clinical evaluation and transabdominal ultrasonography to measure scar thickness. Intraoperative findings were classified into normal, thinned-out, dehiscent, or scar rupture. Statistical analysis was performed to determine the correlation between clinical and radiological findings and results were tabulated. Result(s): In this study, the mean duration since the last LSCS was 4.02 +/- 2.9 years. Intraoperative scar status was categorised as normal (31.6%), thinned-out (56.6%), scar dehiscence (11.66%), and no cases of scar rupture. Clinical assessment showed scar tenderness with a sensitivity of 36.58%, specificity of 94.73%, positive predictive value (PPV) of 93.75%, and negative predictive value (NPV) of 40.90%. Radiological assessment of scar thickness had a sensitivity of 17.07%, specificity of 89.47%, PPV of 77.77%, and NPV of 33.33%. Scar tenderness demonstrated a significant correlation with intraoperative findings (p-value 0.011), whereas scar thickness did not (p-value 0.705). Conclusion(s): In this study, clinical examination for scar tenderness was a more reliable predictor of intraoperative scar status compared to radiological assessment of scar thickness. This study suggests that clinical evaluation remains a crucial tool for predicting scar integrity in women with previous LSCS, however the role of measurement of scar thickness radiologically has also shown promising results in a few studies. Also the measurement of scar thickness depends upon inter-observer and intra-observer error.
