"Background: Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR) is a significant obstetric challenge characterized by a fetus failing to achieve its genetically determined growth potential. Accurate prenatal diagnosis is essential due to associated adverse perinatal outcomes (APOs). Doppler ultrasound indices, including cerebroplacental ratio (CPR) and the newer cerebrouterine ratio (CUR), may improve risk stratification in late-onset FGR. Objective(s): To evaluate the diagnostic and prognostic value of CPR and CUR in pregnancies complicated by late-onset FGR. Method(s): This cross-sectional study included 100 antenatal women at 34-38 weeks gestation (50 FGR cases and 50 gestational age-matched controls) at Government Medical College, Amritsar. Doppler ultrasound measured pulsatility indices (PI) of the umbilical artery (UA), middle cerebral artery (MCA), and uterine artery (UtA). CPR (MCA PI/UA PI) and CUR (MCA PI/UtA PI) were calculated. Primary outcomes were CPR <1 and CUR <5th percentile. Secondary outcomes included adverse perinatal events such as non-reassuring fetal heart rate, low APGAR scores, cesarean for fetal distress, NICU admissions, and neonatal death. Result(s): Maternal age was comparable between groups, but BMI and symphysis-fundal height were significantly lower in FGR cases (p<0.05). Estimated fetal weight and placental maturity also differed significantly. Doppler indices revealed impaired fetal and uteroplacental circulation in FGR cases, with CPR <1 in 36% of cases vs. 2% controls (p=0.031) and CUR <=5th percentile in 60% of cases vs. 22% controls (p=0.001). CUR showed higher sensitivity (97.56%) and specificity (97.14%) for predicting APOs compared to CPR. Combined CPR and CUR improved negative predictive value to 98.59%, though positive predictive value decreased. FGR cases had higher rates of non-reassuring fetal heart rate, low Apgar scores, NICU admissions, and neonatal mortality. Conclusion(s): Reduced CPR and CUR are strong predictors of adverse perinatal outcomes in late-onset FGR, reflecting uteroplacental insufficiency. CUR demonstrated superior predictive performance, and combining it with CPR enhances risk stratification. These Doppler indices should be integrated into routine third-trimester surveillance to optimize fetal monitoring and timing of delivery. Larger studies are warranted to confirm combined predictive utility.Copyright (c)2025 Int. J. Life Sci. Biotechnol. Pharma. Res."""
