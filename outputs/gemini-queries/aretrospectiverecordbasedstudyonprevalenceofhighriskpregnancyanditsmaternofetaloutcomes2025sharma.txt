"Background: High-risk pregnancy (HRP) remains a significant contributor to maternal and fetal morbidity and mortality, particularly in low-resource settings. Early detection and management of high-risk conditions are crucial to improving perinatal outcomes. Aim(s): A Retrospective record-based study on prevalence of high-risk pregnancy and its maternofetal outcomes. Material(s) and Method(s): A retrospective record-based study was conducted in the Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology at PCMS & RC Bhopal, analyzing records of pregnant women who delivered between January 1, 2024, and December 31, 2024. High-risk pregnancies were identified using Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA) criteria. Maternal and neonatal outcomes were analyzed and compared between high-risk and normal pregnancies using descriptive statistics. Result(s): Out of 606 deliveries reviewed, 260 (42.9%) were classified as high-risk. The most prevalent risk factor was a history of previous 1 LSCS (43.46%), followed by hypertensive disorders (21.54%) and PROM (16.92%). Sociodemographic analysis showed a significant association between low socioeconomic status and high-risk pregnancy (p=0.02). High-risk pregnancies had a higher incidence of preterm births (16.5% vs. 10.6%) and low birth weight infants (18.4% vs. 8.6%) compared to normal pregnancies. Live birth rates were slightly lower in the high-risk group (90%) compared to the normal group (92.6%). Conclusion(s): Nearly half of the pregnant women were classified as high-risk, with hypertensive disorders being the most common contributor. High-risk status was significantly associated with adverse obstetric and neonatal outcomes.
