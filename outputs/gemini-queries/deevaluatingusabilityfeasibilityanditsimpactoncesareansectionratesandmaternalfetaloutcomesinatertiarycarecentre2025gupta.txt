"Background:Rising cesarean section (CS) rates and variable intrapartum monitoring practices are pressing global health issues. To address these, the World Health Organization Labour Care Guide (LCG) was developed as an innovative tool to promote structured, evidence-based labour monitoring and woman-centered care. The present study was undertaken to evaluate the usability and feasibility of implementing the LCG in a tertiary care setting, and to assess its potential impact on cesarean section rates and maternal-fetal outcomes. Method(s): A prospective interventional study was carried out involving 120 parturients, divided into two groups: Group A:- 60 participants were studied using standard partograph, and Group B:-60 participants were monitored with the WHO Labour Care Guide (LCG). Prior to LCG implementation, relevant staff underwent structured training. Data were collected on mode of delivery, maternal and neonatal outcomes, and staff perceptions regarding LCG usability, and were subsequently analyzed to assess the impact and feasibility of LCG integration in clinical practice. Result(s): Both groups were comparable at baseline with no significant differences in maternal age, parity, BMI, or gestational age. The LCG group showed higher usability, with 85% of staff finding it easy to use, 90% reporting feasibility, and 78% noting improved decision-making. LCG use was associated with an increase in normal vaginal deliveries (70% vs. 56.7%) and a reduction in cesarean sections (25% vs. 38.3%). Postpartum hemoglobin levels were lower, oxytocin use higher, and labor duration longer in the LCG group, though overall maternal complications were similar. Neonatal outcomes were favorable in both groups, with significantly fewer NICU admissions in the LCG group, and healthcare providers expressed high acceptability and satisfaction with its use. Conclusion(s): The WHO Labour Care Guide (LCG) is a feasible and user-friendly tool that enhances the quality of intrapartum care.
