In female caesarean section patients, this study compares hyperbaric bupivacaine and levobupivacaine for efficacy and safety. Methodology: This 14-month prospective, randomised, double-blind clinical experiment was conducted at Patna Medical College & Hospital, Bihar. At elective caesarean delivery, 91 women were randomly assigned hyperbaric or levobupivacaine (Group B, n=45 or Group L, n=46). Sensory and motor blockade start and duration were primary goals, hemodynamic stability, adverse effects, surgical analgesia, and maternal satisfaction were secondary. Conclusion(s): Hyperbaric levobupivacaine is safer and more successful for spinal anaesthesia in caesarean sections than hyperbaric bupivacaine because to its faster onset, better hemodynamic stability, fewer side effects, and higher maternal satisfaction.
