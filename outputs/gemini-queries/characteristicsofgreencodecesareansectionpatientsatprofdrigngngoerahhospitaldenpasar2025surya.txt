"Background: Maternal mortality rate (MMR) and infant mortality rate (IMR) are critical health indicators of a country's overall well-being. One contributing factor to high MMR and IMR is inadequate emergency management in hospitals. The "green code" cesarean section procedure was introduced to address this issue, emphasizing rapid response times as a key measure to reduce maternal and infant mortality. Method(s): This retrospective descriptive study analyzed medical records of pregnant women who underwent green code cesarean sections at Prof. Dr. I.G.N.G. Ngoerah Hospital Denpasar between January 2020 and December 2022. Result(s): A total of 123 green code cesarean sections were performed. The highest number occurred in 2020, with 47 cases (38.2%), primarily in multigravida patients (67%), and with preterm births in 60.2%. The most common indication for cesarean was fetal distress, accounting for 69.1% of cases. Most procedures (84%) were performed under subarachnoid block anesthesia. The average response time was 21.03 +/- 10.17 min. However, only 6.5% of cases met the hospital's response time standard of <=8 min, with response times ranging from 4 to 53 min. Conclusion(s): The current response times for green code cesarean sections do not meet the hospital's standards.
