"Introduction: Recent evidence suggests that ambulation may provide various benefits, such as pain relief, improved labor progression, and better fetal outcomes. The World Health Organization recommends encouraging mobility in labor, but its impact on high-risk pregnancies remains unclear. This study examines how ambulation during labor affects pain perception, fetal distress, mode of delivery, postpartum hemorrhage (PPH), and APGAR scores in high-risk pregnancies. Methodology: An observational study, with 200 participants. High-risk pregnancies (34 weeks gestation and beyond) were selected based on conditions complicating the pregnancy at the time of presentation. Participants were divided into two groups: ambulatory (Group A) and nonambulatory (Group B). Group A was encouraged to walk and change positions, with intermittent fetal heart rate monitoring, while Group B received continuous monitoring. Data on pain scores, fetal distress, mode of delivery, PPH, and APGAR scores were analyzed. Result(s): The ambulatory group had significantly fewer cases of fetal distress (11% vs 32%) and a lower cesarean section rate (11% vs 22%). Pain perception and incidence of PPH were similar between the groups. However, the ambulatory group had higher APGAR scores at 1 minute and 5 minutes with p-values of 0.006 and 0.044 respectively. Conclusion(s): Ambulation during labor in high-risk pregnancies can improve fetal outcomes and promote spontaneous vaginal delivery, with proper monitoring.
