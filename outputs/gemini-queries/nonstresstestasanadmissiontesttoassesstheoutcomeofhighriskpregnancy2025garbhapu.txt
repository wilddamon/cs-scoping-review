"Background: High-risk pregnancies are associated with increased perinatal morbidity and mortality. The Non-Stress Test (NST) is a simple, non-invasive method to assess fetal well-being. Aim and Objectives : This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of NST as an admission test in predicting perinatal outcomes among high-risk pregnancies. Method(s): A prospective observational study was conducted on 87 high-risk pregnant women admitted to the labor ward, Department of OBG, Prathima Institute of medical sciences, Karimnagar, Telangana. Detailed demographic, obstetric, and medical histories were recorded. NST was performed using an electronic fetal monitor on admission. Based on NST results, patients were categorized into reactive and non-reactive groups, and outcomes were compared. Result(s): Out of 87 patients, 67.8% had reactive NSTs and 32.2% had non-reactive NSTs. Cesarean delivery was significantly higher in the non-reactive group (82.1%) compared to the reactive group (30.5%). Low APGAR scores (<=7 at 5 minutes) were observed in 53.6% of nonreactive NST cases versus 6.8% of reactive cases. NICU admission (53.6% vs 6.8%), prolonged NICU stay (42.9% vs 3.4%), and early neonatal deaths (10.7% vs 0%) were notably more common in the non-reactive group. The findings were consistent with other national and international studies supporting NST's predictive value. Conclusion(s):NST is a reliable, low-cost screening test for fetal compromise in high-risk pregnancies.
