"Background: Preeclampsia is a hypertensive disorder that affects pregnant women, typically after 20 weeks of gestation, and remains one of the leading causes of maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality worldwide. Timely diagnosis and effective treatment are critical in preventing complications such as eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, and preterm birth. Various treatment strategies are employed, but comparative effectiveness data in real-world clinical settings remain limited, particularly in resource-constrained environments. Objective(s): To evaluate and compare the effectiveness of different treatment options for preeclampsia in terms of maternal and fetal outcomes among patients admitted at Patna Medical College and Hospital (PMCH) between October 2021 and October 2024. Method(s): A hospital-based observational study was conducted at PMCH, including 100 women diagnosed with preeclampsia. Data were collected retrospectively from patient records. Treatment modalities included antihypertensive medications, magnesium sulfate, delivery interventions (cesarean vs. vaginal), and conservative monitoring. Patient outcomes such as maternal complications, blood pressure control, birth weight, gestational age at delivery, and Apgar scores were analyzed. Statistical comparisons between treatment groups were performed using SPSS software. Result(s): The study found that the combination of antihypertensive drugs and magnesium sulfate was significantly more effective in controlling maternal blood pressure and reducing complications compared to other treatment modalities. Patients managed with early planned delivery had better maternal outcomes but were associated with increased rates of neonatal prematurity and low birth weight. Conservative management without pharmacological intervention resulted in higher maternal and fetal complications. Cesarean sections were more common among severe cases, particularly when urgent delivery was indicated. Conclusion(s): Combination therapy using antihypertensives and magnesium sulfate appears to be the most effective treatment strategy for preeclampsia in terms of maternal outcomes. However, a careful balance must be maintained to reduce neonatal risks.
