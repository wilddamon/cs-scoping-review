"Background: PROM is a critically important obstetrics, clinical and public health problem, occurs in 8-10% of term pregnancies. Time interval from PROM and onset of spontaneous labour show that 60-80% of patient begin spontaneously within 24 hour and 95% of patients begin labour within 72 hours. As the time between the rupture of membranes and labour increases, so may the risk of maternal and fetal complications. There is limited information regarding which approach is better. Objective(s): the present study is aimed to compare maternal and perinatal outcome of early induction vs delayed induction in women with PROM at term. Method(s): A prospective study was conducted at GMC&H Jammu, India.A total of 194 term pregnant patients with PROM fulfilling the inclusion criterion were selected and induction of labour was done.The two groups were analysed with respect to mode of delivery, maternal and neonatal morbidity. Result(s): In our study, 75.3% (73) of patients delivered vaginally and 24.7% (24) underwent LSCS in early induction group as compared to 63.9% (62) pts. delivered vaginally and 36.1% (35) underwent LSCS in delayed induction. Primary indication for LSCS was fetal distress(AFD) accounting for 75% (18) in immediate induction and 88.6%(31) in delayed induction group. 2.1% of neonates required NICU admissions in early induction group whereas 5.2% of neonates required admissions in delayed induction group. Conclusion(s):Our study reveal no significant differences in most maternal and neonatal outcomes but early induction can reduce PROM- delivery interval but did not significantly impact mode of delivery and NICU admissions .Therefore a personalised approach is recommended to induction by considering patient specific factors.Copyright (c)2025 Int.
