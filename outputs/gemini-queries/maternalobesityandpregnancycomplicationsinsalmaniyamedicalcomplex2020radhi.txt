"Objectives of the study: To determine the maternal and fetal outcome during pregnancy in pregnant women with high BMI and compare it with pregnant women with normal BMI in the Kingdom of Bahrain. Study design: Case control retrospective study. Study area: Salmaniya Medical Complex \ Labor room and postnatal wards.Manama - Kingdom of Bahrain. Population: The study included 200 women with high BMI at first antenatal visit (BMI >= 30), and 203 women with normal BMI (BMI 18.5-29.9) who had delivered at SMC between 16 july 2016 till 15 july 2017 Methods: Data collected from medical records in the labor room, postnatal wards and filling department 2at Salmaniya Medical Complex. Results: This study showed that pregnant women with high BMI had significant rise in recurrent pregnancy loss (16.5% vs. 9.35%, p-value = 0.019), gestational diabetes (19.5% vs. 11.82%, p-value = 0.025), and hypertensive disorders (20.5% vs.3.85%, p-value = 0.000) when compared to pregnant women with normal BMI. Cesarean section delivery was significantly more common in women with high BMI (54.5%) as compared to women with normal BMI (34%) (p-value = 0.000). They are more likely to have instrumental delivery (2.5%) as compared to (0.5%) of the normal BMI group (p-value = 0.004). Women with high BMI had an increased risk of postpartum hemorrhage (4.5% vs. 0.5%, p-value = 0.000), and wound infection (7% vs. 2.5%, p-value = 0.003). Other adverse outcomes were higher in the high BMI group as venous thromboembolism (1.5%) as compared to (0.5%) but the difference didn't reach significant level. No significant difference was noticed in high BMI women in preterm delivery and low birth weight when compared to women with normal BMI. As for fetal outcome pregnant women with high BMI carried a higher risk for stillbirth and neonatal death (4.5% vs. 0.5%, p-value = 0.000), and as well a higher risk for delivering babies with macrosomia (5.5% vs. 1%, p-value = 0.000), with low Apgar score (4.5% vs. 1%, p-value = 0.000), and with congenital anomalies (4% vs. 0.5%, p-value = 0.000). Conclusion: Obesity in pregnancy is a significant risk factor affecting both mother and fetus, and it associated with many adverse perinatal outcomes. Maternal obesity may be one of the most preventable risk factors for perinatal mortality and morbidity. Recommendations: * Postpartum nutritional counseling, Preconception counseling, careful prenatal management, tight monitoring of weight gain is advised in such cases, and longterm follow-up to minimize the social and economic consequences. * Obesity during pregnancy should be considered as high risk. * Obesity prevention efforts are needed to increase the number of women who are of healthy weight before pregnancy. * Multidisciplinary approach in such case is needed to improve the medical and surgical management which would be a major contribution to a safe pregnanc outcome for both mother and baby.
