"Background: Unquestionably, an emergency obstetric hysterectomy is a sign of severe maternal morbidity and, in many ways, the last resort for treating life-threatening disorders including severe postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) and uterine rupture. In no other obstetrical or gynecological procedure is the surgeon faced with a more difficult decision than choosing to perform an emergency hysterectomy. At the same time, the mother's potential to procreate is sacrificed, even though it is the last option to preserve her life. In this study, the prevalence, demographics, indications, and feto-maternal outcomes of emergency obstetric hysterectomy in a tertiary care center are investigated. Method(s): Present prospective, observational, and analytical study was conducted at Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Darbhanga Medical College and Hospital, Laheriasarai, Bihar from December 2023 to November 2024. A total of 56 cases of emergency obstetric hysterectomy (EOH) were done in this study period. Result(s): Our study found that the incidence of EOH was 12 after vaginal delivery and 44 after caesarean section. There were 56 cases for every 21,128 deliveries. The most frequent indication was uterine rupture (37.5%), which was followed by placenta accrete spectrum (10.7%) and atonic postpartum hemorrhage (25%). Disseminated intravascular coagulation (21.4%) and febrile morbidity (25.7%) were the most common consequences. The perinatal mortality rate was 51.7%, while the maternal mortality rate was 17.1%. Conclusion(s): When conservative surgical techniques are unsuccessful and interventional radiology is not readily available, a well-rounded approach to EOH may save lives.
