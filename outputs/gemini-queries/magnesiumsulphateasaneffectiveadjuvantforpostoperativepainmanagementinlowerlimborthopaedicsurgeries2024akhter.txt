"Objective: To observe the total analgesic requirement in the first 24 hrs of the postoperative period, if there is any prolongation of the time to rescue analgesia and incidence of complications in patients given Magnesium sulphate and the control group. Method(s): The study comprised 50 patients undergoing lower limb orthopaedic surgery who were ASA Grade I & II and were between the age of 20 and 65. After receiving spinal anaesthesia, 25 of these patients (Group A) got Magnesium Sulphate at a dose of 50 mg/kg in 100 ml bottles of normal saline, while the other 25 patients (Group B) received only NS (Group B). It was recorded when the first dose of analgesia was needed. Patients were watched for signs of sedation, hypotension, shivering, nausea, vomiting and pruritus. The total amount of analgesics needed in thefirst24 hours following the operation was noted. Result(s): In Group A and Group B, the mean +/- SD of the time to rescue analgesia was 444+/-130 minutes and 287+/- 55 minutes, respectively. The total analgesic requirement of Group A was significantly less as compared to Group B in the first 24 hours of the surgery. Incidence of complications were not significantly higher in the study group.
