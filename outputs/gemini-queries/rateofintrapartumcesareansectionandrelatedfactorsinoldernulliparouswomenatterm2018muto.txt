"AIM: The aim of this study was to determine the effect of maternal age on emergency cesarean section (CS) during labor, and to identify the independent risk factors for emergency CS. METHODS: This was a retrospective cohort study using data from 2009 to 2014 from a tertiary perinatal care center in Japan. Inclusion criteria were maternal age >= 35 years, nulliparous singleton pregnancy at term and a cephalic presentation without indication of elective CS at onset of labor. The primary outcome was rate of emergency CS, while independent risk factors were elucidated using multivariate logistic regression analysis. RESULTS: Of 953 women, 199 (20.9%) delivered by emergency CS. Rates of emergency CS were 18.3% (129/706) in women aged 35-39 years and 28.3% (70/247) in those aged >= 40 years (P < 0.01).
