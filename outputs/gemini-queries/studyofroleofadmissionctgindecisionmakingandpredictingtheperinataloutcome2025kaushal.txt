"Background: Continuous fetal surveillance during both the antenatal period and labour is essential to ensure timely detection of fetal compromise and reduce perinatal morbidity and mortality. Cardiotocography (CTG), simultaneously monitors fetal heart rate (FHR) patterns, uterine contractions, fetal movements during the CTG thus enabling early recognition of hypoxia, guiding interventions to prevent adverse outcomes Methods: This analytical study was conducted in the Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Government Medical College, Amritsar, from September 2023 to December 2024. A total of 200 antenatal women at >=32 weeks' gestation were enrolled, including 100 high-risk women (Group I) with risk factors for fetal hypoxia and 100 low-risk women (Group II). Admission CTG was performed on all participants, and only those who delivered within 24 hours of admission were included to enable accurate outcome correlation. Data were analyzed using SPSS; p < 0.05 was considered significant. Result(s): In the high-risk group, admission CTG was reactive in 71%, non-reactive in 22%, and ominous in 7%. Among reactive cases, 20 were managed expectantly due to prematurity. Cesarean section rates were higher in non-reactive CTG cases (63.63%) compared to reactive ones (39.21%) (p = 0.001; RR = 3.57). Abnormal CTGs (non-reactive + ominous) were significantly associated with increased fetal distress (71%; p = 0.0082), meconium-stained liquor (52%; p = 0.0065), NICU admission (47.4%; p = 0.0042), and neonatal resuscitation (82%; p = 0.005). Neonatal mortality in this cohort was 6.9%. All patients with ominous CTG underwent emergency cesarean (100%), with 80% requiring NICU care and resuscitation. In the low-risk group, the majority (89%) had reactive tracings and favorable outcomes, with significantly fewer interventions and neonatal complications. Conclusion(s): Admission CTG proved to be a valuable triage tool for early risk stratification in both high-risk and low-risk pregnancies. Reactive patterns predicted favorable outcomes, whereas non-reactive and ominous tracings were strong predictors of adverse perinatal events, underscoring the importance of timely intervention.Copyright (c)2025 Int.
