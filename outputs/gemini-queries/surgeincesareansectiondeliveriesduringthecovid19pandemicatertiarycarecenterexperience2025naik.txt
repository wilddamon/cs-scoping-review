"Introduction: Cesarean section rates have been rising steadily worldwide over the past few decades and further escalated during the COVID-19 pandemic. Disruption of maternal healthcare services, altered clinical decision-making, and concern for maternal-fetal safety during the pandemic may have contributed to this increase. Material(s) and Method(s): This hospital-based observational comparative study was conducted at a tertiary care center to evaluate the pattern of cesarean section deliveries during the pre-COVID and COVID periods. A total of 240 women were included, with 120 deliveries in each group. Maternal age, parity, gestational age, mode of delivery, type and indications of cesarean section, and neonatal outcomes were analyzed. Statistical analysis was performed using appropriate parametric and non-parametric tests, and a p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Result(s): The cesarean section rate increased significantly from 35.0% in the pre-COVID period to 56.7% during the COVID period (p < 0.001). Mean gestational age at delivery was significantly lower during the COVID period (37.4 +/- 1.9 weeks vs 38.1 +/- 1.6 weeks, p = 0.001). Neonatal birth weight was also significantly lower, and NICU admissions were nearly doubled during the pandemic (24.2% vs 11.7%, p = 0.01). Maternal age, sex of the newborn, parity, type of cesarean section, and major indications for cesarean delivery were comparable between the two periods. Conclusion(s): The COVID-19 pandemic was associated with a significant surge in cesarean section deliveries, earlier gestational age at birth, lower neonatal birth weight, and increased NICU admissions.
