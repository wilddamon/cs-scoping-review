"Background: Oxytocin, although routinely administered during caesarean sections to promote uterine contraction, is associated with abrupt hemodynamic changes such as hypotension, tachycardia, and reflex sympathetic activation. These can lead to maternal discomfort, nausea, vomiting, and compromised uteroplacental perfusion. Phenylephrine, an alpha1-adrenergic agonist, has shown promise in maintaining maternal blood pressure during spinal anaesthesia. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of phenylephrine in preventing oxytocin-induced hemodynamic alterations in parturients undergoing lower segment caesarean section (LSCS) under spinal anaesthesia. Method(s): This prospective, randomized, double-blinded study was conducted in the Department of Anaesthesiology, Anugrah Narayan Magadh Medical College and Hospital, Gaya, Bihar, from February 2020 to January 2022. A total of 120 pregnant women scheduled for elective LSCS under spinal anaesthesia were enrolled and randomly divided into two groups: Group P (received prophylactic phenylephrine infusion) and Group C (control group received normal saline). Hemodynamic parameters such as heart rate, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, and mean arterial pressure were recorded at baseline, during oxytocin administration, and at subsequent intervals. Result(s): Patients who received phenylephrine infusion (Group P) showed significantly better hemodynamic stability compared to the control group. The incidence of oxytocin-induced hypotension and associated symptoms like nausea and vomiting were markedly lower in Group P. The need for rescue vasopressors was also significantly reduced in the phenylephrine group. Conclusion(s): Prophylactic phenylephrine infusion is effective in attenuating the hemodynamic responses associated with oxytocin administration during caesarean section under spinal anaesthesia.
