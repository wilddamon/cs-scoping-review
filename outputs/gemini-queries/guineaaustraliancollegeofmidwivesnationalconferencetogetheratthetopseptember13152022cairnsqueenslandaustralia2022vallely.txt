"Papua New Guinea has among the highest rates of maternal and newborn mortality and stillbirths globally. The burden of maternal and newborn morbidity in this setting is less well described. Conducted in PNG, WANTAIM (2017- 2022) was a cluster randomised cross-over trial of testing and treatment for sexually transmitted infections among antenatal women. In line with good clinical practice, all serious adverse events among women and their newborns were classified and reported to relevant bodies. Information describing hospital admissions, maternal deaths, stillbirths, neonatal deaths, and other events was systematically collected using a study specific template ensuring standard, comprehensive information was collected for every event. We reviewed all serious adverse event reports among WANTAIM women and newborns for the duration of the study. We describe rates and causes of all events and factors associated with all stillbirths. Among 4,532 women we had 4,409 births. We report 1,006 adverse events including: 765 hospital admissions, 105 stillbirths, 56 early and 11 late neonatal deaths, 11 maternal deaths, 30 congenital abnormalities, 25 miscarriages and 3 cases of domestic violence. There were 439 maternal admissions, 16% were emergency caesarean section, 15% due to pre-eclampsia and 10% were due to malaria. Among the 326 newborn admissions 33% had neonatal sepsis, 21% were born preterm, and 13% had meconium aspiration syndrome. The stillbirth rate and perinatal mortality rates were 23.4 and 36.5 per 1,000 births, respectively. Among all stillbirths we identified 178 avoidable factors associated with the adverse outcome. We identified a high burden of perinatal deaths and maternal and newborn morbidity among a well characterised study cohort. In this cohort, potentially preventable causes and avoidable factors were identified that require health system strengthening, particularly the upskilling of primary level health staff, as well as targeted health education of women and the community."""
