"INTRODUCTION Pain has been ranked as the highest undesirable clinical outcome associated with cesarean section under subarachnoid block. Melatonin is a potential perioperative adjunct pain medication with minimal adverse effects. AIM To determine the effectiveness of preoperative sublingual melatonin (3 mg and 6mg) on postoperative pain intensity and supplemental analgesic requirement. METHODS A Prospective, double-blind, clinical study was conducted among parturient undergoing elective Lower Segment Cesarean Section (LSCS) with pfannensteil incision under subarachnoid block. A total of 60 parturient were divided into 3 groups (n = 20 per group); Participants in Group A received 6 mg of Tab. Melatonin, those in Group B received 3 mg of Tab. Melatonin and Group C were taken as the placebo group. Melatonin was administered one hour before surgery. The postoperative pain intensity was measured using Visual Analog Scale (VAS). Rescue analgesia, adverse effects, and patient satisfaction were also recorded. Hemodynamic parameters were recorded at 0 minutes, 30 minutes, 1 hour, 2 hours, 4 hours, 8 hours, 12 hours, 16 hours, 20 hours, and 24 hours after administering Melatonin. RESULTS Pain scores were significantly higher in the placebo group from 8 hours to 24 hours (P < 0.05), with similar pain scores between 6 mg and 3 mg of Melatonin. Consumption of rescue analgesia was significantly more in the placebo group and the least adverse effects were observed among those taking 6 mg followed by 3 mg of Melatonin. Satisfaction levels were higher among participants taking 6 mg followed by 3 mg of Melatonin. CONCLUSION Our study found out that there is an equivocal response between melatonin 6 mg and 3 mg. Additionally, compared to a placebo, there was a reduction in postoperative pain scores, less need for rescue analgesia, stable hemodynamic parameters, a lower postoperative nausea and vomiting and significant patient satisfaction.Copyright (c) 2025, Anka Publishers.
