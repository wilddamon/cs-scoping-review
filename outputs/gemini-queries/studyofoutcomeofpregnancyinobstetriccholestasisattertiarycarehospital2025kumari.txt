"""abstract ID: studyofoutcomeofpregnancyinobstetriccholestasisattertiarycarehospital2025kumari
"Background: The most prevalent liver condition during pregnancy is obstetric cholestasis, has better prognosis for the mother; with symptoms resolving after delivery. But has higher risk of recurrence in future pregnancy. This condition has significant implication for the fetus, which include increased risk of preterm birth, respiratory problem and still birth. This study aims to investigate the effects of obstetric cholestasis on maternal and perinatal outcomes throughout pregnancy. Method(s): Eighty cases were included in this prospective observational analysis. Obstetric cholestasis was diagnosed based on the presence of persistent pruritus, either localized or generalized, biochemical evidence of altered liver function, and the exclusion of other liver and skin disorders. Everyone was given access to medical care and active management, which included fetal monitoring and pregnancy termination at 37-38 weeks. Perinatal and maternal outcomes were examined. Result(s): 1.9% of women had obstetric cholestasis. Among multiparous women, the recurrence rate was 61.1%, and the majority of women (55%) were primigravida. The primary symptom of 90% of patients was widespread pruritus, which was discovered in the third trimester, or after 28 weeks, in the majority of cases (88.7%). Caesarean sections (CS) were performed on 43.7% of women, which is a fairly high occurrence. Preterm labor (25%), postpartum hemorrhage (13.75%), and preterm premature rupture of membranes (11.25%) were the most frequent pregnancy problems. Meconium aspiration (20%), preterm birth (30%), low birth weight (35%), fetal discomfort (18.75%), and intrauterine fetal mortality (2.5%) were the perinatal outcomes. 43.75% of newborns needed to be admitted to the neonatal critical care unit. Serum transaminase levels tended to be higher in patients with poor perinatal outcome such as still birth, fetal distress and meconium stained amniotic fluid. LFT (liver function test) returned to normal in 95% of women and 100% women became symptom free after 6 weeks postpartum. Conclusion(s): Early detection of obstetric cholestasis through meticulous clinical examination and biochemical testing is crucial since it has a negative impact on the fetal prognosis.
