"Background: Adolescent pregnancy continues to be a public health concern, especially in low-resource settings, due to its association with adverse maternal and neonatal outcomes. Teenage mothers are at higher risk of poor antenatal care utilization, obstetric complications, and compromised fetal health compared to adult mothers. Aim(s): To compare maternal and fetal outcomes among teenage and adult pregnant women delivering in a tertiary care center. Method(s): A comparative cross-sectional study was conducted at a tertiary hospital over OneYear, involving 60 participants: 30 adolescent (13-19 years) and 30 adult (20-35 years) pregnant women. Data were collected using structured proformas, focusing on sociodemographic details, antenatal care, obstetric history, delivery mode, and neonatal outcomes. Statistical analysis was done using SPSS version 23.0, with p<0.05 considered significant. Result(s): Teenage mothers had significantly higher illiteracy (40% vs. 13.3%) and belonged more often to lower socioeconomic classes (66.7% vs. 33.3%). Adequate ANC visits were significantly lower in adolescents (56.7% vs. 86.7%, p=0.01). Mean birth weight was significantly lower in neonates of teenage mothers (2.45 +/- 0.38 kg vs. 2.88 +/- 0.36 kg; p<0.001), with a higher incidence of low birth weight (60% vs. 20%, p=0.002) and APGAR scores <7 at 1 min (26.7% vs. 6.7%, p=0.04). NICU admissions and neonatal complications like jaundice and sepsis were also more common among the adolescent group. Conclusion(s): Teenage pregnancy is significantly associated with poor antenatal care and adverse neonatal outcomes such as low birth weight and low APGAR scores. These findings emphasize the vulnerability of adolescent mothers and the need for focused healthcare strategies.
