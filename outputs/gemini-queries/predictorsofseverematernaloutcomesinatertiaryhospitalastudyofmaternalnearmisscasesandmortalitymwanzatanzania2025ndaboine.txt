"Background: Severe maternal outcomes (SMOs), encompassing maternal near-miss events and maternal deaths, serve as essential indicators of the quality of maternal healthcare and continue to pose a major public health challenge in resource-limited settings. Sub-Saharan Africa experiences the highest global rates of maternal mortality, largely attributed to delays in accessing care and insufficient healthcare resources. This study examined SMOs among women admitted for obstetric-related conditions over seven months at Bugando Medical Centre (BMC) in Mwanza, Tanzania, aiming to shed light on these outcomes' burdens, causes, and predictors. Method(s): This retrospective cross-sectional study, conducted from November 2021 to May 2022, included 287 women with obstetric-related conditions who delivered at Bugando Medical Centre. Maternal near-miss cases and deaths were identified using the WHO Severe Maternal Outcomes tool. Descriptive statistics summarized demographics, while chi-square and Fisher's exact tests identified factors associated with SMOs (p<0.05). Significant variables underwent multivariate logistic regression. Result(s): Among 3,071 deliveries, 175 cases of Severe Maternal Outcomes (SMOs) were recorded, including 138 maternal near-miss cases (78.9%) and 37 maternal deaths (21.1%). The Severe Maternal Outcome Ratio was 56.9 per 1,000 deliveries, the Maternal Near Miss Ratio was 44.9 per 1,000 deliveries, and the Maternal Mortality Ratio was 1,204 per 100,000 live births, with a near-miss to mortality ratio of 3.7:1. Significant risk factors for SMOs included admission delays >30 minutes (OR: 6.3, p=0.033), admission to special wards (OR: 33, p<0.001), and facility referrals (OR: 6.1, p<0.001). Worse outcomes were more common among those with fewer than four antenatal care visits (81.3%) and higher parity (>=4) at 83.6%. Conclusion(s): Severe maternal outcomes (SMOs) remain a major concern.
