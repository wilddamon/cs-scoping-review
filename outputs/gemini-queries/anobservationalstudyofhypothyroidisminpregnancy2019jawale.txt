"""abstract ID: anobservationalstudyofhypothyroidisminpregnancy2019jawale
"Objectives: To establish the prevalence and the effect of hypothyroidism on pregnancy outcomes in Indian population. Material(s) and Method(s): The study cohort comprised of 952 consecutive pregnant women in the first trimester attending the antenatal clinic of a tertiary center in Pune, India. TSH levels were estimated. Patients with serum TSH > 2.5uIU/ml, were treated with adjusting doses of thyroxine with their TSH monitored every 4 weeks and doses adjusted accordingly. Subjects were followed until delivery. Result(s): Out of 952 patients, 836 were Euthyroid, 116 were Hypothyroid (43 overt hypothyroid and 73 subclinical hypothyroid). Prevalence of hypothyroidism in this study population was 12.2%, prevalence of subclinical hypothyroidism being 7.7% and that of overt hypothyroidism 4.5%. Maternal and Perinatal complications like PIH, IUGR, Birth asphyxia, Preterm birth, LBW, Still birth were increased in the hypothyroid group, although statistical significance was proved only in case of PIH (0.02), Birth asphyxia (0.04) and low birth weight (0.01) in our study. Conclusion(s): Our study shows high prevalence of subclinical and overt hypothyroidism among Indian pregnant women with associated adverse pregnancy outcomes. Based on the results of the present study we therefore suggest for a decreased threshold for screening and detection of hypothyroidism among Indian pregnant women attending to routine antenatal clinic and to be potentially aware of associated maternal and fetal complications so as to improve maternal and perinatal outcomes.
