"Background: Fraction of exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) is used for diagnosing and monitoring asthma in children, but the influence of allergic sensitization is still poorly understood. Here, we investigate how asthma and allergic sensitization influence FeNO levels during childhood. Methods: We investigated the associations between asthma, aeroallergen sensitization, and FeNO measured from age 5-18 years in the COPSAC<inf>2000</inf> birth cohort of 411 children using repeated measurement mixed models adjusted for gestational age, sex, concurrent airway infection, inhaled corticosteroids, and tobacco exposure. Results: In the COPSAC<inf>2000</inf> cohort, 133 had asthma between age 5 and 18 years, and in the COPSAC<inf>2010</inf> cohort, 112 had asthma between age 5 and 10 years. However, asthma was associated with increased FeNO among children with aeroallergen sensitization: 1.44 (1.23-1.69), p <.0001, whereas asthma was associated with decreased FeNO among nonsensitized children: 0.80 (0.65-0.99), p =.05 (p-interaction<.0001 for asthma x sensitization). Conclusion: Fraction of exhaled nitric oxide is elevated through childhood in children with asthma and is correlated with asthma-associated traits depending on the presence of aeroallergen sensitization.
