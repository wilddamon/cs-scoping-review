"Background: Corticosteroids, thiopurines, and biologics may come into play during pregnancy in women with infammatory bowel disease and potentially impact the developing fetal immune system. We aimed to assess the risk of serious infections in children stratifed by in utero exposure to biologics and immunomodulators or concomitant treatment with corticosteroids. Methods: All singleton IBD pregnancies between 2008 and 2022 at a tertiary IBD center in Denmark were included. Maternal and offspring demographics, maternal disease activity, antenatal medical treatment, and infant infections resulting in hospital admission were recorded after review of medical records. Results: In 602 live births (99.0%), we registered exposure to antenatal treatment as follows: biological monotherapy (n = 61, 10.2%), thiopurines (n = 110, 17.9%), biologics and concomitant thiopurines (n = 63, 10.3%), and controls (ie, no treatment with biological and/or thiopurines; n = 369, 60.6%). Preterm delivery (<37 gestational weeks) and systemic steroid administration during the third trimester were associated with an increased risk of serious infection in the offspring immediately after birth (relative risk = 17.5; 95% confdence interval, 7.8-39.8; P < .001, and relative risk = 4.8; 95% confdence interval, 1.5-12.7; P = 0.003, respectively). Intra-uterine exposure to biologics or combination treatment were not associated with a statistically signifcant higher risk of serious infections compared with controls; however, combination treatment showed an inclination towards an increased risk across analyses. Conclusion: Preterm birth and systemic corticosteroid administration late in pregnancy are signifcant risk factors for serious infections in the offspring of IBD mothers. (c) The Author(s) 2024.
