"People who experience human trafficking (HT) have complex medical and psychosocial needs. THRIVE, a clinic serving individuals who have experienced HT in South Florida, has shown promise as a model of care for pregnant persons who have experienced HT. This unique population requires support to empower women to have safe pregnancies. While some published studies report on overall pregnancy outcome, few studies have reported on maternal fetal outcomes. To describe outcomes in pregnancy among people who have experienced HT, we conducted a retrospective record review and included patients who had at least one appointment at THRIVE. Between January 2016 and March 2023, 58 pregnant patients who experienced HT were seen at THRIVE. The majority (88%) were of racial minority, primarily Black/African American or Hispanic/Latino and 36% were either unhoused or lived in a shelter. Most pregnancies were unplanned or a result of rape and violence that occurred during HT. Approximately 80% of patients reported substance use with 28% reporting active use within one month of delivery. Ninety percent reported a comorbid psychiatric disorder with 81% diagnosed with post-traumatic stress disorder. Major antenatal outcomes included inpatient antepartum admission (28%), gestational hypertensive disorder (28%), fetal growth restriction (24%), and anemia (59%). Delivery outcomes included a 38% cesarean section rate, and no patients underwent trial of labor after cesarean (TOLAC). Major neonatal outcomes included an average gestational age at delivery of 35.4 weeks and infants with low birth weight (21%). Major postpartum outcomes included anemia with an average hemoglobin of 9.3 g/dl after delivery. Findings from the THRIVE cohort demonstrate pregnant patients who have experienced HT have a high prevalence of major adverse pregnancy outcomes. Identifying prenatal conditions and improving outcomes for pregnancy helps achieve health equality and empower all patients with a history of HT in furtherance of UN Sustainability Goal 4.
