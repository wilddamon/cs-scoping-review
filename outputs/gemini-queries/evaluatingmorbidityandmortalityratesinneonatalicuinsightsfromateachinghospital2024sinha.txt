"Background: A newborn's first 28 days of life are particularly susceptible to illness and problems. Neonatal Intensive Care Units play a critical role in reducing neonatal mortality rates. Inequities in healthcare access contribute significantly to elevated neonatal mortality rates, particularly in low-income countries. Objective(s): The purpose of this study is to examine morbidity and mortality patterns in a teaching tertiary care hospital's NICU to identify key neonatal health trends and intervention areas. Methodology: A retrospective study was conducted at the Department of Pediatrics, Kurji Holy Family Hospital, Patna, India. Data from 96 neonates admitted to the NICU were collected and analyzed, encompassing demographics, birth weight, primary diseases, and mortality outcomes. The diagnosis was conducted according to WHO standards, and statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 21. Result(s): 67.7% of the 96 newborns were male, and 56.2% were delivered outside the hospital. LSCS constituted the predominant mode of delivery, accounting for 58.3% of cases. Low birth weight, defined as less than 2.5 kg, was observed in 54.2% of cases. The primary causes of morbidity included prematurity (33.3%), sepsis (30.2%), and birth asphyxia (12.5%). The overall mortality rate was 19.8%, with prematurity accounting for 47.4% and sepsis for 26.3% of the cases. Mortality rates were higher in males (63.2%) and in neonates delivered outside the hospital (63.2%). Conclusion(s): Low birth weight is a major source of morbidity, and premature delivery and sepsis are the main causes of neonatal mortality in the NICU.
