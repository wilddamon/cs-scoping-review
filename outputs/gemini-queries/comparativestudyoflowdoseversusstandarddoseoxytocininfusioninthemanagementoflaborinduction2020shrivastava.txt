"Background:Oxytocin is widely used for labor induction, but the optimal dosing strategy-whether low-dose or standard-dose-remains a subject of clinical debate. Balancing efficacy in labor progression with maternal and fetal safety is crucial for selecting the appropriate regimen. Aim(s):To compare the effectiveness and safety of low-dose versus standard-dose oxytocin infusion in the induction of labor at term with respect to maternal and neonatal outcomes. Material(s) and Method(s):This prospective, comparative study was conducted at a tertiary care teaching hospital with 100 term pregnant women aged 18-35 years undergoing labor induction. Participants were randomized into two groups: Group A (n=50) received a low-dose oxytocin infusion starting at 1 mU/min, and Group B (n=50) received a standard-dose regimen beginning at 4 mU/min. Dose escalation followed institutional protocols, and outcomes assessed included labor progression, delivery mode, uterine hyperstimulation, fetal heart rate abnormalities, postpartum hemorrhage, Apgar scores, and NICU admissions. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 16.0, with p<0.05 considered significant. Result(s):Both groups were comparable in baseline characteristics. The standard-dose group had significantly shorter time to active labor (4.8 +/- 1.5 vs. 6.2 +/- 1.8 hours) and overall labor duration (8.3 +/- 2.1 vs. 10.1 +/- 2.4 hours; p<0.001). However, uterine hyperstimulation (20% vs. 6%; p=0.04) and non-reassuring fetal heart rate patterns (24% vs. 10%; p=0.05) were significantly more frequent in the standard-dose group. No statistically significant differences were observed in delivery mode, postpartum hemorrhage, Apgar scores, or NICU admissions. Conclusion(s):Standard-dose oxytocin accelerates labor progression but is associated with a higher risk of uterine hyperstimulation and fetal distress. The low-dose regimen, while slower, demonstrated a safer profile with comparable delivery and neonatal outcomes. Individualized dosing may optimize labor induction outcomes.Copyright (c)2020 Int. J.
