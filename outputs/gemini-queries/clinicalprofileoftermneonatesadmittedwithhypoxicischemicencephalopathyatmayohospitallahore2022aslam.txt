"Objective: To determine clinical profile, associated factors, and outcome of term newborns admitted to the NICU with Hypoxic-Ischemic Encephalopathy (HIE). Study Design: Cross-Sectional Observational study Place and Duration of Study: Study was conducted in NICU Paeds Medicine Unit-I, King Edward Medical University, Lahore (January 2020 To December 2021). Material(s) and Method(s): All term newborns admitted with clinical diagnosis of Hypoxic-Ischemic Encephalopathy (HIE) were included by consecutive sampling. The baseline characteristics, antenatal and perinatal associated factors,severity of HIE, and outcome were noted. The Chi-Square test was applied to compare the severity of HIE among the stratified data, and a p-value 0.05 was regarded as significant. Result(s): Total 2330 newborns were admitted in (NICU), 302(12.96%) were with HIE. Majority were male (63.2%), with mean age 14.10 +/-39.82 hours (median of 2.75 hours). Most neonates had mild HIE (55.6%), moderate and severe in 12.3% and 32.1%. Mortality occurred in 113 (37.4%) neonates, highest in severe HIE (93.8%), moderate (43.2%) and mild HIE (3.6%). Antecedent factors like presence of skilled birth attendant,place, mode of delivery,regular antenatal visits were associated significantly with severity of HIE. Similarly, need for respiratory support, ionotropic drugs, fits, multi-organ damage, and abnormal cranial ultrasound scan were also significantly associated with severe HIE (p-value 0.05). Conclusion(s): Hypoxic-Ischemic Encephalopathy carries high case fatality owing to severity at presentation and higher complication rates. The severity of HIE is found to be associated with several modifiable antenatal and perinatal factors. If such factors are addressed timely, the outcome of these neonates may improve.Copyright (c) 2022, Pakistan Pediatric Journal. All rights reserved."""
