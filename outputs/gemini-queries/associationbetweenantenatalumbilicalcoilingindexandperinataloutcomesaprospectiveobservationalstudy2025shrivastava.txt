"Background:The umbilical coiling index (UCI) reflects the structural characteristics of the umbilical cord. Abnormal coiling patterns, such as hypocoiling and hypercoiling, have been linked to adverse maternal and neonatal outcomes. Measuring UCI antenatally may help in predicting perinatal risks. Objective(s):To determine the association between antenatal umbilical coiling index measured during the second trimester ultrasound and maternal and neonatal outcomes. Method(s):This was a prospective observational study conducted over 24 months at the Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, LN Medical College and JK Hospital, Bhopal. A total of 120 pregnant women with singleton pregnancies were enrolled during their second trimester and followed until delivery. Based on the antenatal UCI, participants were classified into normocoiled (10th-90thpercentile), hypocoiled (<10thpercentile), and hypercoiled (>90thpercentile) groups. Maternal and neonatal outcomes were recorded and analysed using appropriate statistical tests. A p-value <0.05 was considered significant. Result(s):Among 120 participants, 76.7% had normal coiling, 13.3% were hypercoiled, and 10% were hypocoiled. Gestational hypertension, polyhydramnios, oligohydramnios, and antepartum haemorrhage were significantly more frequent in the abnormal UCI groups. Preterm birth was more common in the hypercoiled (50%) and hypocoiled (58.3%) groups compared to normocoiled (16.3%) (p=0.0003). NICU admission rates were also higher in abnormal UCI groups. Intrauterine foetal death occurred in one case each in hyper-and hypocoiled groups. SGA/IUGR was significantly associated with both abnormal UCI patterns. Conclusion(s):Abnormal antenatal UCI, whether hypo-or hypercoiled, is associated with increased risk of adverse perinatal outcomes. Antenatal assessment of UCI during the second trimester may serve as a useful tool for risk stratification and early intervention.Copyright (c)2025Int. J. LifeSci.Biotechnol.Pharma.Res."""
