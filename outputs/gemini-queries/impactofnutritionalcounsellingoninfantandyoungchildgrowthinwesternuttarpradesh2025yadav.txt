"Background and Aim: Multiple inappropriate Infant and Young child feeding (IYCF) practices are prevalent in community which have significant implications on child's health. The study was designed to assess impact of nutritional counsellingon IYCF practices in children (6 month to 2 Years) attending OPD clinic in tertiary care hospital in Western Uttar Pradesh. Material(s) and Method(s): This before-and-after intervention study included mothers and their infants attending immunization clinic of Department of Paediatrics, GIMS, Greater Noida, over a period of 18 months. Result(s):A total of 210 infants were enrolled.Breastfeeding was initiated within 30 minutes of birth in 84 (40%) infants and 154(73.3%) received colostrum within 5 days.Almost 205(97.6%) received prelacteal feed in form of honey,ghutti. 167(79.5%) were exclusively breastfed for 6 months.Complementary feeding between 6-9 months was initiated in 133 (63.3%).At baseline, moderate undernutrition was present in 22 (10.5%) children and severe undernutrition in 13 (6.2%). After nutritional counselling, prevalence decreased to 3.3% for moderate and 1% for severe undernutrition. Vaginal delivery was strongly associated with successful first breastfeeding (p < 0.001). Severe undernutrition was lower in children of educated mothers (p = 0.004). Post counselling, complementary feeding practices improved, with increased intake of appropriate foods and decreased consumption of junk food. Conclusion(s): Emphasis should be given to IYCF education program during all contacts with eligible mothers.
