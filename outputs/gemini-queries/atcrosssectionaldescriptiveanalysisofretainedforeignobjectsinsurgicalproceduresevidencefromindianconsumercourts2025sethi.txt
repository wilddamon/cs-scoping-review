"Background The inadvertent leaving of surgical instruments post-surgery inside the patient's cavity is called as retained surgical foreign body [RFB]. Except few narrative reviews, radiological pictorial review and multiple case reports from India, structural analysis of Medico-Legal cases of RFB could not be found on conducting a literature review. When patients allege medical negligence in India they can seek compensation by approaching National Consumer Dispute Redressal Commission. In cases of RFB reaching the NCDRC, our objective is to document the verdict of the court, types of RFB involved, specialities of doctors implicated in these cases, surgeries commonly leading to RFB etc. Material and Methods This is a cross-sectional descriptive analysis. Judgments were accessed from https://www.scconline.com/. Keyword search was used and medical negligence cases containing the keywords 'Mop', 'sponge', 'gauze' etc where the doctor was being sued for RFB were included and analyzed. Statistical tests included Chi-square test and Mann Whitney U test Results Twenty two judgments met the inclusion criteria. Verdict in 18 cases [81%] favored the patients. Median compensation awarded was 3.35 lakh. Gynecologists [11, 50%] and General surgeons [36%] were the most commonly implicated specialties. Nonmetallic instruments [64%] were retained more frequently than metallic items [36%]. The type of foreign body did not affect either the amount of compensation awarded [U = 26.5, critical value = 12], the duration of recovery of Patient [p = 0.2], or the time elapsed before the object was discovered. [U = 32.5, P = 0.45]. Conclusions Nonmetallic RFB are more common than their metallic counterpart with surgical mop being most common overall. Gynecologists was the speciality against whom max no of cases were filled, with caesarean section being the most common surgery which lead to RFB. The type of RFB or speciality of the doctor did not affect the amount of compensation that was awarded Statement of Significance The findings underscore the judiciary's role in recognizing RFB as a preventable medical error and emphasize the need for stringent surgical protocols, enhanced intraoperative vigilance, and adoption of technological innovations to reduce such incidents and improve patient outcomes.Copyright (c) 2025 Wolters Kluwer Health, Inc.
