"Background: Children are distressed by all surgical procedures. It will be harsher if the kids are preschoolers because they are a physiologically delicate age group and find the preoperative time to be very upsetting. In the present study we are going to use nebulized route to deliver the drug via a nebulizer. The drugs included are a combination of dexmedetomidine with midazolam and a combination of ketamine with midazolam. Method(s): The present prospective, randomised, double-blind study was conducted in the department of Anaesthesiology and Intensive care, Government Medical College and Associated Hospitals, Jammu. A total of 60 patients were randomly and equally placed in two groups; Group DM with 30 children each of whom received nebulised solution of dexmedetomidine 2mcgs/kg and midazolam 0.2 mg/kg; 30 minutes before induction of anaesthesia. And Group KM with 30 children each of whom received nebulised solution of ketamine 2 mg/kg and midazolam 0.2 mg/kg; 30 minutes before induction of anaesthesia Results: In group DM, the postoperative sedation score was 2.66 +/-0.479. In group KM, the postoperative sedation score was 2.16+/-0.746. Except for after 40 minutes, the difference in sedation score after surgery was determined to be significant. In group DM, the mean rank score for reaction to venipuncture was significantly higher compared to group KM and the mean rank sore for mask acceptance score was also significantly higher in group DM compared to group KM. Conclusion(s): The current study found that ketamine 2 mg/kg in combination with midazolam 0.2 mg/kg and dexmedetomidine 2 mcg/kg in conjunction with midazolam 0.2 mg/kg both offer effective surgical sedation and facilitate child-parent separation.
