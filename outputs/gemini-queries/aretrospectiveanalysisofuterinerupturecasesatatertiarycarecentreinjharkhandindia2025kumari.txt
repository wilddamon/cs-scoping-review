"Background: Uterine rupture is a life-threatening obstetric emergency that contributes significantly to maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality, especially in low-resource settings like India. It is often linked to previous cesarean sections, obstructed labor, and poor intrapartum care. Objective(s): To determine the incidence, clinical characteristics, risk factors, intraoperative findings, maternal and fetal outcomes, and postoperative complications associated with uterine rupture in patients admitted to a tertiary care hospital in Jharkhand, India. Method(s): This retrospective observational study analyzed 20 cases of uterine rupture admitted between September 2021 and February 2025 at a tertiary care hospital in Jharkhand. Data were extracted from medical records, including patient demographics, obstetric history, clinical presentation, surgical management, and maternal and fetal outcomes. Descriptive statistics were used for analysis. Result(s): Of the 20 uterine rupture cases, most patients were aged 20-25 years (35%) and from rural areas (60%). The majority were unbooked (70%) and multiparous. Previous cesarean section was the most common risk factor. Intraoperative findings frequently included hemoperitoneum and rupture at the lower uterine segment. Surgical interventions included uterine repair with or without tubal ligation and hysterectomy. Maternal mortality was low, but morbidity included hemorrhagic shock, sepsis, and bladder injury. Perinatal outcomes were poor, with a high rate of fetal loss. Conclusion(s): Uterine rupture remains a major obstetric challenge in rural India, predominantly due to unbooked status, prior cesarean scars, and delayed referrals.
