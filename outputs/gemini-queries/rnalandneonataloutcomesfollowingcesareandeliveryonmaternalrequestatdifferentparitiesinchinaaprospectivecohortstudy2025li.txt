However, there is a notable lack of research investigating the influence of parity on the maternal and neonatal complications linked to CDMR, particularly within the Chinese population. This prospective cohort study, conducted in China, aims to investigate the short-term maternal and neonatal complications associated with CDMR among low-risk nulliparous and parous populations. Methods The study cohort consisted of 336 women, including both nulliparous and parous (second birth and first cesarean delivery) who elected for CDMR. Comprehensive data regarding maternal and neonatal complications were collected during the perioperative period and throughout the first year postpartum. RESULTS: After adjusting for age-related factors, our findings reveal that nulliparous experienced a higher incidence of postoperative complications. Furthermore, the risk of hospitalization due to neonatal jaundice was significantly elevated among the newborns of nulliparous (P < 0.05). CONCLUSIONS: In comparison to parous (second birth and first cesarean delivery), nulliparous who underwent CDMR exhibited a higher incidence of short-term maternal and neonatal complications.
