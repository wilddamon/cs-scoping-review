"BACKGROUND: Multiple sclerosis (MS) is a chronic inflammatory disease affecting the central nervous system that is more prevalent in women, with the diagnosis often made during childbearing years. Relapse risk decreases during pregnancy and then increases in the postpartum period. Previously reported pregnancy-related complications in patients with MS (PwMS) have included small for gestational age infants, maternal infections, or placental complications. There is a paucity of evidence to guide decisions surrounding the use of high-efficacy disease-modifying therapies (DMTs). Pregnancy counseling now demands a nuanced discussion between neurologists and PwMS. Therefore, there is a need for more study-based evidence to help further guide the uncertainties surrounding pregnancy outcomes in PwMS under today's treatment paradigm of early high-efficacy therapy. OBJECTIVES: Determine peripartum related complications in PwMS on high-efficacy DMTs who have become pregnant. METHODS: Retrospective chart review of patients seen in the Duke MS clinic that had a diagnosis of MS and became pregnant; charts were identified using SlicerDicer. RESULTS: Nineteen patients were identified with a diagnosis of MS and pregnancy, with a total of 34 pregnancies. Average age was 33 at the time of delivery. Twenty-seven pregnancies were completed and 7 were terminated. Pregnancy complications included 6 hospitalizations, 2 diagnoses of preeclampsia, 1 diagnosis of gestational diabetes, and 4 infections consisting of varicella zoster, viral myocarditis plus group A strep, bacterial vaginosis, and chorioamnionitis. The most common form of delivery was cesarean section (69% vs 31%), with the most common gestational age being full-term (60%). DMTs included ocrelizumab (44%), natalizumab (12%), and glatiramer acetate (3%) with the range of length between last administration of DMT and delivery ranging from 7 to 15 months. Resumption of DMT in the postpartum period ranged from 13 days to 14 months. CONCLUSIONS: As early high-efficacy DMT use has been adopted, there has been uncertainty regarding family planning and appropriate preconception counseling. This retrospective study reports pregnancy outcomes in a contemporary cohort. The majority of deliveries resulted in fullterm births.
