"""abstract ID: riskfactorsandneonataloutcomesofpretermbirthacasecontrolstudy2022anushuya
"Background:Preterm birth is one of the major cause of perinatal mortality, short term as well as long term morbidity resulting in disability in later life. This is specially important in developing countries such as India., due to limited source of well established preterm care. The incidence ranges from 5-18% of the all births. In India, out of the 27 million babies that are born, 3.5 million are born premature. Many of the preterm births occur without any risk factors and the process leading to preterm birth is not well understood. Etiologies linked with preterm birth are varied, making it difficult to specify the cause of preterm labour. Aim(s):To evaluate the risk factors and neonatal outcomes in preterm delivery. Method(s):It is a retrospective case-control study, which had 101 preterm delivery as cases, with an inclusion criteria of mothers aged between 18-40 years with a single structurally normal fetus at 28 -36weeks 6 days of gestation with spontaneous onset of labor or with preterm premature rupture of the membranes (PPROM) and 2 consecutive term births as controls which took place on the same day as that of the preterm birth. All the informations were taken from the birth registry maintained in the labour ward of OBGYN department in SRM medical college and research institute, Kattankulathurbetween March 2021-2022. Result(s):Parity, complications of pregnancy (includes Anemia, hypertension, fetal growth restriction, oligohydramnios, antepartum hemorrhage, preterm premature rupture of membranes have been found to be associated risk factors with preterm labour. Study group had higher incidence of risk factors when compared to the control group (P<0.0001). When risk factors within the preterm group (early vs late) were analysed, incidence of anemia, hypertension, hypothyroidism, antepartum hemorrhage, previous history of preterm labour, fetal growth restriction were higher in the early preterm group, but had no statistical significance. Conclusion(s):Anaemiabeing the most commonly associated risk factor with preterm birth in our study, emphasises the need for anemia correction in pregnant mothers. By creating awareness and implementing compulsory adherence to the government reforms can help improve the nutritional status of the pregnant mothers. Early diagnosis and treatment of hypertension, thereby reducing the risk of fetal growth restriction and timely referral to a tertiary care centre can reduce the risk of preterm birth related complications.Copyright (c) 2022, Anka Publishers.
