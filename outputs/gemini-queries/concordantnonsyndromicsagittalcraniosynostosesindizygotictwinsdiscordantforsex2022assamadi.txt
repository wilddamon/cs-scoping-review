"Craniosynostosis is caused by the premature fusion of one or more cranial vault sutures causing abnormal skull development in infants. The pathophysiology of this rare condition, known for its great genophenotypic heterogeneity, is not well understood. A complex interaction of (epi) genetic variants and environmental factors are incriminated. Therefore, twin studies provide a valuable insight to the understanding of such multifactorial diseases. Dizygotic twin of different sex born at 28 weeks were referred for scaphocephaly at the adjusted age of 4 months. Libyan parents were a non-consanguineous couple in their thirties. CT scan with 3D reconstruction confirmed an isolated sagittal craniosynostosis. Both patients underwent strip craniectomy. To our knowledge, this is the first case report of concordant non-syndromic sagittal craniosynostoses in dizygotic twin discordant for sex. Two known and one potential risk factors -twin pregnancy, male twin and chorioamnionitis- were identified.
