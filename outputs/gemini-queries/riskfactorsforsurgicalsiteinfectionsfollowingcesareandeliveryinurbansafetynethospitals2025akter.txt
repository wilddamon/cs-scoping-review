DESIGN: Retrospective case-control study and multivariable regression analyses. SETTING: Multicenter urban hospital system. PARTICIPANTS: All women undergoing C-sections during 2023. METHODS: To identify risk factors for SSIs, patients suffering SSIs were compared to a propensity-matched control group (controlled for the following variables: age, body mass index, diabetes mellitus, American Society of Anesthesia (ASA) score, wound class, and duration of surgery). RESULTS: Of 4,642 C-sections performed, 73 SSIs were identified; 90% were detected after hospital discharge. CONCLUSIONS: For women living in areas of low socioeconomic status, most SSIs after C-sections are detected following hospital discharge. Women who are recent immigrants and living in areas of high poverty are particularly at higher risk.
