METHODS: A total of 89 FMF patients with a history of at least one pregnancy were included in this retrospective study. RESULTS: Overall, 47.2% of patients had FMF attacks during pregnancy (22.5%, 19.1% and 15.7% at the first, second and third trimesters, respectively). Presence of FMF attacks at the second (OR: 15.5, 95% CI: 1.43-167.0, p = 0.024) and third (OR: 20.6, 95% CI: 1.53-278.0, p = 0.023) trimesters was significantly associated with the development of APOs. CONCLUSION: In conclusion, presence of FMF attacks during pregnancy was associated with the increased risk of APOs and NICU admissions in pregnancies with FMF, alongside their potential association with miscarriage and preterm delivery.
