"The administration of intravenous fluids in labour is a commonly used clinical intervention. Recent research reports widespread clinical practice variation across Australia with intravenous fluids in labour identified as a clinical practice that requires improvement. Additionally, midwives have described a risk of harm to mothers and their babies with present practices. A current lack of evidence, formal clinical guidance and education contribute to clinical uncertainty. There is a major research gap around the impact of intrapartum intravenous fluids on maternal and neonatal outcomes. The aim of this research was to investigate the relationship between the volume of intrapartum intravenous fluid administration and maternal and neonatal outcomes. A retrospective cohort study was conducted in an Australian public tertiary referral public hospital between September 2021 and September 2022. Women with a singleton pregnancy, planning a vaginal birth admitted for labour and birth care between 37 weeks and 42 weeks gestation were eligible for inclusion. Hospital birth and postnatal data were obtained from the electronic medical records and paper fluid order documentation. Outcomes measures included epidural use, mode of birth, total labour duration, labour and birth complications and neonatal weight loss following birth. One thousand and nine women with birth and postnatal data were included. Preliminary analysis using multiple logistic regression is being conducted on the sample at the time of abstract submission. The findings of this research will be important to further midwifery and obstetric knowledge relating to the administration of intravenous fluids in labour and what impact this common practice may be having on mothers and their babies. Additionally, it will help to identify research priorities moving forward."""
