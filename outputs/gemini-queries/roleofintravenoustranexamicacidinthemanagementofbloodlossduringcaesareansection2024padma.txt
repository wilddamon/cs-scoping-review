"INTRODUCTION Cesarean section (C-section) is a common obstetric procedure, but it is also associated with a risk of significant blood loss. Despite significant progress in obstetric care, 125,000 women die from obstetric hemorrhage annually in the world. Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH), defined as blood loss of more than 500 mL after a vaginal birth or more than 1,000 mL after a C-section, is a leading cause of maternal morbidity and mortality worldwide. Tranexamic acid (TXA) is an antifibrinolytic medication that has gained increasing attention in the management of bleeding during surgeries, including C-sections. MATERIALS AND METHODS This study was carried out in the department of Obstetrics and Gynecology in a rural hospital in South India from 2023 to 2024. 100 mothers were taken for the study and 50 each were split into two groups: the study group, which received TXA, and the control group, which did not receive TXA. OBSERVATIONS AND RESULTS The Haemoglobin drop was an average of 0.4 in the study group and 0.9 in the control group (p value was significant at 0.01). 52% of subjects in control group had >250 ml of blood loss, whereas, only 9% of subjects in study group had >250 ml blood loss. DISCUSSION The World Maternal Antifibrinolytic Trial (WOMAN Trial), seeks to ascertain how early TXA administration affects hysterectomy, mortality, and other morbidities (blood transfusions, surgical procedures, and risk of non-fatal vascular events) in women with postpartum hemorrhage that has been clinically diagnosed. Several studies in the past have found that TXA is highly potent in management of blood loss after surgery and this study has also found similar results. CONCLUSION Tranexamic acid represents an important advancement in obstetric care for cesarean sections. Its inclusion in standard protocols for PPH prevention, especially in cesarean deliveries, can enhance maternal outcomes by reducing blood loss, lowering the need for blood transfusions, and decreasing PPH-related complications.Copyright (c) 2024 EManuscript Technologies.
