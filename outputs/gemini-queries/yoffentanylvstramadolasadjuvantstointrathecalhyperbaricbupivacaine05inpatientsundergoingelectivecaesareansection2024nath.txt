"Background: Spinal anesthesia is recommended worldwide for elective caesarean section surgery because to its efficacy, rapidity, blood loss reduction, and thromboembolic protection. It offers optimal operating circumstances, including thick analgesia and muscle relaxation. Aim(s): This research aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of intrathecal bupivacaine (0.5% heavy) with fentanyl or tramadol in elective caesarean sections. Method(s): Patients with elective neurosurgery planned for ages 20 to 60 who were ASA grade I or II were included in the research. Exclusion criteria included significant organ problems, pregnancy, ASA grade III/IV, and a history of analgesic use. Patients were allocated at random into 2 groups: Group F received fentanyl (20 microg) and bupivacaine, whereas Group T received bupivacaine and tramadol (20 mg). Result(s): The demographic profile, such as age, gender, and weight, were analogous between Group F (Fentanyl) and Group T (Tramadol), exhibiting no significant differences. Group F had markedly prolonged periods of analgesia, motor block, and sensory block, all with p-values <0.001. Group F had a delayed onset of motor block, but Group T demonstrated a more rapid start of sensory block. Post-operative pain levels in Group F were consistently lower than those in Group T at many intervals, with significant differences seen at 30, 60, and 120 minutes.
