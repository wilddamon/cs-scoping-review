"Introduction: Infant colic is a worldwide condition that often affects infants less than three months and lasts between one and five months. Although infantile colic is thought to be a self-limiting condition, it frequently causes parents concern. In this study, we looked into the frequency of sleep disorders among mothers of infants with infantile colic. Method(s): The study was retrospective and case-control. The study comprised two groups of 65 mothers with infants aged 14 days to 3 months referred to Yazd medical centers: 34 infants with colic and 31 healthy infants. Age, gender, type of milk consumed, mother's age, and type of delivery were included on a pre-prepared checklist used to collect data. Using the Pittsburg questionnaire, sleep disorders among mothers were investigated. After gathering the data, it was entered into SPSS software version 19 and statistical tests were employed to analyze the data. Finding(s): The study revealed that of the 65 infants evaluated, 50.8% were female and 49.2% were male. The average age of infants was 7.21 weeks, while the average age of mothers was 30.32 years. Between the two investigated groups, there was a statistically significant difference in the average Pittsburgh test score, sleep quality score, sleep delay score, sleep disorders score, and dysfunction score over the last three months of pregnancy. And the average of the aforementioned indices was significantly higher in mothers of infants with colic than in mothers of healthy infants.
