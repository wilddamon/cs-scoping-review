"""abstract ID: associationofbodymassindexwithgestationaldiabetesmellitusanindianviewpoint2022patwa
"India is considered as a "diabetes capital" of the world. The incidence of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) has doubled over the past few years and is coinciding with the obesity pandemic. The objective of this study was to investigate the association of body mass index (BMI) and GDM in Indian ethnicity women of Western Rajasthan, India. It was a prospective cohort study. Out of 200 recruited women, 183 women completed the follow-up. The BMI was recorded as per the World Health Organization (WHO) standards and GDM was diagnosed according to the International Association of the Diabetes in Pregnancy Study Group (IADPSG) criteria. Multiple logistic regression (MLR) was used to calculate the odds ratio (ORs) for the association between BMI and GDM and other predictors of GDM. The incidence of GDM was 36.06% in the current study. The proportion of women with BMI >= 25 kg/m2 who developed GDM was 47.6%. MLR documented BMI (adjusted odds ratio [AOR]:1.074, P = 0.048), age > 25 years (AOR: 2.292, P = 0.025), and history of GDM in previous pregnancy (AOR: 10.267, P = 0.001) to be independent risk factors for GDM. There was no statistically significant difference in the rate of adverse maternal and neonatal outcomes. However, an interesting finding of single raised glucose value was observed in 54 (81.8%) cases with 33 women having only increased "fasting" plasma glucose. A high incidence of GDM was noted in the study population and body mass index >= 25 kg/m2 has a significant association with the development of GDM. Previous history of GDM is another important predictor of GDM in the current pregnancy. Furthermore, it also emphasized the importance of early diagnosis and optimum sugar control in mitigating maternal and neonatal complications in GDM.Copyright (c) 2022, The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer Nature Switzerland AG."""
