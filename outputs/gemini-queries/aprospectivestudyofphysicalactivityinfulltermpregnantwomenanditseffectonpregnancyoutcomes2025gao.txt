"Background: The effects of physical activity (PA), especially brisk walking, on labor outcomes and pregnancy health have not been clearly established. This study aimed to investigate the relationship between PA and cervical ripeness and pregnancy outcomes in term pregnant women. Basic Procedures: A prospective cohort study was conducted with 52 pregnant women (30.7 +- 3.0 years old) at 37 weeks of gestation who wore accelerometers to measure PA until delivery. Participants were categorized into brisk walking >=1 h group (AW group, n = 32) and brisk walking <1 h group (BW group, n = 20) according to whether their average daily brisk walking time exceeded 1 h. (3) Daily brisk walking duration was significantly and positively correlated with cervical ripening, with longer durations associated with more favorable cervical ripening (OR = 6.98, 95 % CI: 1.26-38.58, P = 0.026). Principal Conclusions: Brisk walking for >=1 h per day in term pregnant women improves cervical ripening and reduces the need for artificial cervical ripening.
