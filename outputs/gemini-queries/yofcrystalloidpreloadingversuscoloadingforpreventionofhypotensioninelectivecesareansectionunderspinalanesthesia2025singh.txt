"Background: Spinal anesthesia is the preferred technique for elective cesarean sections but is frequently complicated by maternal hypotension, which can compromise both maternal and fetal outcomes. Various fluid management strategies have been employed to prevent this complication, with preloading and co-loading being the most common approaches. This study aimed to compare the efficacy of crystalloid preloading versus co-loading in preventing hypotension during elective cesarean section under spinal anesthesia. Method(s): This prospective, randomized study included 160 parturients (ASA I-II) undergoing elective cesarean section under spinal anesthesia. Participants were allocated into two equal groups: Group P (preload) received 20 ml/kg Ringer's lactate before anesthesia, while Group C (coload) received the same volume immediately after spinal administration. Hemodynamic parameters (systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, heart rate), incidence of hypotension, and neonatal outcomes (APGAR scores) were recorded and analyzed. Result(s): The coload group demonstrated significantly higher systolic and diastolic blood pressure values at all post-spinal time points (p<0.001). The incidence of hypotension was lower in the coload group (2.5%) compared to the preload group (7.5%), though this difference was not statistically significant (p=0.2765). Baseline diastolic blood pressure emerged as a significant predictor of hypotension (p=0.0015). Neonatal outcomes, as assessed by APGAR scores at 0 and 5 minutes, were comparable between groups. Conclusion(s): Coloading with crystalloids offers superior hemodynamic stability compared to preloading during spinal anesthesia for cesarean delivery.
