"Background: Caesarean section (C-section) is a common surgical procedure in obstetric practice, but it carries a risk of postoperative wound complications that can increase maternal morbidity and healthcare costs. This study aimed to assess the incidence, type, timing, and management of wound complications following C-section. Method(s): A prospective observational study was conducted among 152 women who underwent Caesarean delivery. Data were collected on demographic profile, clinical characteristics, type of surgery, intraoperative findings, and wound complications. Wound assessments were conducted on postoperative days 3, 7, and 14. Management strategies for complications were also recorded. Result(s): The mean age group was 30-34 years (26.3%). A higher proportion of patients had parity >=3 (30.9%), and 28.3% were obese (BMI >=30). Comorbidities were prevalent, with multiple conditions in 25.7% and anemia in 21.7%. The wound complication rate was 11.8%, with the most common presentations being discharge, redness, and inflammation, primarily on postoperative day 3. Symptoms resolved by day 14 in most cases. Conservative treatment with daily dressing and antibiotics was effective in 90% of cases, while only 10% required resuturing. Conclusion(s): Wound complications after Caesarean section, though infrequent, are clinically significant and typically occur within the first postoperative week.
