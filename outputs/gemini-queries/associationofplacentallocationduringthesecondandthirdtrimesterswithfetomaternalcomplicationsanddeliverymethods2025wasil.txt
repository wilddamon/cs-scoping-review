"Background: Placental location plays a crucial role in pregnancy outcomes by influencing uteroplacental blood flow, which affects both maternal and fetal health. Locations such as anterior, fundal, lateral, and low-lying have been associated with pregnancy complications, including preeclampsia, intrauterine growth restriction, and fetal distress. This study aimed to investigate the relationship between placental position during the second and third trimesters and maternal-fetal outcomes, related complications, and delivery methods. Materials and Methods: By the Helsinki Declaration, this prospective observational study was conducted at the Hind Institute of Medical Sciences in Sitapur, Uttar Pradesh, India. A total of 125 pregnant women who underwent ultrasound evaluations during the second and third trimesters were included in the study. Placental locations were classified as anterior, posterior, lateral, fundal, or low-lying. The study assessed the relationships between placental positions and maternal complications, delivery methods, and neonatal outcomes, such as birth weight, appearance, pulse, grimace, activity, and respiration (APGAR) scores, and neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) admissions. Results: Lateral placenta placement was notably associated with higher rates of preeclampsia (30%), eclampsia (20%), and premature rupture of membranes (PROMs) (20%). All cases of low-lying placenta led to cesarean deliveries (100%). Posterior and lateral placentas were linked to neonatal complications such as low birth weight and NICU admissions. Placental location significantly influenced fetal weight and APGAR scores. Conclusion: Placental locations, especially lateral and low-lying, are important predictors of maternal complications, cesarean section rates, and adverse neonatal outcomes.
