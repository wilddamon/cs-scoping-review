"""abstract ID: theeffectofscn9asinglenucleotidepolymorphismsonpostoperativepainandanalgesicconsumption2021karaman
"The aim of the present study was to evaluate the relationship of a postoperative pain after cesarean section with SCN9A single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) rs674030, rs7604448, rs10930214, rs7595255. A total of 199 pregnant women who underwent cesarean-section (cs) under general anaesthesia were included into the study. The postoperative pain intensity was evaluated at 0h (15th min at recovery room) and 2h, 4h, 6h, 8h, 12h and 24h after surgery. The total tramadol consumption dose and additional analgesic requirement were also recorded. A total of 4ml blood was collected from the peripheral antecubital vein of the patients into the ethylene diamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA)-coated vacutainer before the anaesthesia induction. Genomic DNA was isolated using a DNA isolation kit and the four single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) (rs6746030, rs7604448, rs10930214, rs7595255) were amplified using forward and reverse primers. High-resolution melting (HRM) analysis was performed to determine the SNPs. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software version 20.0 (SPSS Inc, Chicago, IL, USA). A p value of < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant. There were no statistically significant differences between postoperative pain scores and total tramadol consumption regarding the SNPs. However, the number of the patients with AA genotype of rs6746030 who used additional analgesic drug was statistically significantly higher than other genotypes (p=0,013). In conclusion, there was no association between SCN9A SNPs (rs7604448, rs7595255, rs10930214) with postoperative pain scores and total opioid consumption. The additional non-opioid analgesic requirement was slightly higher in the patients with AA genotype of rs6746030.Copyright (c) Nova Science Publishers, Inc."""
