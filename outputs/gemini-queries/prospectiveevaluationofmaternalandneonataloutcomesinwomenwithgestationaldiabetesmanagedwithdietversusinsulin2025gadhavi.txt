"Background: Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is a prevalent metabolic disorder of pregnancy that contributes significantly to maternal and neonatal morbidity. Management strategies generally begin with dietary modification and lifestyle changes, with insulin therapy introduced when glycemic targets are not achieved. The choice of treatment modality may influence maternal complications, delivery outcomes, and neonatal health, making comparative evaluation essential in clinical practice. Aim(s): The aim of this study was to prospectively evaluate and compare maternal and neonatal outcomes among women with GDM managed with diet alone versus those requiring insulin therapy in a tertiary care setting. Material(s) and Method(s): This prospective observational study was conducted at a tertiary care hospital and included 94 women diagnosed with GDM after 24 weeks of gestation using standardized oral glucose tolerance testing. Women with multiple gestations, pre-existing diabetes, or chronic comorbidities were excluded. Participants were stratified into two groups: the diet group (n=56), managed with medical nutrition therapy and physical activity, and the insulin group (n=38), requiring insulin in addition to dietary advice. Maternal data collected included age, BMI, gestational age at diagnosis, obstetric complications, and mode of delivery. Neonatal outcomes assessed included birth weight, Apgar scores, large-for-gestational age (LGA) and small-for-gestational age (SGA) status, hypoglycemia, hyperbilirubinemia, respiratory distress, NICU admission, and perinatal mortality. Result(s): Baseline characteristics were comparable between groups, except for BMI, which was significantly higher in the insulin group (27.64 +/- 3.42 vs 26.12 +/- 3.18 kg/m ; p=0.048). Maternal complications such as gestational hypertension (15.79% vs 8.93%) and preeclampsia (10.53% vs 5.36%) were more frequent in the insulin group but not statistically significant. Cesarean deliveries were higher among insulin-treated women (57.89% vs 42.86%). Neonates in the insulin group had significantly higher mean birth weight (3.26 +/- 0.46 vs 3.02 +/- 0.42 kg; p=0.022). Adverse neonatal outcomes including hypoglycemia (18.42% vs 7.14%) and NICU admissions (26.32% vs 14.29%) were more common in the insulin group, though differences were not statistically significant. Logistic regression identified preterm delivery (OR 4.85; p=0.010) and birth weight >=3.5 kg (OR 3.21; p=0.046) as independent predictors of NICU admission. Conclusion(s): Women with GDM requiring insulin therapy exhibited higher BMI and trends toward greater maternal and neonatal complications compared with diet-treated women. However, preterm birth and macrosomia emerged as the most significant determinants of adverse neonatal outcomes. Early risk stratification, strict glycemic control, and vigilant perinatal monitoring are essential to improve outcomes.Copyright (c) 2025Int. J. Life Sci. Biotechnol. Pharma. Res."""
