"Purpose: The objective of this study was to compare two salient neonatal outcomes-respiratory disorders and hyperbilirubinemia-between late-preterm (34-36weeks) and full-term (37-41weeks) singleton infants both at the population level and within families. For population-level models, logistic regression analyses were conducted to estimate unadjusted and adjusted differences in outcomes by gestational age. For within-family analyses, unadjusted and adjusted logistic fixed-effects models were estimated with the latter including factors that differed across births to the same mother. Results: Late-preterm birth increased the odds of a neonatal respiratory condition by more than fourfold (odds ratio, 4.08-4.53) and of neonatal hyperbilirubinemia by more than fivefold (odds ratio, 5.11-5.93) even when comparing births to the same mother and controlling for demographic and economic, behavioral, and obstetric factors that may have changed across pregnancies.
