"Background: Eclampsia, a severe manifestation of preeclampsia characterized by the onset of seizures, remains a significant cause of maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality, particularly in developing countries. Despite advancements in maternal healthcare, the management and outcomes of eclampsia continue to pose challenges, necessitating comprehensive studies to evaluate current practices and outcomes. This study aims to evaluate the maternal and perinatal outcomes of eclampsia, and to identify areas for improvement in management practices. Method(s): A prospective observational study was conducted, including 210 patients diagnosed with eclampsia. Data on demographic profiles, antenatal care, clinical and laboratory findings, seizure management, delivery methods, and maternal and perinatal outcomes were collected and analyzed. Seizure control was achieved using Magnesium Sulfate according to the Pritchard regimen. Statistical analysis was performed using the student t-test and ANOVA to determine significant associations. Result(s): The study revealed a maternal mortality rate of 4.8% and a neonatal mortality rate of 14.3%. Significant morbidities included postpartum hemorrhage (9.5%) and HELLP syndrome (7.1%) in mothers, and preterm birth (38.1%) and low birth weight (42.9%) in neonates. Antenatal care was significantly associated with reduced maternal and neonatal mortality (p = 0.001 and p = 0.002, respectively). The average length of hospital stay for mothers was 7.5 days. Conclusion(s): The findings highlight the critical role of antenatal care in improving maternal and perinatal outcomes in cases of eclampsia. Effective seizure management and timely delivery are essential components of care. However, gaps in healthcare infrastructure and access to antenatal services remain significant barriers. Recommendations: To enhance outcomes, there is a need for increased awareness and accessibility of antenatal care services, improved healthcare infrastructure, and the implementation of standardized management protocols for eclampsia.
