"Background: Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) remains a leading cause of maternal morbidity and mortality, particularly following lower segment Caesarean section (LSCS). Oxytocin, auterotonic drug, plays a pivotal role in preventing PPH by promoting uterine contraction. However, the optimal administration method-bolus versus infusion-remains a subject of clinical debate due to its impact on maternal hemodynamics, blood loss, and adverse effects. This study compares the effects of bolus and infusion regimens of oxytocin in LSCS patients to determine the optimal approach for maintaining uterine contractility and hemodynamic stability. Method(s): This prospective, double-blind, randomized comparative study was conducted at the National Institute of Medical Sciences & Research (NIMS), Jaipur, Rajasthan, over a three-year period (May 2021-October 2024). A total of 92 patients undergoing elective LSCS under spinal anesthesia were randomly assigned to two groups: the bolus group (3 IU IV bolus, followed by 2 IU after 3 minutes) and the infusion group (1 IU/min continuous infusion over 5 minutes). Pre-, intra-, and postoperativehemodynamic parameters (SBP, DBP, HR, SpO2), intraoperative blood loss, adverse effects, and uterine contractility were assessed and compared statistically using a significance threshold of p < 0.05. Result(s): Both groups had comparable demographic and pre-operative hemodynamic parameters (p > 0.05). During the intraoperative period, the bolus group exhibited a higher heart rate (87.952 +/- 5.831 bpm) compared to the infusion group (83.976 +/- 5.819 bpm, p = 0.002), while the infusion group had slightly greater blood loss (236.565 +/- 20.445 mL vs. 226.122 +/- 22.158 mL, p = 0.027). No significant differences were observed in SBP, DBP, and SpO2 intraoperatively. The bolus group had a higher proportion of "well-contracted" uterine tone (score 3), while the infusion group had more cases of "hard rock" uterine tone (score 4), indicating a sustained uterotonic effect. Postoperatively, the infusion group exhibited higher HR (79.143 +/- 9.153 bpm vs. 74.381 +/- 9.313 bpm, p = 0.020) and lower SpO2 (97.000 +/- 2.107% vs. 98.810 +/- 0.969%, p < 0.001), while SBP and DBP remained comparable between the groups. Adverse effects, including chest pain, headache, and nausea, were low and statistically insignificant between both groups. Conclusion(s): Both bolus and infusion regimens of oxytocin are effective in preventing PPH. The bolus method is preferable for rapid uterine contraction and reduced intraoperative blood loss, while the infusion method provides a gradual and sustained uterotonic effect but requires closer monitoring for hemodynamic changes. Clinicians should tailor oxytocin administration based on individual patient profiles and surgical conditions to optimize maternal safety and minimize complications.
