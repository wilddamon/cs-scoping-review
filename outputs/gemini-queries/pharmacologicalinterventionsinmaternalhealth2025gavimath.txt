"Background: Pharmacological interventions are essential for managing pregnancy-related complications, such as gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes, anemia, infections, and preterm labor. However, the safety and efficacy of these treatments need careful evaluation due to potential risks to both maternal and fetal health. Objective(s): To evaluate the pharmacological interventions used in maternal health, assess their efficacy and safety, and identify factors influencing their use in managing common pregnancy-related complications. Method(s): A prospective observational study was conducted at Navodaya Medical College Raichur, India -among 205 pregnant women diagnosed with various pregnancy-related complications. Data were collected on pharmacological treatments, including antihypertensive medications, insulin therapy, iron supplementation, corticosteroids, and antibiotics, as well as maternal and fetal outcomes. Result(s):Patients with pregnancy-related complications (n = 65) required more interventions, including antihypertensive treatment (43.1% vs. 10.7%, p < 0.001), insulin therapy (33.8% vs. 0%, p < 0.001), and corticosteroids (18.5% vs. 0%, p < 0.001). They had worse outcomes, including lower gestational age at delivery (37.8 +/- 2.0 weeks vs. 39.2 +/- 1.1 weeks, p < 0.001), higher rates of preterm birth (27.7% vs. 4.3%, p < 0.001), and more cesarean sections (49.2% vs. 20.0%, p < 0.001). Adverse effects like hypertensive crisis (6.2% vs. 0%, p = 0.01) and hypoglycemia (4.6% vs. 0%, p = 0.03) were more common in the complication group. Conclusion(s): Pharmacological interventions are critical for managing pregnancy-related complications but come with associated risks, including increased rates of preterm birth, cesarean deliveries, and adverse drug reactions.
