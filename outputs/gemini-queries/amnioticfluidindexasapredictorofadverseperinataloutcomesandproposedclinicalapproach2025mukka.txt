"Background: The Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI) is a major ultrasonographic element indicating fetal well-being. Oligohydramnios (AFI <5 cm) has been weaponized with elevated perinatal morbidity and mortality. Despite its wide usage, the predictive value of AFI to adverse perinatal outcomes and the applicability of AFI-based management strategy is the topic of clinical interest.This study evaluates the association between low AFI and adverse outcomes and proposes a stratified clinical management protocol to optimize maternal and neonatal outcomes through timely intervention. Method(s): This prospective observational study was conducted on 200 singleton pregnancies >=28 weeks gestation. Subjects were categorized into two groups based on AFI (<5 cm and >5 cm). Baseline characteristics, maternal complications, and perinatal outcomes were compared. Outcomes assessed included cesarean for fetal distress, meconium-stained liquor, low Apgar scores, umbilical artery pH, NICU admissions, and neonatal morbidities. Data were analyzed using SPSS. Multivariable logistic regression identified independent predictors of adverse outcomes. A clinical protocol was developed based on AFI stratification. Result(s):Of 200 participants, 40 had AFI <5 cm. This group showed significantly higher rates of cesarean delivery (60%), fetal distress (40%), meconium-stained fluid (35%), NICU admission (30%), and lower birth weight (p<0.05). Multivariable regression confirmed AFI <5 as an independent predictor of adverse perinatal outcomes (adjusted OR: 3.85; p<0.001). No significant differences were found in maternal comorbidities. These findings emphasize the role of AFI as a clinically useful predictor of perinatal risk, supporting targeted surveillance and timely obstetric intervention. Conclusion(s):Low AFI (<5 cm) in the third trimester is significantly associated with adverse perinatal outcomes, independent of maternal risk factors. Findings support the integration of AFI into routine antenatal risk assessment. A structured management protocol based on AFI levels can enhance fetal surveillance, guide clinical decisions, and potentially reduce perinatal complications. Routine monitoring and early intervention in pregnancies with oligohydramnios may improve neonatal outcomes, making AFI a valuable tool for clinicians managing high-risk pregnancies in the third trimester.Copyright (c)2025Int.
