"INTRODUCTION: Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy has been consistently associated with a higher incidence of adverse pregnancy outcomes. Previous studies mainly focused on the effects of intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy on pregnant mothers and fetuses, and few studies reported the postpartum growth and development of fetuses in pregnant women with intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy. The aim of this study was to investigate impact of maternal serum total bile acid levels on maternal and neonatal outcomes as well as child growth and food allergy. MATERIAL AND METHODS: A retrospective longitudinal cohort investigation was carried out among 751 pregnant women with intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy at 30-32 weeks of gestation from the Longitudinal Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy Study (LoICPS). RESULTS: In our cohort, the average maternal serum total bile acid level was 35.09+-30.02 mmol/L, with 58.8% of mothers suffering from mild intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy and 29.2% suffering from severe intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy.
