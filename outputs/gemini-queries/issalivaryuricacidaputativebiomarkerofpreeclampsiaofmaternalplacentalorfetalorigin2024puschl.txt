"OBJECTIVES: Increased salivary uric acid (sUA) represents a potential biomarker predictive of pre-eclampsia (PE), but its origin is unclear. The study explores whether sUA levels reflect maternal or feto-placental physiological stress and whether sUA levels in these cases correlate with amniotic fluid (fetal origin), maternal blood (maternal origin), or cord blood (fetal vs placental origin). STUDY DESIGN: Pregnant women (n = 39) undergoing amniotomy or caesarean section after 34 gestational weeks were designated into three groups of either maternal, feto-placental, or no signs of physiological stress: women (n = 15) in the established first phase of active labour and without any signs of fetal growth restriction (FGR) or PE were assigned to the maternal stress group, women (n = 6) with an ultrasound-based diagnosis of FGR, with or without PE, were assigned to the feto-placental stress group, and women (n = 18) not yet in active labour and without any signs of FGR or PE, were assigned to the control group.
