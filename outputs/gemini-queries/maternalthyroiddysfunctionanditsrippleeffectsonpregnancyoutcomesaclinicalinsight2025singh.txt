"Background:Thyroid dysfunction is among the most prevalent endocrine disorders during pregnancy, yet often remains underdiagnosed due to its nonspecific clinical presentation. Emerging evidence indicates that maternal thyroid disorders can significantly affect both maternal and foetal outcomes during and after pregnancy. Inadequate adaptation to the physiological thyroid changes of pregnancy may result in dysfunction and consequent feto-maternal complications. This study aimed to evaluate the thyroid dysfunction in pregnancy and its impact on maternal and perinatal outcomes. Method(s):This prospective observational study included 313 pregnant women attending the antenatal clinic or admitted to the Obstetrics and Gynaecology Department over 18months. Participants underwent detailed clinical evaluation and routine antenatal investigations, including serum TSH and free T4 measurements. Maternal outcomes (anaemia, preeclampsia, gestational diabetes mellitus, mode of delivery, etc.) and perinatal outcomes (gestational age at delivery, birth weight, SGA, LGA, NICU admission, perinatal mortality) were assessed. Result(s):Of the 313 participants, 84.03% were euthyroid, while 15.97% had thyroid dysfunction. Overt hypothyroidism and subclinical hyperthyroidism were significantly associated with increased risks ofgestational diabetes mellitus (p <0.05). Overt hyperthyroidism was also significantly associated with increased risk of antepartum haemorrhage (p <0.05). Both overt hypothyroid and hyperthyroid groups demonstrated higher incidences of low birth weight, SGA infants, NICU admissions, and perinatal mortality. Conclusion(s):Thyroid dysfunction, particularly overt forms, is linked to a higher incidence of adverse maternal and foetal outcomes.
