"Objective: To evaluate the incidence, indications, intraoperative findings, and outcomes of relaparotomies performed after obstetric surgeries in two tertiary care hospitals of Bihar. Method(s): A multicentric prospective observational study was conducted in the Departments of Obstetrics and Gynecology at Darbhanga Medical College & Hospital, Laheriasarai, and Sri Krishna Medical College & Hospital, Muzaffarpur, from March 2023 to April 2025. A total of 110 women who underwent relaparotomy after obstetric surgeries were included. Data regarding demographics, primary surgery, indications, operative findings, management, and maternal outcomes were collected and analyzed. Result(s): Out of 110 women, 71 (64.5%) had undergone cesarean section and 39 (35.5%) obstetric hysterectomy as their primary surgery. The most frequent indication for relaparotomy was hemorrhage (56.3%), followed by peritonitis/sepsis (25.4%), burst abdomen (10.9%), and intestinal obstruction (7.2%). The mean interval between primary surgery and relaparotomy was 3.9 +/- 2.1 days. Intraoperative findings included pelvic hematomas (36 cases), uterine wound dehiscence (19 cases), generalized peritonitis (28 cases), and bowel obstruction (8 cases). Surgical interventions included evacuation of clots, hemostatic suturing, peritoneal lavage, re-suturing of burst abdomen, and bowel resection where required. Postoperative morbidity occurred in 40 (36.3%) women, while maternal mortality was observed in 13 (11.8%) cases, mainly due to uncontrolled hemorrhage and septicemia. Conclusion(s): Relaparotomy after obstetric surgeries, though uncommon, is associated with significant maternal morbidity and mortality. Hemorrhage and sepsis remain the predominant causes. Early recognition of complications, timely surgical intervention, and improved perioperative care are crucial to improving maternal outcomes in tertiary care settings.Copyright (c)2025Int. J.
