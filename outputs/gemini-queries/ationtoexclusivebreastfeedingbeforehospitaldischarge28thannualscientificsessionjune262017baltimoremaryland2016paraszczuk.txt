"Background/Purpose: Research has identified numerous health benefits of breastfeeding for infants and mothers with longer breastfeeding duration. Although current breastfeeding initiation rates are high, a rapid decline in breastfeeding rates occurs over the first 6 months after birth. Several factors have been shown to affect breastfeeding success including the delivery method, mother's intention to breastfeed, and early breastfeeding experiences. Early breastfeeding difficulties may contribute to mothers doubting their ability to breastfeed resulting in use of artificial feeding. This may negatively impact milk supply and decrease breastfeeding duration. Further investigation of these factors is necessary to understand their effect on breastfeeding exclusivity. The purpose of this study was to explore the relationship of delivery method to mothers' intention to breastfeed, and newborns' initial breastfeeding duration to exclusive breastfeeding before hospital discharge. Theoretical Framework: Bandura defines self-efficacy as individuals believing in their ability to achieve certain outcomes. Self-efficacy may be affected in mothers who are anticipating surgical birth or struggle with breastfeeding initiation. Methods: This sample consisted of women delivering healthy term newborns who were placed in skin-to-skin contact within sixty minutes after birth (N =185). Results: Results indicated that significantly fewer mothers admitted for planned cesarean deliveries intended to breastfeed as compared to those who expected vaginal deliveries (p =.05). For mothers who intended to breastfeed, there was a significant difference in the rate of exclusive breastfeeding before hospital discharge for newborns whose initial breastfeeding session was longer in duration (p=.03). Additional analyses revealed no significant differences between delivery method, gravida and para of the mothers and infant birth weight. Conclusions/Implications: These findings indicate that mothers who know they will have a surgical birth are less likely to breastfeed their infants. With an increased number of planned cesarean deliveries occurring presently, nurses need to provide appropriate guidance and assistance to encourage these women to breastfeed. For women who intend to breastfeed, strategies to increase initial breastfeeding duration may improve the rate of exclusive breastfeeding before discharge.
