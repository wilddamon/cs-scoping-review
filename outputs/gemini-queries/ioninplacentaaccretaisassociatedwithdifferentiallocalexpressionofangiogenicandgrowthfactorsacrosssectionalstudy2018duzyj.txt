"Objective: Placenta accreta is clinically associated with maternal uterine scar. Our objective was to investigate the biochemical contribution of maternal scarring to hyperinvasive trophoblast. Design: Cross-sectional series. Setting: Yale-New Haven Hospital. Population: Women with histologically confirmed normal and abnormal placentation. Methods: Placental invasion site tissue sections were immunostained for endoglin and other angiogenic regulators, and transforming growth factor b (TGFb) proteins. Maternal serum endoglin, and the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) mediators hypoxia-inducible factor-1a (HIF1a) and endostatin, were assessed using immunoassay. Maternal serum endoglin decreased in praevia and accreta when corrected for gestational age. Conclusion: Angiogenic and growth factors at the placental invasion site are altered in accreta, both by decidual absence and within myometrial scar. We postulate this promotes the invasive phenotype of placenta accreta by activating hyperinvasive trophoblast and by dysregulating placental vascular remodelling.
