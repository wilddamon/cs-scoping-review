"Objective: This study compared the impact of dynamic ultrasound monitoring versus digital examination on second-stage labor (SSL) delivery outcomes, with the primary outcome being cesarean delivery rate and secondary outcomes including perineal lacerations, postpartum hemorrhage, and neonatal complications, providing scientific evidence to optimize obstetric decision-making in the delivery room. Method(s): A semi-randomized trial included 60 pregnant women with slow second stage of labor (SSL) at hospital (June 2023-December 2024), divided into an ultrasound group (observation group, n = 30) and a digital examination group (control group, n = 30). Neonatal outcomes (infections, injuries, asphyxia) were similar (P > 0.05). The observation group had higher assisted vaginal delivery success (86.7 % vs. 66.7 %, P < 0.05) and lower CS rates (13.3 % vs. 33.3 %). AOP (AUC = 0.89, cutoff = 125degree) and HPD (AUC = 0.92, cutoff = 2.7 cm) were strong predictors. Conclusion(s): AOP and HPD are crucial ultrasound markers for predicting successful vaginal assisted delivery in SSL. Combining these parameters (AOP >125degree and HPD <=2.7 cm) improves decision-making accuracy. Dynamic intrapartum ultrasound monitoring, compared to digital examination, optimizes delivery decisions, reduces unnecessary CS and perineal trauma, and improves outcomes.
