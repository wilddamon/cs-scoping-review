"Background and Objective: Effective labor analgesia is essential for reducing labor pain, decreasing cesarean delivery rates and promoting reproductive health. Good analgesia is an important basis to ensure the quality of delivery of pregnant women. Therefore, this study explored the analgesic effect of remifentanil-controlled analgesia and combined spinal epidural anesthesia in the delivery of pregnant women. Material(s) and Method(s): As 90 cases of labor and delivery were selected and performed in the People's Hospital of Chongqing Liangjiang New Area between August, 2020 and July, 2022, dividing them into group A (n = 40, intravenous loading tramadol followed by remifentanil self-administration) and group B (n = 50, subarachnoid injection of ropivacaine and fentanyl followed by an epidural analgesic pump). The Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) scores, plasma norepinephrine and epinephrine levels in different stages of labor and neonatal Apgar scores between the two groups after administration of the drug were compared. Result(s): There was no statistically significant difference in VAS scores between the two groups before analgesia (p>0.05) and the VAS scores of patients in group A were significantly lower than those in group B at 5, 10, 15, 30 and 60 min of analgesia (p<0.05). Blood norepinephrine and epinephrine levels were significantly lower in group A than in group B (p<0.05). Apgar scores of neonates in both groups were evaluated 5 min after delivery and compared between groups, showing no statistically significant difference in Apgar scores between the two groups (p>0.05). The overall complication rate in group A was 50% (20/40), which was statistically significant (p<0.05) compared to 82% (41/50) in group B. Conclusion(s): Remifentanil self-administration has a positive effect on reducing pain during labor among multiparous women, which may be related to its ability to reduce maternal norepinephrine and epinephrine secretion.Copyright (c) 2023 Cong Wang et al."""
