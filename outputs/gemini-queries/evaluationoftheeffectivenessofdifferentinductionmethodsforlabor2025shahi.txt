"""abstract ID: evaluationoftheeffectivenessofdifferentinductionmethodsforlabor2025shahi
"Background: A crucial obstetric procedure, labour induction aims to guarantee safe results for both the mother and the baby by stimulating uterine contractions prior to the start of spontaneous labour. Common induction methods include the use of Prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) gel, oxytocin infusion, Foley catheter insertion, and combinations of these. Nevertheless, it is crucial to consistently assess these approaches in designated clinical contexts to determine their relative efficacy and safety. Objective(s): The purpose of this study was to assess the efficacy of various ways of inducing labour among pregnant women admitted to Patna Medical College and Hospital (PMCH) in terms of induction-to-delivery time, mother and newborn outcomes, and overall satisfaction. Method(s): A Retrospective observational study was conducted from October 2021 to October 2024, comprising two hundred pregnant women who were using an induction method for labour. Separated into four groups, participants were given either PGE2 gel (Group A), oxytocin (Group B), a Foley catheter (Group C), or a combination of the two (Group D). The following data was collected and statistically analysed: demographics, labour duration, delivery mode, maternal problems, and newborn outcomes. ANOVA and Chi-square tests were used. Result(s): The mean induction-to-delivery interval was shortest in Group D (Foley + Oxytocin) at 8.5 +/- 2.3 hours, followed by Group A (PGE2) at 10.2 +/- 2.8 hours, Group B (Oxytocin) at 12.7 +/- 3.1 hours, and Group C (Foley alone) at 13.4 +/- 3.6 hours. Vaginal delivery rates were highest in Group D (84%) and Group A (76%), while Group B and C had lower rates (64% and 60%, respectively). Apgar scores >=7 at 5 minutes were observed in over 90% of neonates across all groups, and NICU admission rates remained below 10%, with no significant differences.
