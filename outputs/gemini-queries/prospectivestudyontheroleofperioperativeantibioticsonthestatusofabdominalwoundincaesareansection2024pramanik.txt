"Introduction: The Caesarean Section is a surgical procedure to deliver a baby or babies by doing a laparotomy and entering into the gravid uterus to access the term fetus and remove it. The first such procedure was documented in 1020 AD. The procedure has been classically preferred when there are any problems during delivery on either the mother's side such as cardiac issues or non-progression of labour or on the baby's side such as shoulder dystocia, breech position. Aim(s): To study and analyze the role of perioperative prophylactic antibiotics on the status of abdominal wound in caesarean section. Also, to detect as far as possible the factors responsible for impaired abdominal wound healing after caesarean section Material(s) and Method(s): The present study was a Longitudinal Study. This Study was conducted from 1st April, 2021 to 31st September, 2022 at Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Burdwan Medical College and Hospital. Result(s): It is clear that the group of study participants who were not provided with antibiotic prophylaxis fared worse on all outcome parameters: 20% had contracted fever, 30% had endometritis and 24% had SSI occurrence, as compared to the group which experienced prophylaxis: 10% had fever, 8% endometritis, and 14% SSI occurrence. Conclusion(s): Perioperative antibiotics significantly reduce the rate of postoperative wound infections and improve wound healing in cesarean sections. The findings support the routine use of prophylactic antibiotics in cesarean deliveries to enhance surgical outcomes. Further research is warranted to refine protocols for antibiotic administration, including optimal timing and duration, to maximize patient benefit and minimize potential risks..Copyright (c) 2024 Institute of Advanced Studies.
