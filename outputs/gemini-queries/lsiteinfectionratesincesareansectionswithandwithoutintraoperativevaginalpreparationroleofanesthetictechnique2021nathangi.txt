"Background: Surgical site infections (SSIs) remain a common postoperative complication following cesarean sections. Intraoperative vaginal preparation and the choice of anesthetic technique may influence SSI incidence, but their combined effect is underexplored. Aim(s): To evaluate the impact of intraoperative vaginal preparation and anesthetic technique (spinal vs. general) on the rate of surgical site infections in women undergoing cesarean sections. Material(s) and Method(s): This prospective observational study included 80 pregnant women undergoing elective or emergency cesarean delivery at a tertiary care teaching hospital. Participants were divided into two equal groups (n = 40) based on whether intraoperative vaginal preparation with 10% povidone-iodine was performed. Each group was further stratified by anesthesia type-spinal or general. Intraoperative details, postoperative wound status, and SSI rates were assessed up to 30 days postoperatively. Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 21.0, with p< 0.05 considered significant. Result(s): Baseline demographic and intraoperative parameters were comparable between groups. The SSI rate was lower in the vaginal preparation group (5.0%) compared to the non-preparation group (17.5%) (p = 0.078). Spinal anesthesia was used in 72.5% of patients and was associated with significantly lower SSI incidence (5.2%) compared to general anesthesia (27.3%) (p = 0.011). Conclusion(s): Intraoperative vaginal preparation may reduce the risk of surgical site infections after cesarean section, though statistical significance was not achieved. Spinal anesthesia was associated with a significantly lower SSI rate than general anesthesia. These findings support the adoption of antiseptic vaginal preparation and spinal anesthesia as preferred practices in cesarean deliveries.Copyright (c)2021Int. J. LifeSci.Biotechnol.Pharma.Res."""
