"""abstract ID: complementproteinsannexinvandangiogenicmarkersinplacentaltissueofautoimmunediseasepatients2025castellanosgutierrez
"Introduction: Autoimmune diseases (AID) such as systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) or non-criteria-obstetric antiphospholipid syndrome (NC-OAPS) increase the risk of adverse perinatal outcomes (APO). During pregnancy the deregulation of the complement system and the angiogenic factors cause damage to the placenta, resulting in the development of APO. The aim is to evaluate the placental expression of complement proteins, Annexin V (ANNV) and angiogenic factors in patients diagnosed with AID and compare it with healthy controls and fetal growth restricted (FGR) cases. Materials and methods: Placental samples were classified into: AID with (13) and without (9) APO, FGR without AID (13), and healthy control group (13). Tissue sections were stained using antibodies against human complement proteins and ANNV. Results were expressed as mean-of-fluorescence-intensity (MFI) values. Total RNA was extracted from paraffin sections and mRNA expression of the angiogenesis-related factors was evaluated by qPCR and normalized using the 2-DCt method relative to GAPDH. Results: Lower expression of ANNV in AID + APO (p = 0.028), and AID (p = 0.034) compared to the control. Higher levels of C5b-9 (p = 0.0005 AID + APO and p < 0.0001 AID) C3b (p < 0.0001 both AID groups) and C4d (p < 0.0001 both AID groups and p = 0.0006 in FGR) were found compared to the control group. PlGF expression showed significant increase in the AID group without APO compared to the control group. Conclusion: Excessive complement activation and placental deposition while reduced ANNV is present in SLE, APS and NC-OAPS patients. Elevated PlGF placental expression is a feature of patients with AID without APO. (c) 2025 The Authors"""
