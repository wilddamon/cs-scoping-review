"Objective Infants in the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) are at high risk of adverse neuromotor outcomes. Atypical patterns of heart rate (HR) and pulse oximetry (SpO <inf>2</inf>) may serve as biomarkers for risk assessment for cerebral palsy (CP). The purpose of this study was to determine whether atypical HR and SpO <inf>2</inf>patterns in NICU patients add to clinical variables predicting later diagnosis of CP. Study Design This was a retrospective study including patients admitted to a level IV NICU from 2009 to 2017 with archived cardiorespiratory data in the first 7 days from birth to follow-up at >2 years of age. Results Seventy infants with CP and 1,733 controls met inclusion criteria for a 3.8% population prevalence. Conclusion In this study including NICU patients of all gestational ages, HR but not SpO <inf>2</inf>patterns added to clinical variables to predict the eventual diagnosis of CP.
