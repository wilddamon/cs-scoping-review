"Background: Oxytocin is the most effective uterotonic for treating and preventing postpartum hemorrhage (PPH). Tranexamic acid (TXA) and misoprostol, when administered in combination with oxytocin, may improve hemostasis and reduce blood loss. This research evaluated the benefits of adding intravenous TXA or intrauterine misoprostol to oxytocin in minimizing bleeding during cesarean section (CS) in high-risk women for postpartum bleeding. Patients and Methods: This prospective clinical research was done at Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital from October 2023 to June 2024, with 194 pregnant women at high risk for postpartum bleeding receiving CS. Women were split into two equal sets: Tranexamic acid (n = 97), who were given 1 gm of TXA intravenously, and misoprostol (n = 97), who received 400 microg of intrauterine misoprostol. Blood loss was measured using calculated estimated blood loss (cEBL) based on pre-and postoperative hematocrit levels. Secondary outcomes included PPH incidence, usage of extra uterotonics, and the requirement for a blood transfusion. Result(s): The misoprostol group had substantially lower cEBL than the TXA group (p = 0.007). Gravimetric blood loss measures favored the misoprostol group (p = 0.018) and exhibited a sufficient correlation (r = 0.871, p < 0.001). Both groups had no significant changes in PPH frequency (p = 0.446), further uterotonic usage, or transfusions. Antepartum hemorrhage and extended surgery were associated with higher cEBL. Conclusion(s): Intrauterine misoprostol with oxytocin during CS lowers intraoperative blood loss more efficiently than intravenous TXA in high-risk PPH patients, although both treatments are similarly successful in PPH prophylaxis.Copyright (c) The Author(s)."""
