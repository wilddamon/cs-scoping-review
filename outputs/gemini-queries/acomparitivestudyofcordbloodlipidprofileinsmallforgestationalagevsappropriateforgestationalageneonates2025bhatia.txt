"Background: This was a comparative study of cord blood lipid profile in small for gestational age vs appropriate for gestational age neonates. Material(s) and Method(s): A cross-sectional study was conducted in the NICU of Santosh Hospital on neonates born via normal vaginal delivery and caesarean section. Informed written consent was obtained from the parents or guardians of the neonates. A 5 ml sample of cord blood was collected from the umbilical cord immediately after delivery, specifically from the placental end. An infantometer was used to measure the length of the neonate. The neonate was placed on the infantometer with legs fully extended, and the movable foot end was adjusted to the soles of the feet to record the length. The weight of the neonate was recorded using an electronic weighing machine, without any clothing, immediately after birth. Head circumference was measured using a non-elastic measuring tape by determining the greatest occipitofrontal circumference. The data were initially checked for completeness, and data was cleaned for errors and missing values. The corrected data was then entered into Microsoft Excel after preparing a Master-chart. After data entry of every ten questionnaires, one random form was picked and data entry was re-checked. An independent person verified data entry of two randomly chosen forms after entry of every fifth questionnaire. Data analysis was done using licensed SPSS software version 24.0 (Chicago, Illinois). Result(s): Among SGA term neonates, 52.8% had a ponderal index <2, and 47.1% had 2-2.5. In contrast, 88.1% of AGA neonates had a ponderal index >2.5. No SGA neonates had index >2.5 as it is usually seen in only AGA or term neonates. In the preterm group, 55% of SGA neonates had a ponderal index <2, and none had >2.5. Among AGA neonates, 52.8% had a ponderal index >2.5, highlighting clear nutritional disparities. Cesarean section was more frequent in preterm neonates (39%) compared to term neonates (15.8%), and this difference was statistically significant (p = 0.00001). Level of HDL in term SGA neonates was between 75th-95th percentile (18.8%) as compared to the term AGA neonates accounting for 9.6%. Very few were below the 50th percentile. The difference was significant (p = 0.0001). Preterm neonates had significantly higher total cholesterol (237.32 +/- 59.68 vs. 210.41 +/- 77.67, p = 0.043), triglycerides (170.34 +/- 55.03 vs. 148.73 +/- 53.53, p = 0.00058), and LDL (270.47 +/- 93.59 vs. 232.53 +/- 111.96, p = 0.0053) than term neonates. HDL values were slightly lower in preterm neonates but the difference was not significant (p = 0.882). Conclusion(s): This study demonstrates that gestational age and intrauterine growth status substantially influence cord blood lipid profiles in neonates. Preterm birth is associated with a more atherogenic lipid profile, characterized by significantly elevated total cholesterol, triglycerides, and LDL levels compared to term birth. The relationship between growth status and lipid profiles is more complex and varies by gestational age and specific lipid fractions.Copyright (c)2025Int. J. Life Sci. Biotechnol. Pharma. Res."""
