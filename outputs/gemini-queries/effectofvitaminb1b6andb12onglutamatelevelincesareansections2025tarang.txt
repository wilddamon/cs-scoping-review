"BACKGROUND: Glutamate is a pain-stimulating neurotransmitter. New evidence shows the therapeutic potential of vitamin B1, B6, and B12 in different pain conditions. This study aims to determine the effect of vitamin B1, B6, and B12 on glutamate levels in patients undergoing cesarean section. METHOD(S): This was an experimental, randomized, double-blind, study on 26 patients undergoing caesarean section conducted at Batara Siang Pangkep Hospital, Pangkajene and Islands Regency, Indonesia from January to February 2024. Patients were divided into two groups: control and treatment. The control group receved no supplements, while the treatment group receved intravenous vitamin B1 100 mg, vitamin B6 100 mg, vitamin B12 5000 mcg in one hour before surgery. Blood sampling to measure glutamate levels was carried out before surgery, immediately after surgery, and 8 hours post-surgery. RESULT(S): The difference in median glutamate level 8 hours post-surgery in the control group (16.89) compared to the treatment group (10.31) was significant (P=0.003). Furthermore, changes in glutamate levels were also significantly different (P=0.001) between the control group (mean 12.52+/-8.45) and the treatment group (-0.01+/-7.93). CONCLUSION(S): Vitamin B1, B6, and B12 might play a role in reducing glutamate level 8 hours post-cesarean section.Copyright (c) 2024 EDIZIONI MINERVA MEDICA."""
