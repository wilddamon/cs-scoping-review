"Deeply engaged head in second stage C-section is not a novel occurrence; all obstetricians have probably seen it and related issues numerous times during their professional lives. Because the lower segment is thinned out and oedematous, caesarean sections performed at full cervical dilatation with impacted fetal heads are technically challenging and are linked to a higher risk of morbidities. The objective of present study was to study delivery in deeply engaged head in labor by Patwardhan technique and fetomaternal outcome. The present study was prospective study. Study was conducted from January 2021 to December 2023 in the Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Bankura Sammilani Medical College and Hospital, Bankura, West Bengal, India. Patients were divided into two groups: group -1 where baby delivered by Patwardhan technique and group 2 where baby delivered by push or pull technique. Total 240 women included in each group. Maximum mothers 156 (65%) in Patwardhan's technique and 154 (64.17%) mothers in control group belonged to age group of 20-30 years. Emergency cesarean section was fetal distress in late stage of labour (30% vs. 24.174%). Out of 240 cases in the Patwardhan's Technique, delivery of fetal head was difficult in 158 cases (65.83%) as compared to control group it was 98 cases (40.83%), p < 0.05. In study group, extension of uterine incision occurs in 54 (22.50%) cases as compared to 98 (40.83%) cases in control group. NICU admission was 74 (30.83%) vs 92 (38.33%). The APGAR scores of the babies at 1min and 5 min were not statistically significant. When delivering a profoundly impacted fetal head after a second-stage cesarean procedure, the Patwardhan's approach is far less likely to result in the uterine incision being extended than the push or pull method.Copyright (c) MAK HILL Publications."""
