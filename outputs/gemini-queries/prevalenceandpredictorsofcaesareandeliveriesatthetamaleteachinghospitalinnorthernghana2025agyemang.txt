While CS is often viewed as a life-saving intervention, it is associated with both immediate and long-term complications for the mother, newborn, and future pregnancies. To better understand the medical and non-medical reasons for CS among women, this study was conducted to identify the socio-demographic and obstetric factors that influence CS in the Tamale Metropolis. METHODOLOGY: A retrospective cross-sectional study was conducted among 318 postpartum mothers at the Tamale Teaching Hospital.
