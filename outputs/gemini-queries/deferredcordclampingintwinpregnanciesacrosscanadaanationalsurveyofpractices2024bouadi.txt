"Objective: Guidelines recommending deferred cord clamping (DCC), delaying cord clamping for at least 30 seconds post-birth, have shown significant benefits in preterm singleton births. However, evidence supporting DCC in twins is scarce due to limited trial data, leading to practice variations. This study aims to assess current reported DCC practices for twin pregnancies in tertiary hospitals across Canada. Methods: A web-based survey was distributed to neonatologists and obstetrician investigators associated with the Canadian Neonatal and Preterm Birth Networks operating maternity and neonatal units. Results: The site response rate was 93% (28/30 sites), with 83% (25/30) for neonatologists and 56% (17/30) for obstetricians. Neonatologists varied in their practices, with 32% placing the baby on the mother's abdomen, 32% on the mother's thighs, and 28% holding the baby at the height of the perineum. Divergent opinions were observed regarding contraindications, including risks of postpartum hemorrhage and velamentous cord insertion. Conclusions: DCC is reported to be practised in most twin deliveries among Canadian Neonatal and Preterm Birth Network centres.
