"Background: Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is the most common endocrinopathy of pregnancy. Adequate detection of GDM can prevent maternal and foetal adverse outcomes. In 2014 changes in GDM diagnostics and management in Poland were implemented. Aim(s): The comparison of frequency of gestational diabetes mellitus diagnosis, rates of foetal growth, birth mode and the condition of the newborn before and after the implementation of the new standards. Method(s): Study group included 626 diabetic parturients: Group A n = 107, diagnosed with previous criteria, and Group B n = 519, tested with the use of the new standards. We analysed frequency of GDM class occurrence (G1, treated with diet only and G2, treated with diet and insulin) maternal age and parity, gestational age at birth, mode of birth and the condition of the newborn. Result(s): In period I women with gestational diabetes mellitus accounted for 1,69% of all birth givers, while in period II the percentage was as high as 6,38%. In Group A we found class more prevalent, while most of women in Group B was classified as G1. Most women in Group B had caesarean section (60,89%), which were performed mostly due to maternal indications. Large for gestational age newborns were more frequent in Group B, while small for gestational age infants were more prevalent in Group A. Conclusion(s): The introduction of new GDM diagnostic criteria influenced the number of detected cases and classification of gestational diabetes mellitus, as well as pregnancy duration, birth mode, type of indications for caesarean section and neonatal birth weight.Copyright (c) 2020 Elsevier Ltd"""
