"Background: Adolescent pregnancy continues to be a significant public health issue in developing nations, particularly prevalent in rural and neglected areas. The style of delivery, especially cesarean sections compared to vaginal births, might substantially affect neonatal outcomes in this population. Despite the increase in cesarean deliveries among teenagers, their relative impact on infant health outcomes remains insufficiently examined. This study assesses and contrasts the initial newborn outcomes of cesarean vs vaginal deliveries among adolescent moms. Objective(s): To conduct a comparative analysis of neonatal outcomes following cesarean section versus vaginal delivery in adolescent pregnancies. Method(s): This retrospective hospital-based study was performed at Darbhanga Medical College and Hospital and Government Medical College, Bettiah, involving 96 adolescent parturients (aged 13-19 years) who delivered from September 2015 to August 2016. The cohort was categorized according to delivery method: Group A (Cesarean section) and Group B (Vaginal delivery). Evaluated newborn outcomes encompassed birth weight, Apgar score, NICU admissions, neonatal respiratory distress, and early neonatal mortality. Statistical analysis was conducted utilizing SPSS version 25.0. Result(s): Out of 96 deliveries, 42 (43.8%) were cesarean sections, whereas 54 (56.2%) were vaginal births. The prevalence of low birth weight (<2.5 kg) was markedly greater in the cesarean cohort (47.6%) than in the vaginal cohort (27.8%) (p < 0.05). Admissions to the NICU were more prevalent after cesarean delivery (38.1%) compared to vaginal birth (14.8%). The incidence of infant respiratory distress was elevated in the cesarean cohort (23.8%) relative to the vaginal cohort (7.4%). Early newborn mortality occurred in two cases, both of which were cesarean deliveries. Nonetheless, Apgar scores at 5 minutes exhibited no substantial disparity between the groups. Conclusion(s): Cesarean delivery in adolescent women correlates with elevated incidence of neonatal problems, notably low birth weight, respiratory distress, and heightened NICU admissions. Although cesarean delivery may be justified for obstetric reasons, a meticulous risk-benefit analysis is essential to enhance outcomes in this susceptible population.
