"Background: Meconium-stained amniotic fluid (MSAF) has a strong correlation with considerable newborn morbidity and death and is a crucial marker of foetal distress. Meconium aspiration syndrome (MAS), respiratory distress, and other serious newborn disorders can result from meconium in the amniotic fluid. The study aims to evaluate the incidence of MSAF and its impact on neonatal morbidity and mortality in a cohort of deliveries. Method(s): A total of 850 pregnant women were included, with 100 presenting with MSAF. Data were collected on maternal demographics, obstetric history, delivery details, and neonatal outcomes. SPSS version 23.0 was used for the statistical analysis, with a significance level of p<0.05. Result(s): The incidence of MSAF was found to be 11.76%. Neonates born with MSAF had significantly lower Apgar scores at 1 and 5 minutes, higher NICU admission rates (40% vs. 8%), and increased neonatal sepsis (15% vs. 2.7%). The mortality rate in the MSAF group was significantly higher at 8% compared to 1.3% in the non-MSAF group. Detailed morbidity analysis revealed higher rates of respiratory distress, MAS, hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, and seizures in the MSAF group. Conclusion(s): Increased morbidity and mortality as well as unfavourable newborn outcomes are strongly linked to MSAF. These results emphasise the importance of close observation and careful handling of pregnancies complicated with MSAF.Copyright (c) 2025 Dr. Yashwant Research Labs Pvt. Ltd..
