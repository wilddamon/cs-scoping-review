"Background: Spinal anesthesia for cesarean sections often leads to hypotension, posing risks to maternal and fetal health. Serotonin receptor antagonists, such as ondansetron and granisetron, have shown potential in mitigating this complication. Objective(s): This study compares the efficacy of intravenous ondansetron (8 mg) versus granisetron (3 mg) in preventing hypotension during subarachnoid block for elective lower segment cesarean section (LSCS). Method(s): In a double-blinded, randomized controlled trial, 120 parturients (ASA I-II, aged 20-40 years) were assigned to three groups (n=40 each): ondansetron (8 mg IV), granisetron (3 mg IV), or placebo (10 ml saline). Administered five minutes before spinal anesthesia, hemodynamic parameters, vasopressor use, and neonatal outcomes were recorded. Result(s): Both ondansetron and granisetron significantly reduced ephedrine requirements compared to placebo (p<0.001). Granisetron showed a lower incidence of hypotension (p<0.001) compared to ondansetron and placebo. No significant differences in neonatal Apgar scores were observed. Conclusion(s): Granisetron (3 mg IV) is more effective than ondansetron (8 mg IV) in preventing spinal anesthesia-induced hypotension during LSCS, with comparable safety for maternal and neonatal outcomes.Copyright (c) 2025 Dr. Yashwant Research Labs Pvt.
