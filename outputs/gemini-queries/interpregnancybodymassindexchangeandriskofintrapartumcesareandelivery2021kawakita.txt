Interpregnancy BMI change was calculated by the change of early pregnancy BMI measured in the office. Multivariable logistic regression was performed to calculate adjusted odds ratio (aOR) with 95% confidence interval (CI) after adjusting for predefined covariates. RESULTS: Of 2,168 women who were analyzed, 258 (12%), 1,192 (55%), and 718 (33%) had interpregnancy BMI loss more than 2 kg/m(2), BMI change +- 2 kg/m(2), and BMI gain more than 2 kg/m(2), respectively. Women with BMI gain more than 2 kg/m(2) compared with those with BMI change +- 2 kg/m(2) had increased odds of intrapartum cesarean delivery (7.4 vs. 4.5%; aOR: 1.78; 95% CI: 1.10-2.86) and cesarean delivery for arrest disorders (3.1 vs. 1.1%; aOR: 3.06; 95% CI: 1.30-7.15).
