"How the model of maternity care impacted women's experience and perinatal outcomes during the COVID-19 pandemic is not well known. There is some evidence that birth at home and midwifery continuity of care models were viewed positively by women giving birth during the pandemic, but it is less certain as to whether birth outcomes were impacted. Research has shown the model of maternity care can impact on perinatal outcomes and women's experiences. In Australia, there are around 10 different models of care that fall into 5 categories: standard care (SC) which is public and fragmented in nature; general practitioner (GP) public hospital shared care; midwifery continuity of care (MWCOC) in the public system; private obstetric (PO) care in the private system and private practising midwifery (PPM) care 1. The aim of this study is to examine the impact of the five main models of maternity care on the labour, birth and postpartum outcomes of women who gave birth in Australia during the COVID-19 pandemic The Birth in the Time of COVID (BITTOC) project is a mixed-method, longitudinal study investigating the pandemic-related experiences and mental health of women who were pregnant or have given birth during the pandemic. Using nationally distributed surveys the BITTOC study was conducted in Australia from March to December 2020 (BITTOC 2020), and again from August 2021 to March 2022 (BITTOC 2021). A total of 6154 women responded to the surveys and of these 3725 women were postnatal and could provide information on birth outcomes and therefore were included in this study. Sample weighting was used, and a multivariable model with three series of logistic regression models was performed. The first stage (model 1) adjusted for the survey year, model of care and demographic factors. The second stage (model 2) included model 1, plus obstetric factors. The third stage (model 3) included model 2 and mental health risk factors. This type of structured logistic regression model allowed us to examine birth outcomes depending on the model of care received by women during and after childbirth. Compared with standard fragmented care (SC), continuity care with a midwife employed in public health (MWCOC) and under a privately practising midwife (PPM) led to higher: spontaneous labour, spontaneous vaginal birth, postnatal visits at home by a midwife, feeling supported postnatally, feeling the care provider showed commitment and feeling the care provider knew them. MWCOC and PPM care led to lower: induction, elective and emergency caesarean births, augmentation with oxytocin, fetal distress, admission of a baby to SCN/NICU, Jaundice (only for PPMs), baby being given formula when chosen to breastfeed (only for PPMs), rates of birth trauma. Compared with SC, care under a private obstetrician (PO) led to higher: elective caesarean section and feeling the care provider showed commitment and feeling the care provider knew them. Care under a PO led to lower: spontaneous labour, spontaneous vaginal birth, postpartum haemorrhage, baby going to SCN/NICU, midwife visiting at home postnatally and birth trauma. Compared to SC, the best perinatal outcomes for women who gave birth during the COVID-19 pandemic were achieved for women accessing midwifery models of care, particularly when cared for by PPMs. Perinatal outcomes for GP-shared care differed little from SC."""
