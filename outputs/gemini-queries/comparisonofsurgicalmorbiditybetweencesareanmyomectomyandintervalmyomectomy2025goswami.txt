"Background: Uterine fibroids are the most common benign uterine tumors in women of reproductive age, often complicating pregnancy and delivery. Myomectomy remains the standard uterus-preserving treatment, usually performed as an interval procedure outside pregnancy. However, cesarean myomectomy has emerged as a practical alternative to avoid repeat surgery and hospitalization, though its safety remains debated due to concerns about hemorrhage and perioperative morbidity. Aim(s): The present study was conducted to compare surgical morbidity between cesarean myomectomy and interval myomectomy in women with symptomatic uterine fibroids at a tertiary care hospital. Material(s) and Method(s): This comparative observational study included 70 patients who met the inclusion criteria, divided equally into two groups. Group A consisted of 35 women who underwent cesarean myomectomy, while Group B included 35 women who underwent interval myomectomy. Preoperative parameters such as age, body mass index, parity, fibroid size, and hemoglobin levels were documented. Intraoperative outcomes included operative duration, estimated blood loss, need for transfusion, and complications. Postoperative outcomes assessed were hemoglobin drop, analgesic requirement, hospital stay, wound infection, febrile morbidity, paralytic ileus, secondary hemorrhage, and readmission within 30 days. Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26.0, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Result(s): Both groups were comparable in baseline characteristics. The cesarean myomectomy group had significantly longer operative time (92.54 +/- 14.26 vs. 85.12 +/- 13.18 minutes, p = 0.031) and higher blood loss (480.65 +/- 95.42 vs. 410.35 +/- 88.28 mL, p = 0.004). Postoperative hemoglobin drop was greater in Group A (1.28 +/- 0.45 vs. 0.92 +/- 0.40 g/dL, p = 0.002), and mean hospital stay was longer (4.68 +/- 1.05 vs. 3.42 +/- 0.88 days, p < 0.001). Other parameters, including blood transfusion requirement, wound infection, febrile morbidity, ileus, and readmission, showed no significant differences. Regression analysis identified type of surgery, blood loss, and operative duration as independent predictors of surgical morbidity. Conclusion(s): Cesarean myomectomy is associated with greater operative time, blood loss, and longer hospital stay compared to interval myomectomy, but without a significant increase in major complications.
