"Background: Candidemia, a serious bloodstream infection caused by Candida species, is a growing concern in neonatal intensive care units (NICUs) and sick newborn care units (SNCUs) worldwide. Neonates, particularly those with low birth weight and prematurity, are at high risk due to their underdeveloped immune systems and frequent exposure to invasive medical procedures. Aim(s): To assess the incidence, risk factors, and outcomes of candidemia in neonates. Method(s): A prospective observational study was conducted, including 150 neonates. Blood cultures were obtained to identify candidemia, and relevant clinical data were collected. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 23.0 to identify significant risk factors and outcomes associated with candidemia. Result(s): The incidence of candidemia was 14.7%, with Candida albicans being the most commonly isolated species (50%). Significant risk factors identified included low birth weight (OR: 4.25, p=0.001), central venous catheter use (OR: 3.76, p=0.002), and broad-spectrum antibiotic use (OR: 2.88, p=0.015). Candidemia was associated with a higher mortality rate (45.5%) and longer hospital stays (mean: 28.5 days) compared to neonates without candidemia. Conclusion(s): Candidemia remains a significant cause of morbidity and mortality among neonates in intensive care. Low birth weight, central venous catheter use, and broad-spectrum antibiotic use are key independent risk factors. Early diagnosis and targeted interventions are crucial to improving outcomes in this vulnerable population. Recommendations: Healthcare providers should focus on minimizing the use of invasive devices and broad-spectrum antibiotics in neonates, and implement early screening protocols for those at high risk of candidemia.
