"Background: Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR) is a significant pregnancy complication associated with increased risks of perinatal morbidity and Mortality. Understanding maternal, fetal, and neonatal outcomes in FGR pregnancies is crucial for optimizing clinical management. Objective(s): This study aims to compare the outcomes of pregnancies complicated by FGR and to identify key factors influencing these outcomes. Methodology: A retrospective observational study was conducted at the Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Nalanda Medical College and Hospital, Patna for a period of Sep 2023 to Oct 2024. 150 singleton pregnancies diagnosed with FGR between 28 and 40 weeks of gestation were analyzed. Data were collected on maternal factors (age, parity, comorbidities), fetal parameters (birth weight, Apgar scores, Doppler findings), and neonatal outcomes (NICU admission, morbidity, Mortality). Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS, with significance at p < 0.05. Result(s): Hypertensive disorders, particularly preeclampsia, were observed in a significant proportion of mothers. Cesarean section rates were notably high, reflecting the need for timely delivery in FGR cases. Fetal outcomes showed a predominance of low and very low birth weights, with abnormal Doppler findings in 50% of cases. Neonatal outcomes included high rates of NICU admission due to respiratory distress syndrome, sepsis, and hypoglycemia, with a neonatal mortality rate of 9.3%. Comparative analysis revealed that the severity of FGR and earlier gestational age at delivery were associated with poorer outcomes. Conclusion(s): This study underscores the substantial burden of FGR on maternal, fetal, and neonatal health. Early detection through rigorous antenatal screening, timely interventions, and robust neonatal care are critical to improving outcomes. The findings highlight the need for individualized management strategies and the importance of enhancing antenatal and neonatal care facilities.
