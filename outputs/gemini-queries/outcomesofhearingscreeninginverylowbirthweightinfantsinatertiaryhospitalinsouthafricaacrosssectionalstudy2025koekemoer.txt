"Background. In resource-constrained settings, targeted hearing screening is a feasible short-term option to identify infants with hearing loss. The risk factors which guide targeted hearing screening are based on data collected in high-income countries, with very little data available from low-and middle-income countries. Many of these risk factors are associated with very-low-birthweight (VLBW). Similarly, VLBW infants are more likely to experience complications of prematurity. Objectives. To identify associations between the complications of prematurity and failing hearing screening. Methods. We conducted a retrospective record review of 508 infants admitted to Tygerberg Hospital during 2022 who underwent hearing screening. Univariate and multivariate logistical regression was used to demonstrate an association with failing the hearing screen. The proportion of hearing screening failures, as well as the frequency of complications of prematurity were also determined. Results. A total of 508 VLBW were enrolled in the study. The proportion of hearing screening failures was 7% (95% CI 5-9). Factors with a statistically significant association with failing the hearing screen included being extremely low-birthweight (ELBW) (p=0.02) and exposure to gentamicin (p=0.04). Conclusion. We found a significant association between failing the hearing screening, birthweight and exposure to gentamicin.
