"Background: Inspite of various programs implemented, Anaemia remains the most common morbidity affecting pregnant women, along with its complications. Objective(s): To estimate the maternal and perinatal outcomes in pregnant women with anemia. Method(s): A Hospital based cohort study on pregnant women attending Sri Balaji Medical College Hospital and Research Institute, Tirupati, India between Jan 2023 and Jan 2024. Women were diagnosed with anemia based on two criteria: third-trimester hemoglobin value or a delivery admission diagnosis of anemia (made before delivery). Anemia was categorized into no anemia (hemoglobin 11 g/dL or greater), mild (9-10.9 g/dL), moderate (7-8.9 g/dL), severe (less than 7 g/dL), or anemia of unspecified severity (with diagnosis made before delivery). Logistic regression was used to estimate adjusted odds ratios (aOR) and 95% CIs expressing the association between anemia and maternal and perinatal outcomes. Result(s): Of 108 women in the study population, 77(71.3%),22(20.37%), and 9(8.33%) had mild, moderate, and severe anemia, respectively, and 0.53% had anemia of unspecified severity. Anemic women had longer hospitalization duration and more antenatal admissions, and rates of preeclampsia, placenta previa and cesarean delivery were higher among women with anemia. Anemia was associated with preterm birth (mild anemia, aOR 1.09, 95% CI 1.05-1.12; moderate anemia, aOR 2.26, 95% CI 2.02-2.54; anemia of unspecified severity, aOR 2.27, 95% CI 2.06-2.50), small-for-gestational-age live birth, low 5-minute Apgar score, neonatal death, and perinatal death. Conclusion(s): Maternal anemia in pregnancy represents a common and potentially reversible risk factor associated with antepartum, intrapartum, and postpartum maternal morbidity and perinatal morbidity and mortality.Copyright (c)2025Int.
