"Background: Membrane sweeping is a mechanical method used to stimulate labor in a term pregnancy. It reduces the need for formal induction. Its efficacy and safety are a matter of debate, particularly for balancing maternal and neonatal outcomes along with patient comfort. The current study was designed to evaluate the efficacy of membrane sweeping at term pregnancy, comparing it with expectant management in terms of pregnancy duration, labor outcomes, and maternal tolerability. Method(s): This prospective study was done on 50 term pregnant women. They were randomly allocated equally (n=25) to two groups: the membrane sweep group and the expectant management group. The baseline characteristics, including labor onset within seven days, induction rates, delivery outcomes, maternal and neonatal parameters apart from the procedure-related tolerability, were recorded and analyzed by application of suitable statistical methods. Result(s):The results of the study showed that 76% of cases of the sweep group versus 44% of the expectant management group had spontaneous labor in 7 days (p=0.03). Labor induction was necessary in 24% and 56% respectively (p =0.03). The median time between randomization and delivery was found to be significantly lower in the sweep group (4 vs. 7 days, p<0.01). There were no differences in mode of delivery and neonatal outcomes, such as Apgar scores and NICU admissions. The discomfort and spotting were common with the procedures, and there were no serious complications in either group. Conclusion(s): Membrane sweeping appears to be an effective, safe, and relatively well-tolerated procedure for initiating labor at term. It decreases the pregnancy duration and reduces the need for formal induction without any adverse maternal or neonatal outcomes. Therefore, because of its simplicity and cost-effectiveness, it can be routinely considered in clinical practice for women with a term pregnancy.Copyright (c)2020Int. J. Life Sci. Biotechnol. Pharma.
