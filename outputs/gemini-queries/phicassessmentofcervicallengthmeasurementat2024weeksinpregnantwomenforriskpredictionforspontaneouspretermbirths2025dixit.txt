"Introduction: Preterm birth (PTB), defined as delivery before 37 weeks of gestation, is a major cause of neonatal morbidity and mortality worldwide. Early identification of women at risk is crucial for timely intervention and improved outcomes. Risk factors for PTB include a history of preterm delivery, uterine anomalies, infections, and cervical insufficiency. Among these, cervical length measurement by transvaginal ultrasonography (TVUS) at 20-24 weeks of gestation is a reliable, noninvasive predictor of spontaneous PTB. Studies show that a shorter cervical length significantly increases the risk of early delivery, making TVUS an essential tool in obstetric care. Objective(s): To assess the effectiveness of ultrasonographic cervical length measurement at 20-24 weeks of gestation in predicting the risk of spontaneous PTB and to determine the correlation between cervical length and gestational age at delivery. Method(s): This cross-sectional study was conducted at the Department of Radiodiagnosis, Rajshree Medical Research Institute, Bareilly, Uttar Pradesh. Cervical length was measured using TVUS, and patients were followed until delivery to record gestational age at birth. Data were analyzed using STATA statistical software. Result(s): The mean cervical length was 3.68 cm. Women who delivered preterm (<37 weeks) had a significantly shorter mean cervical length (2.1 cm) compared to those who delivered at term (>=37 weeks, 3.76 cm) (p<0.05). A negative correlation (r = -0.78) was observed between cervical length and gestational age at delivery, indicating that shorter cervical length increases the risk of PTB. Conclusion(s): TVUS cervical length measurement at 20-24 weeks is an effective predictor of PTB.
