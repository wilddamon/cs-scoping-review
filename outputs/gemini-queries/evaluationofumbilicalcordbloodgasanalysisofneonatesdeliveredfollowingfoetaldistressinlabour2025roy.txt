"""abstract ID: evaluationofumbilicalcordbloodgasanalysisofneonatesdeliveredfollowingfoetaldistressinlabour2025roy
"Background: Perinatal hypoxia-ischaemia remains a leading cause of neonatal morbidity and mortality. Umbilical cord arterial blood gas (UABG) analysis provides objective evidence of intrapartum hypoxia and complements clinical tools (Apgar score, cardiotocography [CTG]) that have limited specificity. We evaluated the prevalence and correlates of abnormal UABG in neonates delivered after a diagnosis of foetal distress, and its association with immediate neonatal outcomes. Method(s): We conducted an institutional cross-sectional observational study at Calcutta National Medical College & Hospital, Kolkata, over 16 months (June 2020-September 2021). Consecutive singleton pregnancies >=32 weeks with intrapartum foetal distress (clinical and/or CTG) were included; major maternal comorbidities, multiple gestation, fetal anomalies and intrauterine foetal demise were excluded. UABG was sampled immediately after delivery from a clamped cord segment and analysed within 30 minutes. Outcomes included Apgar scores, need for positive-pressure ventilation (PPV), NICU admission and early neonatal death. Result(s): Among 240 mother-infant dyads, mean (+/-SD) UABG pH was 7.24 +/- 0.069; mean pO2 18.17 +/- 5.07 mmHg; pCO2 54.45 +/- 5.38 mmHg; base excess -4.92 +/- 1.33 mmol/L. UABG pH was 7.1-7.3 in 73.3%, > 7.3 in 20.0%, and < 7.1 (severe acidaemia) in 6.7%. Meconium-stained liquor (36.6%) and CTG abnormalities (25.0%) were the commonest triggers for intervention; 86.7% delivered by caesarean section. Overall, 30.0% required PPV; 56 neonates (23.3%) required NICU admission. All severe acidaemia cases clustered in cord prolapse and placental abruption, with high early mortality (12/16; 75%). No infant with pH > 7.3 required NICUcare. Clinical diagnostic modalities (meconium, foetal bradycardia/tachycardia) showed positive association with abnormal pH (< 7.3), whereas CTG abnormality alone did not. Conclusion(s): Intrapartum foetal distress is frequently accompanied by mild umbilical acidaemia; severe acidaemia is uncommon but strongly predicts adverse early outcomes and is concentrated in sentinel events (abruption, cord prolapse). Routine UABG in high-risk deliveries refines risk stratification beyond CTG and Apgar, guides immediate care, and may curb unnecessary caesarean sections when CTG is the sole trigger.Copyright (c)2025Int. J. Life Sci. Biotechnol. Pharma. Res."""
