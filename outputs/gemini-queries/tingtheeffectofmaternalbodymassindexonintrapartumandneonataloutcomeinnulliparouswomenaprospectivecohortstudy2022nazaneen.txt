"Background: Maternal obesity has emerged as a major public health concern worldwide, influencing pregnancy, labour, and neonatal outcomes. The impact of increasing maternal body mass index (BMI) on intrapartum and neonatal outcomes remains a critical area for investigation, particularly in nulliparous women. Aim(s): To evaluate the effect of maternal BMI on intrapartum and neonatal outcomes in nulliparous women with singleton term pregnancies. Material(s) and Method(s): This prospective cohort study was conducted in the Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology at a tertiary care teaching hospital. A total of 160 nulliparous women were enrolled and classified into three BMI categories: normal weight (18.5-24.9 kg/m2), overweight (25.0-29.9 kg/m2), and obese (>=30.0 kg/m2). Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 21.0 with significance set at p < 0.05. Result(s): The incidence of caesarean section was significantly higher in obese women (37.5%) compared to normal weight women (15.4%) (p = 0.004). Induction of labour increased with BMI (50.0% in obese vs. 23.1% in normal weight; p = 0.002). Obese women experienced prolonged labour (8.2 +/- 1.9 hours; p = 0.012) and higher wound infection rates (12.5%; p = 0.007). Neonatal birth weight was significantly higher in the obese group (3350 +/- 375 g; p = 0.003), although Apgar scores and NICU admissions did not differ significantly among groups. Conclusion(s): Increasing maternal BMI in nulliparous women is associated with higher risks of labour induction, caesarean delivery, prolonged labour, neonatal macrosomia, and maternal wound infections.
