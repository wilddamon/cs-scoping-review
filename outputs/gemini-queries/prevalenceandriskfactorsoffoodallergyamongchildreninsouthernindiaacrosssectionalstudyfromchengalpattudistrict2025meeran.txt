"Food allergy is an increasing health concern among children, particularly in developing regions such as India, where reliable data remain limited. This cross-sectional study aimed to estimate the prevalence of food allergy and identify associated risk factors among children aged 5 to 15 years attending a tertiary care hospital in Chengalpattu district, Tamil Nadu. A total of 180 children were enrolled using consecutive sampling. Data were collected through a pre-tested semi-structured questionnaire assessing sociodemographic details, clinical history, and early-life exposures. The prevalence of food allergy was found to be 15.6% (28 out of 180 children; 95% CI: 11.0-21.6%). Among affected children, common comorbidities included allergic rhinitis (23.3%), asthma (17.2%), and eczema (13.3%). A family history of allergy was reported in 25% of the total sample. Most children resided in urban areas (60%), and the mean age was 10.2 years. In bivariate analysis, food allergy was more frequent in children with eczema, asthma, and allergic rhinitis. A higher prevalence was also observed in those delivered via cesarean section and those with shorter breastfeeding duration, although not all associations reached statistical significance. Multivariable logistic regression confirmed that eczema, allergic rhinitis, asthma and paternal unemployment were significantly associated with increased odds of food allergy, while male gender and older age (12-15 years) were linked to reduced risk. These findings highlight a considerable burden of food allergy in the region and underscore the need for integrated allergy screening and early risk assessment in paediatric care.
