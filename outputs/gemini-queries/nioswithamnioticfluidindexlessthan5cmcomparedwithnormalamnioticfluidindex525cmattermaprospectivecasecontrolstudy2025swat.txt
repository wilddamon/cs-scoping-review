"Background and Objectives: Amniotic fluid is crucial to the foetus's growth and proper development of the foetal musculoskeletal system and long bones. Volume of amniotic fluid gradually increases as gestation progresses until about weeks 31-33, after which it significantly decreases until the anticipated date of confinement. Oligohydramnios, which is characterised by amniotic fluid index of less than 5 centimetres or less is found to be significantly linked to a number of concerning outcomes of pregnancy including perinatal mortality, foetal distress during labor, and impaired infant health. The present study was conducted at a tertiary acre level teaching hospital to assess the association of perinatal outcome in cases with idiopathic oligohydramnios with AFI<5cm diagnosed at term and comparison of results with perinatal outcome of controls with normal AFI (5-25 cm). Method(s): This prospective Case control study was carried out in the Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Narayan Medical College and Hospital, Jamuhar, Bihar, India from 2022 to 2024 on 200 pregnant women of 37 weeks to 42 weeks of gestation, They were divided into 2 groups: "cases' group comprising 100 patients with AFI<5cm and another group with 100 patients (Control group) with AFI (5-25cm). Result(s): The two groups didn't differ significantly in terms of age, systolic or diastolic BP or period of gestation. Majority of the patients belonged to 18-20 years age group with 52% and 55% in cases and control respectively. 41% women among the cases had unfavourable cervix with Bishop's score <=6 as compared to 13% in control group (P<0.001). There was a significantly higher rate of labour induction in the cases (46%) than controls (14%). Overall, 31% patients out of 200 patients had meconium-stained liquor which was significantly higher in study group (44%) than control group (18%, p value =0.01). The incidence of abnormal CTG was significantly higher in cases (50%) than in control group (11%). The incidence of requirement of emergency caesarean section was also significantly higher in cases (61%) as compared to controls (28%). Incidence of LBW birth was also significantly higher in cases (64%) as compared to controls (31%). It was found that 74% of babies born with AFI <5 (cases) had Apgar score of <7 at 1 minute whereas in control, 56% of the patients had Apgar score of <7. Similarly, 25% of babies born with AFI <5 (Cases) had Apgar score of <7 at 5 minute whereas, in control only 7% had Apgar score of <7. Not surprisingly, 37% babies born to cases needed admission in NICU as comparison to control in which only 9% babies needed admission. Conclusion(s): AFI is an important and convenient screening test for prediction of perinatal outcome. Isolated oligohydramnios in the absence of any other maternal or foetal complicating factor is found to increase the incidence of operative intervention and adversely affects the foetal outcome.Copyright (c)2025Int.
