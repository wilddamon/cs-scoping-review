"Background: Spinal-induced hypotension (SIH) is a common complication during caesarean sections under spinal anaesthesia, often leading to maternal discomfort and compromised fetal perfusion. Ondansetron, a 5-HT3 receptor antagonist, has shown potential in mitigating SIH. Aim(s): To evaluate the effectiveness of prophylactic ondansetron in reducing the incidence and severity of SIH in parturients undergoing caesarean section. Material(s) and Method(s): This prospective, randomized, double-blind study included 60 parturients undergoing elective caesarean section at IGIMS, Patna, over 12 months. Patients were randomized into two groups: Group O (8 mg ondansetron IV) and Group C (placebo). Incidence of hypotension, vasopressor use, and maternal side effects were recorded. Result(s): The incidence of hypotension was significantly lower in Group O (26.7%) compared to Group C (63.3%) (p < 0.01). Vasopressor requirement and maternal nausea/vomiting were also significantly reduced in Group O.
