"Spinal anesthesia is considered as common practice in modern medicine. It has various advantages as decreases blood loss, early post-operative recovery and infrequent nausea and vomiting. The purpose of this study was to compare the clinical and anesthetic effects of bupivacaine and lidocaine when administered intra-thecally to patients undergoing spinal anesthesia surgery.A cross-sectional study in Sulaymaniyah city was carried out Shar General Hospital, Maternity Teaching Hospital, Shorsh Hospital and Smart Tower Hospital., from 1st September 2021 to 2nd April 2022. The patients separated intobupivacaine and lidocaine group. The bupivacaine group provided significantly longer durations of both motor and sensory blocks than the lidocaine group.T9 and T8 were the highest levels of sensory block reached in both groups, respectively. Bupivacaine group experienced more severe motor block than lidocaine group. Patients in the lidocaine group had least post-operative side effects in comparison to the first group. Recovery time was longer in bupivacaine, however, it was significantly shorter in the other group.Our findings revealed that the block time was shorter in the lidocaine group, which may be an issue for longer operative procedures. However, with proper patient selection, this can be avoided. Lidocaine was more eligible regarding the post operative side effects in spinal anesthesia. The recovery time was earlier in the lidocaine group. Thus, a postoperative neurological examination can be performed sooner. Furthermore, early mobilization can help with post-operative recovery.Copyright (c) 2022, Anka Publishers. All rights reserved."""
