If not feasible at the place of arrest, victims must be transported to a suitable room to perform ECMO while effective, sustained resuscitation maneuvers are performed. Methods: A total of 26 nursing students participated in a randomized crossover study. In pairs, the rescuers performed two 2 min tests, consisting of five rescue breaths followed by cycles of 15 compressions and two breaths. The analysis focused on CPR variables (chest compression and ventilation), CPR quality, the rate of perceived exertion and the distance covered. Results: No differences were observed in the chest compression quality variable (82 +- 10% versus 84 +- 11%, p = 0.15). Therefore, the overall CPR quality was worse in the stretcher test (50 +- 9%) than in the control test (56 +- 13%) (p = 0.025). Conclusions: Infant CPR performed by nursing students while walking alongside a moving stretcher is possible.
