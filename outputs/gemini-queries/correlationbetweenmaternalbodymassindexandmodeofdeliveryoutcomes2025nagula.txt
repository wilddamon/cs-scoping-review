"Background: Maternal obesity has emerged as a significant obstetric challenge worldwide, potentially influencing various pregnancy outcomes including mode of delivery. Method(s): This prospective cohort study included 524 pregnant women who delivered singleton term infants at a tertiary care hospital over 18 months. Participants were classified into four BMI categories based on pre-pregnancy BMI: underweight (<18.5 kg/m2), normal weight (18.5-24.9 kg/m2), overweight (25-29.9 kg/m2), and obese (>=30 kg/m2). Emergency cesarean section rates were significantly higher in obese women (45.1%) versus normal-weight women (21.3%, p<0.001). Spontaneous vaginal delivery rates decreased from 59.7% in normal-weight to 28.7% in obese women (p<0.001). Instrumental delivery rates did not differ significantly across BMI categories (p=0.184).
