"Exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) is essential for the health of infants and mothers. Despite this, EBF failure is a common problem all over the world. Objective. To determine the causes of EBF failure in a sample from a primary healthcare center (PHS) in Baghdad city, Iraq. Patients and methods. A cross-sectional study was conducted at Sulayman Al-Faydhi PHC in Hay Albenouk district (Baghdad, Iraq) between August 1 and October 1, 2019. It included 222 mothers who failed to achieve EBF and their healthy infants under 6 months of age. All of these mothers had previously visited antenatal care providers. Results. Out of the total number of cases, 118 (53.3%) children were male, 73 (32.9%) children were first born, and most of them (170 (76.6%)) were delivered by cesarean section. Regarding the age of mothers, 68 (30.6%) of them were between 25-29 years old, and the level of education was secondary in 102 (45.9%). Most of these mothers (165 (74.3%)) failed to exclusively breastfeed their children during the first month after delivery, and 211 (95%) of them had no or poor knowledge about EBF. They mentioned many causes of EBF failure and the most common was low milk supply in 92 (41.4%). The second major cause was the prescription of formula milk in 65 (29.3%). Only 10 (4.5%) mothers mentioned child-related reasons such as prematurity and hospitalization. Conclusion. Beliefs about low milk supply and formula milk prescription were the main causes of EBF failure. This study showed that most mothers have little or no knowledge about EBF.
