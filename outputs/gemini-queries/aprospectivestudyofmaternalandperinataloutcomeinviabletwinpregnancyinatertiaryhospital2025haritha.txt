"Background: Multiple pregnancy, particularly twin gestation, is associated with increased maternal and perinatal complications compared to singleton pregnancies. With the growing use of assisted reproductive techniques (ART) and delayed maternal age, the incidence of twin pregnancies has risen globally. This study aimed to evaluate and compare the maternal and neonatal outcomes of twin and singleton pregnancies. Method(s): A prospective comparative study was conducted from July 2013 to July 2016 at a tertiary care hospital. All viable twin pregnancies beyond 24 weeks of gestation were included as the study group, and singleton pregnancies as controls. Data on maternal demographics, conception method, complications, mode of delivery, and neonatal outcomes were collected and analyzed using t-tests. Result(s): Out of 215 total participants, 100 were twin and 115 were singleton pregnancies. Spontaneous conception accounted for 58% of twin gestations, while 42% followed ART. Twin pregnancies showed significantly higher rates of preterm labour (61% vs 12%), PPROM (33% vs 7%), hypertensive disorders (15% vs 5%), and gestational diabetes (21% vs 11%). Cesarean section rates were higher in twins (54% vs 38%) mainly due to malpresentation (30.5% vs 8%). Neonatal complications were notably increased among twins, including NICU admissions (67.5% vs 14%), respiratory distress syndrome (43.5% vs 6%), and low birth weight (<2500 g in 55.5% vs 25%). Prematurity and growth restriction were the leading causes of morbidity. Conclusion(s): Twin pregnancies carry a significantly higher risk of maternal, obstetric, and neonatal complications compared to singletons, largely due to prematurity and low birth weight. Vigilant antenatal surveillance, timely intervention, and multidisciplinary management are essential to improve outcomes.
