"Background: Pain is usually a common symptom of any disorder especially after surgery, in which pain management is a critical component of health care quality. Objective: The aim of this study was to assess post-operative pain for women underwent caesarean section in the Shifa Medical Complex (Gyn/Obst wards). Methods: This was a hospital-based cross-sectional study. Two hundred seven women who had undergone caesarean delivery were included in the study and interviewed on their first postoperative day using the American Pain Society/Patient Outcome Questionnaire. Results: Response rate was 86.2%. Mean age was 28.1 +-6.5 years old. One hundred nine (46.1%) reported severe pain on Visual Analog Scale (VAS >= 8). Mean pain intensity in the first 24 hours after surgery was 7+-2 out of 10 on the VAS. Women reported moderate interference of pain with activity and sleep pattern (5.88+-2.85 and 5.78+-2.91 respectively out of 10 on the VAS). Women (78.2%) did not involve in the decision of pain management, however they reported satisfaction. Conclusion: Post-operative pain control was sub-optimal, however the patients reported satisfaction.
