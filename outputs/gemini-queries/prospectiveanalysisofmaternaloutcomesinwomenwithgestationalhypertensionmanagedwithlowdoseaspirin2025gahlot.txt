"Background: Gestational hypertension is one of the most prevalent hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, contributing significantly to maternal and perinatal morbidity. Endothelial dysfunction and abnormal placentation are key mechanisms leading to elevated blood pressure and related complications. Low-dose aspirin has been widely recommended as a preventive measure to reduce the risk of preeclampsia and adverse pregnancy outcomes. However, its effectiveness in women already diagnosed with gestational hypertension remains an important clinical consideration, particularly in tertiary care settings. Aim(s): To evaluate maternal and fetal outcomes in women with gestational hypertension managed with low-dose aspirin and to identify independent predictors of adverse maternal outcomes. Material(s) and Method(s): A prospective observational study was conducted on 80 antenatal women diagnosed with gestational hypertension after 20 weeks of gestation at a tertiary care hospital. All participants received low-dose aspirin (75 mg daily) until delivery and were monitored regularly for blood pressure, laboratory parameters, and maternal-fetal well-being. Data regarding maternal age, parity, BMI, gestational age at diagnosis, clinical features, and laboratory results were collected. Maternal outcomes such as progression to preeclampsia, eclampsia, mode of delivery, postpartum hemorrhage, ICU admission, and fetal outcomes including birth weight, Apgar score, and NICU admission were recorded. Result(s): The mean age of participants was 27.84 +/- 4.56 years, with 60.00% being primigravida. Preeclampsia developed in 17.50% of women, and 2.50% progressed to eclampsia. Cesarean section was performed in 42.50%, while preterm delivery occurred in 22.50% of cases. Low birth weight was observed in 20.00%, and NICU admission in 12.50% of neonates. Multiple regression analysis revealed that higher BMI (AOR=1.12, p=0.032), earlier gestational age at diagnosis (AOR=0.88, p=0.039), primigravidity (AOR=1.68, p=0.046), non-compliance (AOR=2.08, p=0.021), and elevated liver enzymes (AOR=1.82, p=0.037) were independent predictors of adverse outcomes. Conclusion(s): Low-dose aspirin was found to be effective in improving maternal and fetal outcomes among women with gestational hypertension.
