"Background: Meconium-stained amniotic fluid (MSAF) causes Meconium Aspiration Syndrome (MAS), a serious respiratory condition that can kill newborns. Early detection and treatment of MAS is critical, but prenatal care can be difficult, especially in low-resource communities. MAS risk factors must be understood to improve neonatal outcomes and guide management. Objective(s): The primary objective of this study was to identify the risk factors associated with the development of MAS among neonates born through MSAF at B.P. Koirala Institute of Health Sciences (BPKIHS), Dharan. Method(s): Case-control research was conducted at BPKIHS from January 2019 to December 2019. Using sequential sampling, the study included 537 MSAF-born infants. Retrospectively, birth charts, hospital records, and labour room registrations were examined. Neonatal indicators (birth weight, Apgar scores, gestational age), intrapartum factors (delivery mode, foetal distress), and mother characteristics (age, parity, comorbidities) were investigated. SPSS chi-square tests and multivariate logistic regression were used to uncover independent MAS predictors. Result(s): Among the 537 neonates with MSAF, the prevalence of MAS was notable. Key risk factors significantly associated with MAS included thick meconium, cesarean delivery, low Apgar score at 5 minutes, presence of fetal distress, and low birth weight. Multivariate analysis confirmed these variables as independent predictors with statistical significance. Conclusion(s): MSAF requires rigorous intrapartum observation and timely neonatal therapy, according to the study.
