"OBJECTIVE: To evaluate postpartum blood loss in women with treated intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy. METHODS: In a retrospective case-control study, 15,083 deliveries including 348 women with intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (2.3%) were analyzed from 2004 to 2014. A Spearman rank correlation was performed to evaluate the association of bile acid levels and blood loss within subgroups. RESULTS: Estimated blood loss (median 400 [300-600] mL compared with 400 [300-600] mL, P=.22), DHb (14.0 [5.0-22.0] compared with 12.0 [4.0-21.0] g/L, P=.09), meconium staining (14.5% compared with 11.4%, P=.12), and number of stillbirths after 26 weeks of gestation (0.6% compared with 1.8%, P=.10) were not significantly different in the study compared with the control group. In moderate and severe intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy, meconium staining was observed significantly more often compared with that in a control group (23.0% and 32.3% compared with 11.4%, P<.01).
