"Background: Post-dated pregnancy, defined as a pregnancy extending beyond 40 weeks of gestation, is associated with increased maternal and fetal risks. Two common management strategies include induction of labor (IOL) and expectant management (EM). The optimal approach to balancing maternal and neonatal outcomes remains a topic of debate. Aim(s): To compare maternal and fetal outcomes between induction of labor and expectant management in postdated pregnancies. Method(s): A prospective observational study was conducted on 100 post-dated pregnancies over 8 months at Bhagwan Mahavir Institute of Medical Sciences, Pawapuri, Nalanda, Bihar, India. Patients were divided into two groups: induction of labor (n=50) and expectant management (n=50). Maternal outcomes (mode of delivery, labor complications) and neonatal outcomes (Apgar scores, NICU admissions) were analyzed and compared. Result(s): The induction of labor group had a higher rate of vaginal deliveries (70%) compared to the expectant management group (50%). Maternal complications, including prolonged labor and need for cesarean sections, were more common in the expectant management group. Neonatal outcomes, including Apgar scores and NICU admissions, were significantly better in the induction group (p<0.05). Conclusion(s): Induction of labor in post-dated pregnancies is associated with better maternal and neonatal outcomes compared to expectant management.
