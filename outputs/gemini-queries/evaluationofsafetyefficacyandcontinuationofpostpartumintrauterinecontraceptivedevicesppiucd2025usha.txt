"Background: Intrauterine contraceptive device is highly effective, safe, long acting, rapidly reversible and cost effective method of contraception. It can be used by women who are breastfeeding. While it is readily available, IUCD acceptance rate can vary. Understanding the barriers to IUCD acceptance and the reasons for discontinuation is crucial for improving access to and utilization of this effective contraceptive method. Studies have explored various factors including sociodemographic characteristics, cultural belief, individual experiences to identify potential area for improvement in counseling and education. Aim(s): To evaluate the safety (perforation, pelvic infection, excessive bleeding, unusual vaginal discharge, pain abdomen), efficacy (expulsion, pregnancy with IUCD in situ) and continuation rates of postpartum intrauterine contraceptive devices (PPIUCD). Method(s): This is a prospective case control study conducted in the department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology in Coimbatore Medical College Hospital. All women delivering vaginally or by caesarian section, counselled for PPIUCD antenatally, in early labour or in the immediate postpartum period and satisfying the inclusion criteria are included in the study. Result(s): Among the 200 women participated in the study, there were no complications in 85% at 6 weeks, 91.5% at 3 months and 93.3% at 6 months. The commonest complications were Heavy menstrual bleeding (5.5%) and pain abdomen (1.2%). Expulsion rate was 3.6% at 6 weeks and 1.7% at 3 months. Conclusion(s): Postpartum intrauterine contraceptives device (PPIUCD) provides a safe, effective and long lasting reversible contraception for women before discharge from the hospital in low resource settings.Copyright (c)2025Int.
