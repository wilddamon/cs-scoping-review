"Background: Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is a common pregnancy complication associated with adverse outcomes for both mother and neonate. It is crucial to investigate these complications at the community level to enable timely interventions and reduce perinatal morbidity and mortality. This study aims to assess the correlation between maternal and neonatal complications in patients with GDM within a defined community setting. Material(s) and Method(s): A prospective observational study was conducted over 18 months in a community health center serving semi-urban and rural populations. A total of 180 pregnant women diagnosed with GDM based on WHO criteria were enrolled. Data on maternal complications such as preeclampsia, polyhydramnios, and cesarean delivery were collected, along with neonatal outcomes like macrosomia, hypoglycemia, NICU admission, and respiratory distress. Statistical analysis was performed using Pearson's correlation and chi-square tests; a p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Result(s): Among the 180 GDM patients, 62 (34.4%) developed preeclampsia, and 78 (43.3%) underwent cesarean delivery. Polyhydramnios was observed in 38 cases (21.1%). On the neonatal side, macrosomia occurred in 51 neonates (28.3%), hypoglycemia in 46 (25.6%), and 59 (32.8%) required NICU admission. A significant positive correlation was found between maternal preeclampsia and neonatal hypoglycemia (r=0.46, p=0.02), as well as between polyhydramnios and NICU admission (r=0.39, p=0.01). Conclusion(s): Gestational diabetes is associated with multiple maternal and neonatal complications, with significant correlations observed between specific outcomes.
