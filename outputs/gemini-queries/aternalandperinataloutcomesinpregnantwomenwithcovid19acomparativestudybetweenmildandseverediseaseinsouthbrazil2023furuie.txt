"BACKGROUND: Pregnant women with COVID-19 presenting mild conditions can have a rapid evolution to moderate and severe conditions, with higher rates of hospital admission, admission to Intensive Care Units. The aim of the present study was to compare maternal and perinatal outcomes in pregnant women with COVID-19 between the mild and severe disease, and to assess the possibility of vertical transmission of SARS-CoV-2. METHOD(S): We performed an observational, longitudinal, and prospective study, with a cohort design. Pregnant women who had SARS-CoV-2 infection laboratory diagnosed were categorized into two groups: mild and severe illness. The database regarding maternal clinical features, obstetrical and perinatal outcomes was obtained by medical records review. The SARS-CoV-2 detection analysis in biological samples, was performed using real time polymerase chain reaction test. RESULT(S): We included 120 pregnant women with COVID-19, of which 88 had mild disease (73.3%), while 32 (26.7%) patients were classified as severe. We found a statistically higher incidence of maternal death, cesarean delivery, prematurity, neonatal Intensive Care Unit admission and a lower birth weight mean in the pregnancies with severe disease. We obtained positive Real Time - Polymerase Chain Reaction for SARS-CoV-2 in three fragments of placenta (3.9%), one umbilical cord blood samples (1.4%) and in two swabs of newborns (7.4%). CONCLUSION(S): Adverse maternal and perinatal outcomes were significantly more frequent in pregnant women with severe COVID-19 as prematurity, lower birth weight, neonatal Intensive Care Unit admission, maternal death, and cesarean delivery.
