"Lower respiratory tract infections (LRTIs) remain the leading cause of infant morbidity and mortality worldwide and affect long-term respiratory health. Identifying immunological determinants of LRTI susceptibility may help stratify disease risk and identify therapies. This study aimed to identify neonatal immunological factors predicting LRTI risk in infancy. Cord blood plasma from 191 neonates from the Boston Birth Cohort was analyzed for 28 soluble immune factors. LRTI was defined as bronchiolitis, bronchitis, or pneumonia during the first year of life. Welch's t-test demonstrated significantly higher log10 transformed concentrations of IL-17 and IFNgamma in the LRTI group compared to neonates without LRTI in the first year of life (p < 0.05). Risk associations were determined using multivariate survival models. There were 29 infants with LRTIs. High cord blood levels of IFNgamma (aHR = 2.35, 95% CI 1.07-5.17), TNF-beta (aHR = 2.86, 95% CI 1.27-6.47), MIP-1alpha (aHR = 2.82, 95% CI 1.22-6.51), and MIP-1beta (aHR = 2.34, 95% CI 1.05-5.20) were associated with a higher risk of LRTIs. RANTES was associated with a lower risk (aHR = 0.43, 95% CI 0.19-0.97). Soluble immune factors linked to antiviral immunity (IFNgamma) and cytokines mediating inflammatory responses (TNF-beta), and cell homing (MIP-1alpha/b), at birth were associated with an increased risk of LRTIs during infancy.Copyright (c) 2024 by the authors."""
