"""abstract ID: riskfactorsforintrapartumacidemiaacohortstudy2019wretler
"Neonatal acidemia is associated with short- and long-termmorbidity in the child. Cardiotocography, or electronic fetal monitoring, is one of the most widely used techniques for intrapartumfetalmonitoring, and the use of adjunctive technologies such as fetal scalp blood sampling (FBS) is the norminmany countries, increasing the specificity of cardiotocography, and is likely to reduce the rate of cesarean deliveries. Optimal intrapartum surveillance and timely interventions may reduce the incidence of birth acidemia, associated with newborn morbidity. Knowledge about conditions that increase the risks might be beneficial for optimal care. The researchers performed a secondary analysis of a cohort study performed at Karolinska University Hospital Stockholm Sweden between February 2009 and February 2011. The study population included 1070 women in labor where FBS was performed. During the study period, FBS was performed in 10.5% (1070/10,143) of all deliveries at Karolinska University Hospital. Information on maternal and infant characteristics was collected from the antenatal and hospital medical records. Maternal factors included body mass index, height, age, nicotine use (smoking or snuffing before or during pregnancy), gestational age at first visit to prenatal care, gestational age at delivery, need of interpreter (defined as minor language barrier if relative translated or second language was used, major language barrier if professional translator had been used), maternal comorbidity, information about parity, previous caesarean delivery, and in vitro fertilization. The researchers also retrieved information about start of labor (spontaneous/induced), rupture of membranes, quality of amniotic fluid, use of epidural, oxytocin augmentation, pyrexia, and stage of labor at sampling, additionally including 2 factors not known at sampling: Fetal gender and birth weight as a proxy for intrauterine growth restriction. In a univariate logistic regression analysis for lactate greater than 4.8 mmol/L at FBS, minor language barriers (odds ratio [OR], 2.54; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.26-5.11), active bearing down (OR, 2.46; 95% CI, 1.12-5.39), and maternal height of less than 155 cm (OR, 2.15; 95% CI, 1.08-4.26) were found as risk factors. In a multivariate logistic regression analysis, minor language barriers (OR, 2.21; 95% CI, 1.05-4.67) and active pushing (OR, 2.68; 95% CI, 1.20-6.00) remained significant. The researchers concluded that language barriers, active pushing, and short stature were found to be significant risk factors for intrapartum acidemia. Women with epidural analgesia were found to have a decreased risk.
