"Background: Effective postoperative pain management is crucial for enhancing patient comfort and facilitating recovery. Multimodal analgesia approaches, combining general and local anesthesia, have shown superior pain control with reduced opioid reliance and fewer adverse effects. Material(s) and Method(s): This prospective, double-blind, randomized clinical study included 80 patients undergoing lower segment caesarean section. Patients were randomly allocated to two groups of 40 patients each. Group A received 0.375% ropivacaine with normal saline, while Group B received 0.375% ropivacaine with 0.5 mcg/kg dexmedetomidine. Result(s): The addition of dexmedetomidine to 0.375% ropivacaine in ultrasound-guided transversusabdominis plane (TAP) block by posterior approach significantly prolonged the duration of analgesia (19.00 +/- 3.58 hours vs. 11.00 +/- 3.00 hours, p < 0.0001). The combination also provided better pain control at 6 hours, with a lower visual analog scale (VAS) score, and reduced the requirement for rescue analgesia. Conclusion(s): The addition of dexmedetomidine to ropivacaine in TAP block by posterior approach provided improved analgesic efficacy and safety for patients undergoing lower segment caesarean section.
