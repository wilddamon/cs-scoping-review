"Background: Eclampsia is one of the most serious complications of pregnancy Magnesium Sulphate (MgSo4) is an anticonvulsant of choice for management and prevention of eclampsia.However, its dose linked toxicity is a major concern. Objective:To compare the effectiveness of low-dose magnesium sulphate regime with Pritchard's standard regime for convulsion management in Indian women with eclampsia. Methodology:A total of 120 women with gestational age >=31 weeks presenting with eclampsia and imminent eclampsia were dividedequally and assigned to two groups i.e.low dose regime (LDR)group and Pritchard standard regime (PSR) group. Details including age, parity, type of eclampsia, total dose of MgSO4 required, number of convulsions before and after treatment, serum magnesium level, urine albumin level, cost of each regime, mode of delivery, complications in mother and neonate were collected. Both the groups were evaluated and compared adopting SPSS 20.0. Results:Most participants in both the groups had no convulsion after the treatment, both the regimens were equally effective in controlling convulsions (90% vs 86.6%, P>1).Reoccurrence of convulsion was seen in both regimens; however, the proportion was high in PSR compared to LDR (10% vs 13.3%).The mean serum magnesium levels were significantly lower in the LDR at 30 minutes as well as at three hours of therapy compared to PSR (P<0.001).No maternal complications or deaths were happened in eitherregimen groups. However, there was a noticeable difference in intrauterine growth restriction, deaths and fresh still birthsproportions, but the difference was not significant (P>0.05). Conclusion: Low dose regime of MgSo4 is comparatively effective, safe and affordable compared to Pritchard regime in controlling convulsions and preventing recurrent convulsions in eclampsia and imminent eclampsia in Indian women.
