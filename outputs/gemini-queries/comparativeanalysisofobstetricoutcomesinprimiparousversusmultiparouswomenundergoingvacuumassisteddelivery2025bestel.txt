"Objective: To assess and compare maternal and neonatal outcomes of vacuum-assisted vaginal delivery in primigravid versus multigravid women. Method(s): This retrospective study analyzed the obstetric outcomes of 65 women who underwent vacuum-assisted vaginal delivery and statistically compared maternal and neonatal outcomes between the primigravid and multigravid groups. Result(s): Significant differences were observed between groups in labor duration, neonatal birth weight, and head circumference (p<0.05). Primigravid women had significantly longer labors, whereas multigravid women delivered neonates with higher birth weights and larger head circumferences. Episiotomy rates were notably higher in the primigravid group (p<0.001); however, no significant differences were found between groups for neonatal intensive care unit admissions, neonatal complications, or maternal complications (p>0.05). Conclusion(s): This study demonstrated that parity influences outcomes of vacuum-assisted vaginal deliveries, with no significant differences in neonatal outcomes between groups. The use of vacuum assistance in vaginal deliveries appears to prevent, rather than cause, asphyxia by expediting labor.
