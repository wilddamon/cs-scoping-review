"""abstract ID: insightsintotheroleofpgf2aincanineperiparturientmyometrium2024jungmann
"Parturition in dogs is subjected to complex hormonal regulation, with the involvement of prostaglandin F2a (PGF2a) still not fully understood. To investigate uterine inertia (UI), the most prevalent maternal reason for dystocia in the bitch, a better understanding of undisturbed uterine, especially myometrial function, is crucial. Our aim was to gain deeper insights into the role of PGF2a in the canine parturient myometrium. Uterine biopsies were obtained during medically indicated cesarean sections. To test for stimulatory effects of PGF2a in vitro, circular and longitudinal myometrial layer tissue strips were challenged with 50 pM, 0.5 uM, and 50 uM PGF2a. Prostaglandin-endoperoxide synthase 2 (PTGS2) and PGF2a-receptor (PTGFR) mRNA expressions were compared between primary UI (PUI) and obstructive dystocia (OD) samples in isolated parturient myometrium. PTGFR protein expression was assessed in full thickness uterine samples. PGF2a concentrations were analyzed in canine interplacental tissue around term. In the organ bath, the contractile response to PGF2a was limited to the circular layer at the highest dosage. Correspondingly, PTGFR immunohistochemical staining was significantly stronger in the circular layer (p <= 0.01). PTGS2 gene expression did not differ between PUI and OD, whereas PTGFR gene expression could not be quantified. Local uterine PGF2a concentrations correlated negatively with serum P4 levels and were the highest during prepartum luteolysis while being significantly lower in PUI. Conclusively, despite the significant increase in local PGF2a concentrations at birth, confirming the interplacental tissue as a production site, our results suggest that PGF2a might affect uterine contractility during labor, mainly indirectly."""
