"Pain treatment during labour has always been shrouded in controversy. Therefore, providing effective and safe analgesia during labour remains a persistent challenge. The purpose of the present study was to compare the analgesic requirement, hemodynamic effects, pain scores, and neonatal outcome when given with the dose of bupivacaine and levobupivacaine to our patients during labour pain. Methods The present prospective study cohort included 60 patients further classified into two groups, of which one group (Group B) received 0.0625% bupivacaine with 2 mcg/ml fentanyl (n = 30) and the other group, i.e., Group L received 0.0625% levobupicaine with 2 mcg/ml fentanyl (n = 30). The block was performed in the L3-L4 interspace. Onset of action, total duration of labour, motor blockade, maternal and fetal hemodynamics, and outcome of delivery were compared in both groups. Results Our results revealed equivalent demographic statistics for group B and group L in terms of patient count, age, weight, and ASA status. Maternal hemodynamics were clinically insignificant in both groups. Mean systolic and diastolic blood pressures were significantly lower in group B near 15-20 minutes after drug administration. When both groups were compared to evaluate the onset of action, total duration of labour, motor blockade, sedation score, and foetal outcome, the data remained non-significant. Also, no significant difference was recorded with respect to pain scores, which were measured using a visual analogue scale amongst both groups. Conclusion We conclude that both groups showed excellent pain relief, which is one of the major advantages of epidural analgesia.
