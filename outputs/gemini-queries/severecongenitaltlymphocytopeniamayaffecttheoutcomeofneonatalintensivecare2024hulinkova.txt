"AIM: Circular DNA segments TREC (T-cell receptor excision circles) formed during T-lymphocyte maturation in the thymus, are a sensitive marker of thymic lymphocyte production in a broader manner. METHODS: We collected 207 dry blood spot samples during the years 2015-2018, from newly admitted risk newborns. TREC values calculated per 10(6) cells were determined and a cut-off values of 5th percentile was set. The positive control group consisted of patients (n=13) with genetically confirmed SCID. RESULTS: The median TREC value was 34,591.56 (18,074.08-60,228.58) for girls resp. 28,391.20 (13,835.01-51,835.93) per 10(6) cells for boys, P=0.046. In the group of preterm newborns (n=104), 3.8% had TREC value < 5(th) percentile, half of them died due to sepsis as opposed to no fatalities in preterm newborns with sepsis and TREC value > 5(th) percentile. In the group of term newborns (n=103) 9 children (8.7%) had TREC < 5(th) percentile, half of them were treated for asphyxia, with no fatal complications. CONCLUSION: TREC levels calculated for the 5th percentile of a risk neonatal group is suggested as a surrogate marker for increased risk of fatal septic complication.
