"Background: Otoacoustic emissions (OAE) test is generally appropriate for screening neonates' hearing. Babies, who are diagnosed and rehabilitated sooner, demonstrate better language and behavioural skills. Consequently, exorbitant costs of treatment would be prevented. The objective is to assess the utility of OAE as a screening tool for hearing loss in asphyxiated new borns and to evaluate the association of birth asphyxia and hearing in new borns. Material(s) and Method(s): A prospective study among 50 asphyxiated newborns in Tamil Nadu, was conducted. Categorical variables were reported using percentages. The relationship between the dependent and independent variables was investigated using the Chi-square test with <0.01 P-value. Result(s): Among the participants 62% were female and 38% were male and majority were born by Caesarean Section (72%) and of normal weight (68%). Among the newborns that underwent OAE in 1st session, 28% and 30% had right and left ear abnormality respectively. In 2nd session among 11 study subjects who had undergone OAE, 66.6% and 72.7% had right and left ear abnormality respectively and 20% were screened to have hearing abnormality in 3rd session and referred to do confirmatory BERA test. A significant association was found between birth weight and OAE and also between APGAR and OAE. Conclusion(s): Screening for hearing impairment is essential in all high risk infants. In this study it shows that OAE is an excellent tool as an initial screening method for high risk new borns.Copyright (c) 2023 The Author(s)."""
