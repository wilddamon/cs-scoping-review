"Objective: To compare maternal birth complications early versus late in the academic year and to evaluate the impact of resident work hour limitation on the July effect. Study Design: We conducted a retrospective, population-based cohort study of 628,414 singleton births in Washington State from 1987 to 2012 measuring the adjusted risk of maternal peripartum complications early (July/August) versus late (April/May) in the academic year. To control for seasonal outcome variation unrelated to trainees' involvement in care as well as long-term trends in maternal complications unrelated to variation in trainees' effect on outcomes across the academic year, we employed difference-in-differences methods contrasting outcomes at teaching to nonteaching hospitals for deliveries before and after restriction of resident work hours in July 2003. Conclusion: Women delivering at teaching hospitals early in the academic year suffered a modest but significant increase in complications before but not after resident work hour reform.
