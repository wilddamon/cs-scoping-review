"Background: Mode of delivery is known to influence neonatal physiological adaptation, but its impact on postoperative recovery following abdominal surgery in neonates remains underexplored. Understanding whether vaginal delivery offers any recovery advantage over cesarean section is essential for comprehensive perinatal planning in high-risk neonates. Aim(s): To compare postoperative recovery outcomes in neonates born via vaginal delivery versus cesarean section who undergo abdominal surgery within the neonatal period. Material(s) and Method(s): This prospective, comparative observational study was conducted over 24 months in the NICU and Department of Pediatric Surgery at a tertiary care teaching hospital. A total of 80 neonates requiring abdominal surgery within 28 days of life were enrolled and grouped based on mode of delivery-40 neonates each in Group A (vaginal delivery) and Group B (cesarean section). Data on maternal history, neonatal surgical indications, intraoperative findings, and postoperative outcomes were collected. Key recovery indicators included time to initiation and completion of enteral feeds, duration of mechanical ventilation, NICU and hospital stay, and incidence of postoperative complications. Result(s): Baseline demographic variables and surgical indications were comparable between groups. Group A neonates showed significantly earlier initiation of enteral feeds (30.6 +/- 6.2 hours vs. 33.1 +/- 7.0 hours, p = 0.041), faster achievement of full feeds (5.6 +/- 1.3 vs. 6.2 +/- 1.6 days, p = 0.048), and shorter ventilation duration (18.4 +/- 5.2 vs. 21.1 +/- 6.3 hours, p = 0.026). NICU and hospital stay were slightly shorter in Group A, though not statistically significant. Postoperative complications, including infections and ileus, were marginally more frequent in Group B. Conclusion(s): Vaginal delivery appears to confer modest advantages in postoperative recovery following abdominal surgery in neonates, including faster gastrointestinal recovery and reduced ventilatory support. Although complication rates did not differ significantly, trends suggest a higher burden in cesarean-born neonates.
