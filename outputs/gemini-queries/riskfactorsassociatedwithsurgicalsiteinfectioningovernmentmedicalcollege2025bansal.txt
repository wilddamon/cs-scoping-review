"Introduction - Surgical site infections (SSIs) are the most common nosocomial infections, accounting for 38% of hospital-acquired infections in obstetrics.Risk of surgical site infection is more in developing countries than thedeveloped countries due to malnutrition, anaemia, poverty and environmental pollution; poor preoperative preparation, poor antibiotic selection, wound contamination, or the failure of an immune compromised patient to fight against the infection. Aim & Objectives - 1.To study the surgical site infection in the department of obstetrics and gynaecology after either vaginal delivery with episiotomy/tear or caesarean delivery.2.To study the risk factors associated with surgical site infection. Methodology - This was a prospective one year observational study which was donein the Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Government Medical College.All the patients who fulfilled the inclusion criteria were asked thorough history for the evaluation of risk factors. Collected data was analysed by using Microsoft Excel office version 2019. Result - Total 4007 patients who delivered eithervaginally with episiotomy/tear or by caesarean section and ectopicpregnancies undergoing laparotomy were followed up to look for developmentof SSI and 246 patients who werediagnosed as having surgical site infectionwere enrolled for the study.The rate of SSI in obstetrics patients in our department was 6.14%.246 patients developed SSI, 110 after vaginal delivery, 132 aftercaesarean section and 4 after laparotomy.27.24% had positive culture report either on vaginal, urine and/orwound culture and 62.20% showed no growth on their culture report.Organisms isolated from wound culture wereStaphylococcus Aureus,Coagulase Negative Staphylococcus Aureus and Klebsiellapneumoniae. Conclusion - Medical disorders including anaemia, hypothyroidism, diabetes and hypertension were found tobe associated with SSI. Increased duration of labour, second stage of labour, surgery and rupture of membranes and more number of vaginal examinations were found to be strongly linked with development of SSI. So appropriate steps should be taken to either eliminate or modify the risk factors listed so that magnitude of SSI can be reduced and thus maternal morbidity.Copyright (c)2025Int. J.
