"Background:Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is associated with increased risks of maternal and perinatal complications. Pharmacological management is often required when lifestyle modifications are insufficient to achieve glycemic control. While insulin has long been the standard treatment, metformin is emerging as a viable alternative. Aim(s):To compare maternal and perinatal outcomes in women with GDM requiring pharmacologic therapy, specifically between those treated with metformin and those treated with insulin.Material and Methods:This prospective cohort study included 90 pregnant women with GDM managed at a tertiary care hospital. Participants were divided into two groups: 80 women received metformin and 10 received insulin. Maternal outcomes such as mode of delivery, gestational hypertension, preeclampsia, and preterm birth were evaluated. Perinatal outcomes assessed included birth weight, neonatal hypoglycemia, NICU admission, and Apgar scores. Glycemic control was analyzed using fasting glucose, postprandial glucose, and HbA1c levels. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 26.0, with p < 0.05 considered significant. Result(s):Baseline characteristics between groups were comparable. The cesarean section rate was 60.00% in the insulin group and 47.50% in the metformin group (p = 0.488). Neonatal hypoglycemia was significantly more common in the insulin group (30.00% vs. 8.75%, p = 0.044). Postprandial glucose levels were also significantly higher in the insulin group (129.40 +/- 9.85 mg/dL vs. 122.63 +/- 10.20 mg/dL, p = 0.037). Other maternal and perinatal outcomes, though not statistically significant, showed a trend toward higher complication rates in the insulin group. Conclusion(s):Metformin was found to be a safe and effective alternative to insulin for the pharmacologic management of GDM. It was associated with better postprandial glucose control and lower neonatal hypoglycemia, with comparable maternal outcomes. These findings support the use of metformin as a first-line pharmacological option in selected patients with GDM.Copyright (c)2025Int. J. Life Sci. Biotechnol. Pharma. Res."""
