"Background:Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (IHCP) is a reversible liver disorder that typically manifests in the second or third trimester and is characterized by pruritus, elevated serum bile acids (SBA), and deranged liver function tests (LFTs). While its fetal implications are well-documented, maternal outcomes and their correlation with timing of onset and biochemical severity remain underexplored. Objective(s):To evaluate and compare maternal outcomes in early-onset (<=32 weeks) and late-onset (>32 weeks) IHCP, and to assess the association of maternal serum bile acid levels with the severity of maternal complications. Method(s):This prospective observational cohort study included 120 pregnant women diagnosed with IHCP based on clinical symptoms and biochemical criteria. Patients were stratified into early (n = 40) and late (n = 80) onset groups and further categorized by SBA levels into four groups (10-19, 20-29, 30-39, >=40 micromol/L). Maternal outcomes-mode of delivery, postpartum hemorrhage (PPH), need for blood transfusion, and prolonged hospital stay-were compared across groups. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 25.0 with p < 0.05 considered significant. Result(s):The mean age of participants was 28.6 +/- 3.2 years. Caesarean section rates were higher in late-onset IHCP (60%) compared to early-onset cases (50%). Complication rates, including PPH, blood transfusion, and prolonged hospital stay, increased significantly with rising SBA levels, particularly in patients with SBA >=40 micromol/L (PPH: 40%, transfusion: 30%, prolonged stay: 40%). Elevated ALT (>45 IU/L) was observed in 92% of cases and was associated with increased maternal morbidity. Conclusion(s):Late-onset IHCP and elevated serum bile acid levels, especially >=40 micromol/L, are significantly associated with adverse maternal outcomes, including higher rates of caesarean section, postpartum hemorrhage, and prolonged hospitalization. Maternal serum bile acid concentration and LFT derangements are important predictors of maternal morbidity in IHCP and should guide clinical decision-making and monitoring.Copyright (c)2025Int. J.
