"Objective: To determine whether the nationality of the patient is a factor that influences pregnancy follow-up, labour characteristics, and subsequent complications during post-partum. Material(s) and Method(s): An observational retrospective study was performed that included every delivery registered in Macarena Hospital in Seville during a 5 months period. The sample was divided into 2 cohorts, one including Spanish patients, and the other including foreign patients. Result(s): Data from 1259 pregnant women (1135 Spanish and 124 foreigners) were registered. The number of visits and prenatal test was greater in the Spanish cohort. The rate of labour induction was higher among Spanish patients (22.1%). However, normal vaginal delivery rate was higher among foreign pregnant women. Caesarean section rate was equal (23.4%) between both cohorts. Tearing rate and non-epidural delivery was higher among foreign patients, while episiotomy rate was lower. Gestational disorders and complications post-partum were higher in the Spanish cohort, with a slightly lower percentage of newborn complications compared to the foreign cohort.
