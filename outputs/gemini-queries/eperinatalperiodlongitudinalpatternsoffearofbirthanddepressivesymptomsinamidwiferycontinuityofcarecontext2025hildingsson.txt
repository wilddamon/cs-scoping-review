Perinatal depressive symptoms affect both maternal and infant well-being and are associated with fear of giving birth. Midwifery continuity of care (MCoC) has shown potential in reducing anxiety and depression, particularly among vulnerable women. The aim of this study was to explore the trajectories of fear of birth and depressive symptoms, in relation to background, attitudes and birth-related data. A prospective longitudinal study was conducted among women with fear of birth or depressive symptoms who, after a screening procedure were offered care in a MCoC model. Fear of birth and depressive symptoms were assessed with the Fear of Birth Scale (FOBS) and Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) and measured at screening, in mid pregnancy and twice after birth. During screening 84% presented with fear of birth and 24% with depressive symptoms. The degree of fear of birth and the depressive symptoms changed over time with the highest prevalence during pregnancy and a decline after birth.
