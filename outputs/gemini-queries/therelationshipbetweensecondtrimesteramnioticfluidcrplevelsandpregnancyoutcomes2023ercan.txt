"Aim: The aim of this study is to investigate the relationship between CRP levels studied in second-trimester amniotic fluid and pregnancy outcomes. Material(s) and Method(s): Seventy-three single pregnant women who underwent amniocentesis for genetic purposes in our perinatology clinic between 16-22 weeks of gestation and gave birth in our hospital were included in the study. Pregnancies resulting in preterm birth (PTB), preeclampsia, preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM), gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) and postterm pregnancies were defined as "composite outcomes" and these patients were evaluated as Group 1. However, this difference was not significant. When CRP values in individual subgroups were compared in terms of composite results (preeclampsia, PTB, GDM, PPROM and postterm pregnancy), amniotic fluid CRP levels were higher than in the general population.
