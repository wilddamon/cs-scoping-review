"Maternal mortality is a huge problem for countries like India and those in sub-Saharan Africa. Maternal near-miss is defined as a woman who nearly died but survived a complication that occurred during pregnancy, childbirth or within 42 days of termination of pregnancy. Near miss cases occur more often than maternal deaths. These may generate more information and provide better insight into health system failures and priorities in maternal health care. This study aims to describe the epidemiological co-relates, clinical spectrum and management of near miss maternal mortality cases in a tertiary level hospital in Eastern India. A descriptive cross sectional study was carried out over a period of eighteen months at a tertiary level healthcare facility. A total of 62 near-miss maternal mortality cases which fulfilled the inclusion criteria were included in the study. Data was collected pertaining to their sociodemographic characteristics, clinical spectrum and interventions used for their management. Descriptive statistics were carried out on the collected data and was reported. 33.9% of the near-miss mothers were between 26-30years of age. Majority were residents of rural areas and belonging to the middle class family with an education up to primary level. 66.1% were booked cases and 50% were referred from other peripheral hospitals. 82.5% were antenatal cases. Multigravidas accounted for 52.6% of cases. Cesarean section was the most common mode of termination of pregnancy accounting for 38.7%. Anaemia was the most common presentation at admission (71%) and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy were the most common diagnosis (46%).
